COMPILE

Role
Compiler (C): contract.

Input
LEVEL ∈ {PROBLEM, CASE}
DOMAIN = D
DOMAIN code = NNN
000_DOMAIN.txt
background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt
Controlled background/NNN_D_research.txt
Existing foreground and relevant controlled background.

Output
NNN_D.txt

Rules
000_DOMAIN.txt is normative.
Compile only from controlled background and normative ontology.
Do not perform new research or silently resolve evidentiary gaps.
Contract aggressively: persist conclusions, not research history.
Foreground must be canonical, terse, internally consistent, and sufficient for downstream use.
Classify the industrial decision, not its formulation.
Preserve material uncertainty only when it affects the canonical foreground; otherwise leave evidence and alternatives in background.
Do not introduce new ontology classes, fields, tag meanings, numbering rules, or case schemas.
If compilation requires a normative rule that 000_DOMAIN.txt does not define, stop and report the gap instead of inventing one.

LEVEL=PROBLEM

Purpose
Compile the controlled domain research into the canonical numbered Problem set.

Tasks
0. Compile domain scope.
Contract the controlled territory and ownership conclusions into a short SCOPE introduction.
Keep only domain-specific information.
Do not include workflow rules, dependency semantics, rating rules, approval status, or research history.
For an Industry Domain include Industry, Primary Domain, Territory, and Macro Domain anchor.

1. Decide the Problem set.
Compile recurring industrial Problems, not deployment-specific contracts, broad archetypes, mathematical families, or software modules.
Admit distinct, commercially material ICO decisions supported by the controlled background.
Exclude aliases, narrow parameter variants, formulations, algorithms, weakly supported candidates, and decisions owned by another Domain.
Merge only where the controlled background establishes the same underlying industrial Problem.
Do not merge merely because candidates share artifact shape, Structure, formulation, or solver encoding.
Do not split merely because owner, department, recipient, approval process, or implementation differs.
Preserve the controlled integrated-Problem boundary; neither one mathematical model nor multiple Structures alone determine merge or split.
Do not hide unresolved material merge/split or ownership defects; return them to R/B.

2. Choose canonical identity.
For each admitted Problem, choose the clearest established shorthand and scope.
Write the DESCRIPTION as the industrial decision and its defining conditions, not as a solver formulation.

3. Compile ontology fields.
Assign Primary and Secondary Regime and Structure only from controlled evidence and 000_DOMAIN.txt.
Use Primary Domain D; record material Secondary Domains only where the foreground format requires them or they add necessary information.
Use Parameters and Descriptors only when supported and useful.
Do not infer Structure from supplied relations, formulation artifacts, objectives, feasibility severity, or solver encoding.

4. Compile Templates.
Choose the best established defensible IE and OR benchmark approaches from the controlled evidence.
Retain material near-equals only where no single approach dominates or where industrial practice, published SOTA, and reproducible benchmark practice materially differ.
Do not invent a Template to preserve a candidate.

5. Compile ratings.
Assign Priority, Relevance, and Hardness from the controlled evidence using the scales in 000_DOMAIN.txt.
Do not infer Hardness from NP-hardness alone.
Do not upgrade weak evidence for completeness.
Compile industrial Hardness in every card.
Compile formal complexity only when controlled evidence provides an informative class or span.
Do not compile statements that a formal class is unknown or not established.

6. Derive sequence and numbering.
Assign each Problem to its normative Regime numbering band.
Regime band is the first ordering criterion.
Within each band, use the controlled dependency graph to derive logical sequence.
Minimize backward dependency flow without moving a Problem across its Regime band.
Resolve remaining ties by stable industrial logic, not alphabetical convenience.
Assign the three-digit permanent Problem number only after sequence is fixed.
Rewrite dependencies using final Problem IDs.
Do not treat mathematical reduction as an industrial dependency.

7. Write Problem cards.
Use exactly the foreground Problem-card schema and tag semantics in 000_DOMAIN.txt.
Use controlled technical English.
Compile TAGS as the compact canonical semantic signature of the Problem.
Include the required closed ontology tags and only useful supported open tags.
Compile a material controlled boundary, exception, scope condition, or variant distinction in COMMENTS when it would otherwise disappear.
Otherwise compile COMMENTS —.
Do not use COMMENTS to carry tags.
Do not create explanatory boilerplate.
Remove research history, generic ontology rules, workflow instructions, approval state, dependency semantics, rating semantics, and other reusable rules from the foreground.
Persist only conclusions specific to D and its Problems.
Do not compile Cases at this level.

8. Compile Candidates shortlist.
After final Problem IDs are assigned, select approximately the top 20% most likely to benefit from structural analysis.
Use controlled comparative judgment from Priority, Relevance, Hardness, Template evidence, and current industrial practice.
Strong signals include a triple Critical rating, a current industrial benchmark no better than heuristic or strongly manually mediated practice, and evidence that structural analysis could materially change the solution approach.
Do not invent a new score.
Write one file-level line: Candidates: <Problem IDs>.

Finish
Output the complete numbered foreground Problem set for D in NNN_D.txt.
Do not modify controlled background.

LEVEL=CASE

Purpose
Compile controlled Case research for one selected parent Problem P into terse foreground Case cards.

Parent set
P is fixed.
Do not add, merge, remove, rename, renumber, or reclassify P.
If controlled Case research exposes a parent-set defect, stop and return it to R/B and the Problem layer.

Tasks
1. Compile the INDUSTRY Case.
The first Case for P is INDUSTRY.
Use SOTA, not STATE.
Compile only the controlled case decision, SOTA, CONTEXT, GAP, ATTEMPTS, TAGS, and COMMENTS.
Compile any controlled proposed uplift direction inside GAP. Do not add a generic QUESTION field.
The foreground does not include the target landscape.

2. Compile CANDIDATE Cases.
Create only controlled prospect-specific Cases.
Use STATE, not SOTA.
Use the 3-4 letter lowercase client shorthand in the Case ID.
Do not name the client in the Case text.
Do not convert public inference into client fact.

3. Compile CLIENT Cases when authorized.
Use STATE.
The client may be named in the Case text.
Do not import confidential evidence into unrelated Cases.

4. Compile Case IDs.
Apply only the Case identifier rules in 000_DOMAIN.txt.
Keep industry suffixes short and client shorthands stable.
Use a trailing integer only for a real variant.

5. Compile proprietary Part 2 when controlled.
Update the same Case card.
Append DIAGNOSIS, BREAKPOINT, PRESCRIPTION, BENCHMARK, RESULT, and NEXT.
Update STAGE, TAGS, and COMMENTS as supported.
PRESCRIPTION is short-run | long-run.
Do not fabricate a measured RESULT from a prediction.
Do not create a second Case merely because proprietary analysis was performed.

6. Keep foreground terse.
Do not compile target lists, source history, formal models, probe coordinates, experiment tables, or publication narrative into the Case card.
Those remain in background or a separate report.

7. Apply the normative Case schema.
Use only Case fields and identifier rules defined by 000_DOMAIN.txt.
If the schema is insufficient, stop and report the normative gap rather than inventing a local field.

Finish
Add or update the controlled Case cards under P in NNN_D.txt.
Do not require Cases for other Problems.
Do not modify controlled background.
