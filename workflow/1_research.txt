RESEARCH

Role
Researcher (R): expand.

Input
LEVEL ∈ {PROBLEM, CASE}
DOMAIN = D
DOMAIN code = NNN
For an Industry Domain: Industry, Primary Domain, Territory, and Macro Domain anchor.
Supplied research reports.
000_DOMAIN.txt
background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt
background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt
Existing foreground and relevant background.

Output
background/<NNN>_<D>_research.txt

Rules
000_DOMAIN.txt is normative.
Research facts, alternatives, boundaries, dependencies, evidence, and uncertainty. Do not compile the foreground.
Maximize coverage and evidentiary value; minimize prose.
Prefer primary literature, authoritative reviews, established benchmark repositories, official datasets, standards, and credible industrial sources.
Trace material claims to sources. Distinguish evidence from inference.
Do not force agreement where sources differ.
Classify the industrial decision, not its formulation.
For structural checks, distinguish supplied structure, chosen construction, delivered solution, constitutive laws, feasibility conditions, objectives, and auxiliary variables.
Record apparent ontology defects; do not modify the ontology.

When D is an Industry Domain:
- treat the supplied code, mnemonic, Industry, Primary Domain, Territory, and Macro Domain anchor as fixed inputs;
- apply that identity consistently to candidate Problems;
- do not rename, recode, split, or merge the Industry Domain;
- if material evidence contradicts the supplied identity or indicates a missing Territory split, record the defect for ontology-level review and continue only within the supplied scope where defensible;
- do not create a Territory split from Regime, Structure, horizon, department, specialization, or lifecycle terminology alone.

When a materially credible candidate is plausibly owned elsewhere or Domain ownership remains borderline, route it through background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt.
Ordinary exclusions and ordinary uncertainty stay in D's research background.
When D is the proposed owner of an OPEN marketplace candidate, assess it during normal candidate discovery and resolve it as CLAIMED, MERGED, REJECTED, or still OPEN.
Do not classify another Domain's foreground on its behalf.

LEVEL=PROBLEM

Purpose
Draw on the supplied research reports and relevant existing evidence.
Produce one consolidated research background from which the canonical Problem set can be compiled.
Resolve material disagreements and omissions rather than treating any individual report as authoritative.

Tasks
1. Map the domain territory.
Identify principal industries, applications, operating systems, interfaces, exclusions, and ownership boundaries.

2. Discover candidate problems.
Search broadly by industry, operating function, Regime, IE literature, OR literature, benchmark tradition, and established terminology.
Search explicitly for integrated decisions, digital production, financial and rights-based decisions, reverse and circular flows, transition and migration decisions, information-producing decisions, and credible ontology-stress cases.
Target near-exhaustive practical coverage of commercially material ICO decisions.
Treat existing lists and supplied reports as discovery seeds, not limits.

3. Resolve problem identity.
For each candidate, record:
- decision and scope;
- bound object or substrate;
- discrete commitment and committed artifact;
- defining feasibility, state-transition or conservation semantics;
- material information timing where it changes the decision;
- aliases and variants;
- distinction from neighboring Problems, families, archetypes, and deployment contracts;
- Domain ownership and material interfaces;
- candidate Regime and Structure;
- Priority, Relevance, industrial Hardness evidence;
- formal complexity class or material class span when defensible;
- useful descriptors;
- Template evidence.

Merge only when controlled evidence shows the same underlying industrial Problem.
Do not merge merely because candidates share artifact shape, Structure, formulation, or solver encoding.
For an integrated candidate, test whether coupled choices form one recurring jointly committed industrial decision. Mathematical co-optimization alone is insufficient; multiple Structures, functions, or interfaces alone do not force a split.
Preserve uncertain merges, splits, exclusions, broad family/archetype groupings, and integrated-problem questions for control.
Do not spend foreground effort proving absence of a full-contract complexity classification. Record uncertainty in background when useful.

4. Establish dependencies.
Record meaningful directed problem dependencies: which decisions feed, constrain, consume, or revise which others.
Research establishes the dependency graph; compilation derives sequence and numbering.
Record cycles and uncertain directions explicitly.

5. Establish Templates.
For each credible problem, identify the best established IE and OR approaches.
Record all material near-equals where no single approach dominates.
Distinguish established industrial practice, published state of the art, and reproducible benchmark practice when they differ.
If no credible Template can be established after adequate research, flag the candidate for exclusion or further investigation.

6. Assess coverage.
Continue independent searching until new results are mainly aliases, narrow variants, low-materiality niches, or problems owned elsewhere.
Record known gaps and unresolved boundary questions.

Problem-level output must support:
- admission or exclusion of each candidate;
- dependency control;
- canonical description and classification;
- Template population;
- later case research.

Do not assign permanent problem numbers or final sequence.

LEVEL=CASE

Purpose
For each controlled foreground problem, establish the industrial cases, current best practices, and benchmark candidates needed for case compilation.

Parent set
Use the controlled foreground problem set as fixed input. Do not add, merge, remove, or rename parent problems. If case research reveals a parent-set defect, flag it for return to the Problem layer.

Tasks
1. Research industrial cases.
Find representative real or realistic applications of the problem across relevant industries and operating contexts.

2. Research best practice.
Identify current SOTA or strongest established operational, IE, and OR approaches.
Test the foreground Template against current evidence.
Record material competing or near-equal approaches.

3. Research benchmark candidates.
Prefer public, reproducible cases that instantiate the problem and its Template and can support a meaningful baseline and later uplift calculation.
For each candidate record:
- source;
- case or dataset;
- industrial analogue;
- scale;
- objective and principal constraints;
- method or implementation used as baseline;
- reported reference result when defensible;
- reproducibility;
- access or license constraints;
- limitations.

4. Filter evidentially.
Reject cases that are toy-only, weakly matched, inaccessible, non-reproducible, or unsupported unless their limitation is itself useful evidence.
If no adequate public benchmark exists, record a benchmark gap.

5. Assess sufficiency.
Research enough candidates to let compilation select a small, strong starter set for every problem.
Do not assign final case IDs or select the canonical starter set.

Finish
Write the research background densely and clearly enough for independent Background Control and later compilation.
Do not imitate the final foreground merely for convenience.
