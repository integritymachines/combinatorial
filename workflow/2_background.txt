BACKGROUND CONTROL

Role
Background Controller (B): check research.

Input
LEVEL ∈ {PROBLEM, CASE}
DOMAIN = D
000_DOMAIN.txt
background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt
background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt
background/<NNN>_<D>_research.txt
Relevant foreground and background.

Output
Version-control review: FAIL with findings, or PASS.
Do not edit the research artifact.

Rules
000_DOMAIN.txt is normative.
Check the research against its purpose, not against a preferred conclusion.
Independently verify material claims and search for material omissions where needed.
Distinguish evidence gaps from reasonable uncertainty.
Do not require prose that will not improve later compilation.
Do not compile, renumber, or rewrite the foreground.
Issue only actionable findings.

Control loop
On FAIL, record findings in the version-control review.
The Researcher implements or rebuts them.
Recheck the revised research.
Repeat until no blocking findings remain.
On PASS, promote the background to the controlled workflow stage.
The review history is the control record.

Finding form
[BLOCK] <location>: <defect>. <required correction or evidence>.
[NOTE] <location>: <non-blocking observation>.
PASS only when all BLOCK findings are resolved or successfully rebutted.

LEVEL=PROBLEM

Check:

1. Territory
- Domain boundary is consistent with 000_DOMAIN.txt.
- Principal industries, applications, operating systems, interfaces, and exclusions are covered.
- Material neighboring-domain collisions are identified.

2. Coverage
- Candidate discovery is broad across industries, operating functions, Regimes, IE, OR, and benchmark traditions.
- Material recurring ICO problems are not obviously missing.
- Claimed near-exhaustive coverage is supported by search saturation, not assertion.
- Existing lists were not treated as exhaustive.
- Material borderline or transferred Domain-ownership candidates are recorded in background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt.
- Ordinary exclusions and ordinary uncertainty remain in domain research rather than the marketplace.
- Inbound marketplace candidates for D are explicitly resolved.

3. Problem identity
- Discovery records, deployment contracts, mathematical families, and software modules are not presumed canonical Problems.
- Each admitted candidate is a distinct industrial decision, not merely an alias, narrow parameter variant, formulation, algorithm, broad archetype, or deployment-specific label.
- Identity is supported jointly by the bound object or substrate, discrete commitment, defining feasibility or state semantics, materially relevant information timing, and committed artifact.
- Common artifact shape, Structure, formulation, owner, department, recipient, or implementation alone did not determine merge or split.
- Synonyms, variants, integrated problems, family/archetype groupings, uncertain merges/splits, and exclusions are handled explicitly.
- Integrated candidates use the same semantic identity test as ordinary candidates; mathematical co-optimization alone does not determine identity.
- Domain ownership and important interfaces are defensible.
- For Industry Domains, the supplied code, mnemonic, Industry, Primary Domain, Territory, and Macro Domain anchor are applied consistently.
- Any material evidence against the supplied identity is flagged for ontology-level review rather than silently resolved.
- Problem families, Regimes, Structures, specializations, departments, and lifecycle labels are not promoted into Territory splits.

4. Dependencies
- Directed dependencies are evidenced and meaningful at problem level.
- Direction distinguishes information or industrial decision flow from mathematical reduction.
- Cycles and uncertain directions are explicit.
- The graph is sufficient for later sequence derivation.

5. Ontology evidence
- Candidate Regime and Structure assignments are consistent with 000_DOMAIN.txt.
- Structure refers to chosen construction, not supplied relations or formulation artifacts.
- Priority, Relevance, Hardness, and descriptor evidence is sufficient for compilation.

6. Templates
- Every credible problem has defensible IE and OR Template evidence.
- Material near-equal approaches are retained.
- Established practice, published SOTA, and reproducible benchmark practice are distinguished where they differ.
- Candidates without credible Template support are flagged rather than forced.

7. Evidence quality
- Material claims are traceable to strong sources.
- Important uncertainty and disagreement are preserved.
- Inference is not presented as sourced fact.

PASS criteria
The background is broad enough to compile a canonical problem set without reopening basic domain discovery, problem identity, dependencies, or Template existence.

LEVEL=CASE

Check:

1. Parent fidelity
- Every Case belongs to the fixed controlled Problem P.
- Case research does not redefine, merge, split, or reclassify P.
- Parent-set defects are returned to the Problem layer.

2. INDUSTRY Case
- The first Case is INDUSTRY.
- SOTA is the strongest defensible relevant industry technology state, not a convenient weak baseline.
- CONTEXT, GAP, ATTEMPTS, and QUESTION are supported and commercially meaningful.
- Industry evidence is broad enough that the unnamed benchmark holder is not merely one vendor or one paper presented as the industry.

3. Benchmark support
- Available public or constructible benchmark material is relevant to P and the Case constraint regime.
- Reported, reproduced, synthetic, overlaid, and predicted results are distinguished.
- Scale, objective, constraints, baseline, access, reproducibility, and material mismatches are controlled where relevant.
- Genuine benchmark gaps remain explicit.
- When public benchmarks are missing, outdated, or materially inadequate, the background contains a purpose-built benchmark brief sufficient to design a dataset that tests the intended SOTA-uplift claim.
- Benchmark construction requirements are industrially grounded and do not encode the desired proprietary result into the data.

4. Target landscape
- The first dozen are credible commercial targets supported by concrete reasons.
- Enterprises, boutique consultants, software vendors, and funded startups were considered; equal representation is not required.
- FIRST, PEAK, FALLBACK, and OUTLIER are distinct, defensible roles rather than a generic ranking.

5. CANDIDATE Cases
- Each CANDIDATE derives from the industry research and has enough public evidence to support a useful prospect-specific STATE or other material field.
- Client identity may appear in background but not in the foreground CANDIDATE text.
- Public fact and inference are separated.
- Weakly evidenced prospects remain targets rather than being forced into Cases.

6. CLIENT Cases
- Internal or supplied evidence is distinguished from observation, reproduction, and inference.
- Named-client treatment is used only when authorized.

7. Proprietary Part 2
- DIAGNOSIS and BREAKPOINT follow from controlled proprietary evidence.
- PRESCRIPTION is no stronger than the diagnosis supports and separates short-run from long-run action.
- BENCHMARK tests the relevant claim.
- RESULT distinguishes measured uplift from prediction.
- The comparison demonstrates or tests uplift against relevant industry SOTA, not merely against a weaker candidate or client STATE.
- NEXT follows from the controlled result and remaining uncertainty.

8. Evidence quality
- Material SOTA, state, gap, attempt, benchmark, and proprietary claims are traceable to strong evidence.
- Important uncertainty and disagreement are preserved.
- Formal models and structural analysis support the Case conclusions but do not substitute for industrial evidence.

PASS criteria
The background is sufficient to compile the applicable terse Case card, target landscape, and derived CANDIDATE Cases without unsupported inference.
