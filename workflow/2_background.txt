BACKGROUND CONTROL

Role
Background Controller (B): check research.

Input
LEVEL ∈ {PROBLEM, CASE}
DOMAIN = D
000_DOMAIN.txt
background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt
background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt
background/<NNN>_<D>_research.txt
Relevant foreground and background.

Output
Version-control review: FAIL with findings, or PASS.
Do not edit the research artifact.

Rules
000_DOMAIN.txt is normative.
Check the research against its purpose, not against a preferred conclusion.
Independently verify material claims and search for material omissions where needed.
Distinguish evidence gaps from reasonable uncertainty.
Do not require prose that will not improve later compilation.
Do not compile, renumber, or rewrite the foreground.
Issue only actionable findings.

Control loop
On FAIL, record findings in the version-control review.
The Researcher implements or rebuts them.
Recheck the revised research.
Repeat until no blocking findings remain.
On PASS, promote the background to the controlled workflow stage.
The review history is the control record.

Finding form
[BLOCK] <location>: <defect>. <required correction or evidence>.
[NOTE] <location>: <non-blocking observation>.
PASS only when all BLOCK findings are resolved or successfully rebutted.

LEVEL=PROBLEM

Check:

1. Territory
- Domain boundary is consistent with 000_DOMAIN.txt.
- Principal industries, applications, operating systems, interfaces, and exclusions are covered.
- Material neighboring-domain collisions are identified.

2. Coverage
- Candidate discovery is broad across industries, operating functions, Regimes, IE, OR, and benchmark traditions.
- Material recurring ICO problems are not obviously missing.
- Claimed near-exhaustive coverage is supported by search saturation, not assertion.
- Existing lists were not treated as exhaustive.
- Material candidates owned elsewhere are recorded in background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt.
- Inbound marketplace candidates for D are explicitly resolved.

3. Problem identity
- Each candidate is a distinct industrial decision, not merely an alias, narrow parameter variant, formulation, or algorithm.
- Synonyms, variants, integrated problems, uncertain merges/splits, and exclusions are handled explicitly.
- Domain ownership and important interfaces are defensible.
- For Industry Domains, Industry, Primary Domain, Territory, and Macro Domain anchor are defensible.
- The default Industry × Primary Domain identity remains unless the Territory split test passes.
- Every proposed Territory split satisfies all four normative conditions.
- Problem families, Regimes, Structures, specializations, departments, and lifecycle labels are not promoted into Territory splits.

4. Dependencies
- Directed dependencies are evidenced and meaningful at problem level.
- Direction distinguishes information or industrial decision flow from mathematical reduction.
- Cycles and uncertain directions are explicit.
- The graph is sufficient for later sequence derivation.

5. Ontology evidence
- Candidate Regime and Structure assignments are consistent with 000_DOMAIN.txt.
- Structure refers to chosen construction, not supplied relations or formulation artifacts.
- Priority, Relevance, Hardness, and descriptor evidence is sufficient for compilation.

6. Templates
- Every credible problem has defensible IE and OR Template evidence.
- Material near-equal approaches are retained.
- Established practice, published SOTA, and reproducible benchmark practice are distinguished where they differ.
- Candidates without credible Template support are flagged rather than forced.

7. Evidence quality
- Material claims are traceable to strong sources.
- Important uncertainty and disagreement are preserved.
- Inference is not presented as sourced fact.

PASS criteria
The background is broad enough to compile a canonical problem set without reopening basic domain discovery, problem identity, dependencies, or Template existence.

LEVEL=CASE

Check:

1. Parent fidelity
- Every case belongs to an existing controlled foreground problem.
- Cases do not silently redefine, merge, split, or extend parent problems.
- Any parent-set defect is flagged for return to the Problem layer.

2. Industrial cases
- Research includes representative real or realistic operating contexts.
- Cases materially instantiate the parent decision rather than merely share terminology.

3. Best practice
- SOTA or strongest established IE, OR, and operational approaches are supported by current evidence.
- Material competing or near-equal approaches are retained.
- Foreground Templates are tested rather than assumed.

4. Benchmark candidates
- Candidates instantiate the problem and relevant Template.
- Public access, reproducibility, scale, objective, baseline, reference result, and limitations are established where applicable.
- Toy, inaccessible, weakly matched, or non-reproducible cases are not treated as strong benchmarks without qualification.
- Benchmark gaps are explicit.

5. Sufficiency
- There are enough credible candidates to let compilation select a small starter set for each problem.
- The research does not prematurely select or number the final cases.

6. Evidence quality
- Material benchmark and SOTA claims are traceable to strong sources.
- Reported results are distinguished from reproduced results.
- Access, license, implementation, and reproducibility claims are not inferred without support.

PASS criteria
The background is sufficient to select defensible starter benchmark cases for every parent problem, or to state a genuine benchmark gap.
