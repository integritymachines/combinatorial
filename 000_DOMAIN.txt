*Industrial Combinatorial Optimization* 
v0.5 Oct 2026

- Canonical ontology for industrial combinatorial optimization (ICO) systems.
- Aims at near-exhaustive, minimally overlapping classification of commercially relevant combinatorial optimization systems.
- Domains provide the canonical ordering axis; Industries provide an open cross-classification axis.

**Ontology**

1. Macro Domain     Closed grouping of Domains.
2. Domain           Closed canonical ordering axis; where the decision lives.
3. Industry         Open context axis; where the decision is instantiated.
4. Territory        Closed value-chain partition within an Industry.
5. Industry Domain  Coherent industrial decision territory defined by Industry, Primary Domain, and, when required for identity, Territory.
6. Regime           Commitment stage of the decision.
7. Structure        Combinatorial construction performed by the solver.
8. Parameters       Operational and execution qualifiers.
9. Descriptors      Open refinements.
10. Problem         Stand-alone industrial combinatorial decision.

**Macro Domains & Domains**

1. #TRANS   Transformation: systems that change material or product state.
   ├ 101. #PROC  Process, Continuous & Batch Process Manufacturing
   └ 102. #PROD  Production, Discrete Manufacturing & Assembly

2. #DISP    Displacement: systems that move goods, people, inventory, or service capacity through space or time.
   ├ 201. #LOG   Logistics & Transportation
   └ 202. #SCM   Supply Chain & Inventory

3. #INFRA   Infrastructure: systems that provide shared physical service substrates and utilities.
   ├ 301. #ENER  Energy & Utility
   └ 302. #CIVIC Civic & Infrastructure

4. #INFO    Information: systems where sensing, computation, coordination, or adversarial data processing dominates.
   ├ 401. #COMP  Computational & Digital Infrastructure
   └ 402. #INTEL Defense, Intelligence & Security

5. #EXCH    Exchange: systems that orchestrate competing, contingent, and collusive claims across entities.
   ├ 501. #MKT   Market & Commercial Allocation
   └ 502. #FIN   Finance, Accounting, Insurance

6. #HUMAN   Human: systems where human capability or human-centric activity dominates.
   ├ 601. #HC    Healthcare & Human-Critical Operational
   └ 602. #HR    Workforce & Organizational

7. #RES     Resource: systems that extract, harvest, or allocate natural or geospatial resources.
   ├ 701. #EXTR  Resource Extraction
   └ 702. #LAND  Land Use

- Every adequately specified Problem has exactly one Primary Domain.
- Secondary Domains are optional and record material operational coupling.
- Macro Domains group Domains and do not own Problems.

A Problem belongs to:
101. #PROC when process units, recipes, material states, or transformation feasibility dominate. 
102. #PROD when manufactured products, components, fabrication, or assembly feasibility dominate. 
201. #LOG when journeys, vehicles, visits, handling, or physical movement execution dominate. 
202. #SCM when sourcing, availability, inventory, replenishment, or supply-position decisions dominate. 
301. #ENER when physical utility production, distribution, balance, or network operation dominate. 
302. #CIVIC when shared civic assets, public-service coordination, public works, or construction execution dominate. 
401. #COMP when computation, communication, sensing, storage, or digital-resource operation dominate. 
402. #INTEL when adversarial, protective, security, denial, or strategic-response conditions dominate. 
501. #MKT when commercial opportunities, offers, bids, rights, assortment, pricing, or market allocation dominate. 
502. #FIN when financial positions, claims, liquidity, settlement, reconciliation, or insurance-risk decisions dominate. 
601. #HC when clinical, patient, or direct-care conditions materially define feasibility or service.
602. #HR when workforce capacity, skills, staffing, or coordinated organizational human activity dominate. 
701. #EXTR when prospecting, extraction, depletion, wells, blocks, or nonrenewable resource activation dominate.
702. #LAND when land, geospatial assets, renewable resources, water rights, basins, or land-use allocation dominate. 

- Primary Domain follows the industrial decision contract.
- It does not change merely because Industry, Regime, Structure, representation, encoding, or solver formulation changes.
- Interfaces record recurring operational coupling between Domains without changing Primary Domain.

**Industries**

- Industries form an open top-level classification axis alongside Domains.
- An Industry may intersect any number of Domains.
- Industry membership and naming are maintained in background research.

**Industry Domains**

- Every Industry Domain has exactly one Industry, one Primary Domain, and one Territory.
- The default identity is Industry × Primary Domain.
- Territory distinguishes Industry Domains only when a Territory split passes the split test below.
- Secondary Domains are optional.
- Every Problem within an Industry Domain has the same Primary Domain and Territory.
- A Problem with another Primary Domain belongs to another Industry Domain.
- Domain remains the canonical Problem-classification axis.
- Macro Domain is the Industry Domain numbering anchor.

Territory split test

Two territories within one Industry and one Primary Domain are separate Industry Domains only when all four conditions hold:

1. Artifact
They commit different substrates or deliver different artifacts to the next industrial contract.

2. Practice
Each has a distinct Problem inventory and professional practice.

3. Domain residual
The Primary Domain test has already been applied and both remain in the same Primary Domain.

4. Not a variant
Neither territory is only a Regime, Structure, Parameter, specialization, department, horizon, or module variant of the other.

Fail any condition and retain one Industry Domain.

A later split does not silently narrow a published Industry Domain.
A new territory receives a new code and a split-from reference.
If an old code cannot retain its published meaning, retire it and record superseded-by. Never reuse the retired code.

**Industry Domain numbering**

Industry Domain codes share the three-digit Domain number space.

X = Macro Domain anchor.

X00-X09 are reserved for generic Domains.
Industry Domains use X10-X99.

Within an anchoring Macro Domain:

XY0       Cluster head.
XY1-XY9   Members of that Industry cluster.

- Cluster membership requires a defensible industrial relationship, not merely a shared Macro Domain.
- Neighboring Y values do not imply adjacency.
- Industry × Primary Domain normally identifies one Industry Domain.
- An earned Territory split takes another free member code in the same Industry cluster.
- Territory splits do not open a new cluster merely because the Territory differs.
- Codes encode identity and cluster membership, not Priority or Relevance.
- Published codes are not renumbered.
- Gaps are allowed.
- Retired codes are not reused.

**Industry Domain nomenclature**

Each Industry Domain has a canonical short mnemonic used in filenames and Problem IDs.

- Prefer 3-4 letters when an established, unambiguous mnemonic is available.
- Six letters is the maximum.
- A stable Industry stem plus one trailing functional letter may be used when it improves discrimination.
- The trailing letter is nomenclature, not another ontology axis.
- Use the Territory letter when no narrower functional letter is clearly earned.
- The initial controlled letter set is:

  #DEVELOPMENT     D development/design | R research
  #PRODUCTION      P production | M manufacturing | O operations or service production
  #DISTRIBUTION    X distribution | S sales | C procurement or sourcing | L logistics
  #ADMINISTRATION  A administration | F finance | H human resources or workforce administration | G governance, legal, or compliance

- Additional one-letter functional discriminators may be admitted case by case if needed.
- The mnemonic must be globally unique.
- Do not reuse a generic Domain mnemonic.
- Do not change a mnemonic merely because the descriptive Industry Domain name changes.
- Industry Domain Problem IDs use <MNEMONIC><three-digit Problem number>.
- Industry Domain files use <NNN>_<MNEMONIC>.txt and background/<NNN>_<MNEMONIC>_research.txt.

Examples:
160 Automotive Manufacturing -> AUTOM.
161 Automotive Development -> AUTOD.

**Territory**

#DEVELOPMENT     Defines a new product, system, service, or capability before routine realization.
#PRODUCTION      Creates, transforms, operates, or directly provides the core industrial output or service.
#DISTRIBUTION    Connects production to upstream supply and downstream demand through procurement, sourcing, movement, positioning, sales, or delivery.
#ADMINISTRATION  Governs rights, claims, finance, workforce, compliance, or organizational arrangements around the other territories.

- Territory partitions the industrial value chain into mutually exclusive and exhaustive parts.
- Assign exactly one Territory to every Industry Domain.
- Apply the Primary Domain test before the Territory test.
- Territory is determined by the delivered industrial contract, not department name or Regime.
- #DISTRIBUTION includes both inbound acquisition/procurement and outbound sales/delivery.
- Maintenance, procurement, service, and similar functions require a contract-level Territory decision.
- Territory is descriptive unless the Industry Domain split test passes.

**Regime**

#design    Configure system structure or capacity before downstream commitment.
#provision Secure a contingent right to designed capacity or capability before a plan names its use.

#plan      Prepare future plans, schedules, campaigns, or policies.
#reserve   Earmark a planned quantity or window for a class of claims without naming the claimant.

#allocate  Assign scarce resources, opportunities, or obligations.

#operate   Orchestrate ongoing activities and execution.
#sequence  Fix the near-term order or batching of work already under operation before evolving state requires local action.

#dispatch  Make short-cycle activation, release, prioritization, relocation, or local-repair decisions under evolving state.
#adapt     Revise prior commitments in response to material change or disruption.

- Primary Regime is one of #design #plan #allocate #operate #dispatch #adapt.
- #provision is allowed only as a Secondary Regime under Primary #design.
- #reserve is allowed only as a Secondary Regime under Primary #plan.
- #sequence is allowed only as a Secondary Regime under Primary #operate.
- Other material Primary-Regime tags may appear as Secondary Regimes when supported.
- Regime is determined by the type of commitment, not horizon alone.

**Problem numbering**

Problem IDs use a three-digit numeric suffix.

The first digit records the ordering Regime:

1##  Primary #design.
2##  Primary #design with Secondary #provision.
3##  Primary #plan.
4##  Primary #plan with Secondary #reserve.
5##  Primary #allocate.
6##  Primary #operate.
7##  Primary #operate with Secondary #sequence.
8##  Primary #dispatch.
9##  Primary #adapt.

The remaining two digits give the Problem sequence within that band.

- Regime band is the first ordering criterion.
- Dependencies order Problems within a band.
- Minimize backward dependency flow without moving a Problem across its Regime band.
- Resolve remaining ties by stable industrial logic.
- Assign permanent numbers only after sequence is fixed.
- A Secondary Regime other than #provision, #reserve, or #sequence does not change the leading digit.

**Structure**

#select  Choose which candidate entities, configurations, or actions are included.
#map     Assign entities to targets, roles, classes, groups, or resources.
#link    Choose relations, adjacencies, transitions, or interconnections.
#place   Position entities or activities in a structured spatial, temporal, metric, or ordered space.

- Structure describes the combinatorial construction performed by the solver.
- Do not infer Structure from supplied relations, formulation artifacts, feasibility conditions, objectives, or solver encoding.
- A Problem has one Primary Structure and may have Secondary Structures required to construct the complete solution.

**Parameters**

#strategic  Strategic decision context.
#tactical   Tactical decision context.
#execution  Execution-stage decision context.
#realtime   Decision must be produced under real-time latency.
#rolling    Decision is repeatedly recomputed over an advancing horizon.
#offline    Decision is computed without live execution-state updates.
#online     Decision uses live or arriving execution-state information.
#openloop   Decision is issued without feedback-driven revision.
#closedloop Decision is revised using observed feedback.

**Priority**

How central is this problem to the Domain's optimization stack.
 1.  Critical   Domain-defining problem encountered by most substantial deployments.
                eg Operating-room scheduling (#HC), vehicle routing (#LOG), campaign scheduling (#PROC)
 2.  High       Common problem across multiple major subdomains but not universally present.
                eg Vaccination allocation (#HC), blend optimization (#PROC), yard management (#LOG)
 3.  Medium     Important recurring problem restricted to particular subdomains or operating modes.
                eg Organ allocation (#HC), refinery turnaround scheduling (#PROC)
 4.  Low        Peripheral, niche, or highly specialized problem.
                eg Newspaper delivery routing (#LOG)

**Relevance**

How much economic, operational, or societal value is created by materially improving this problem.
 1.  Critical   Improvement produces strategic, life-critical, or enterprise-scale impact.
                eg ICU allocation during surges (#HC), inventory positioning (#SCM), unit commitment (#ENER)
 2.  High       Improvement produces substantial recurring operational value.
                eg Operating-room scheduling (#HC), warehouse slotting (#LOG), supplier allocation (#SCM)
 3.  Medium     Improvement produces localized or departmental value.
                eg Diagnostic equipment scheduling (#HC), cross-dock scheduling (#LOG)
 4.  Low        Improvement produces limited operational leverage.
                eg Small-facility visitor parking allocation (#CIVIC)

**Hardness**

How difficult is this problem to solve well at industrial scale using current methods.
 1.  Critical   State-of-the-art deployments rely heavily on decomposition, heuristics, aggregation, rolling horizons, or human intervention.
                eg Hospital-wide patient-flow optimization (#HC), integrated supply-chain planning (#SCM), large combinatorial auction clearing (#MKT)
 2.  High       Industrial instances regularly require custom algorithms, decomposition, or substantial solver engineering.
                eg Operating-room scheduling (#HC), VRP variants (#LOG), refinery scheduling (#PROC)
 3.  Medium     Commercial solvers routinely produce high-quality solutions, but formulation and scaling remain important.
                eg Generic workforce rostering (#HR), capacitated facility location (#SCM), cloud resource placement (#COMP)
 4.  Low        Routinely solved exactly or near-exactly using standard methods at industrial scale.
                eg Standard assignment, bipartite matching, min-cost flow

**Tags**

- '#' prefix   closed tag.
- '##' prefix  open tag.
- ALL CAPS     top-level entity tag.
- lowercase    subordinate controlled value or open refinement.

Closed top-level tags:
- Macro Domain  #TRANS #DISP #INFRA #INFO #EXCH #HUMAN #RES
- Domain        #PROC #PROD | #LOG #SCM | #ENER #CIVIC | #COMP #INTEL | #MKT #FIN | #HC #HR | #EXTR #LAND
- Territory     #DEVELOPMENT #PRODUCTION #DISTRIBUTION #ADMINISTRATION

Open top-level tags:
- Industry      ##ALLCAPS

Closed subordinate tags:
- Regime            #design #plan #allocate #operate #dispatch #adapt
- Secondary Regime  #provision #reserve #sequence
- Structure         #select #map #link #place
- Parameters        #strategic #tactical #execution #realtime #rolling #offline #online #openloop #closedloop

Open subordinate tags:
- Descriptors   ##lowercase

**FOREGROUND DOMAIN FILE**

Each foreground Domain or Industry Domain file contains:

1. Identity and version.
2. SCOPE.
3. Canonical Problem set.

SCOPE is a short domain-specific introduction.
It states the decision territory, ownership rule, and material neighboring boundaries.
For an Industry Domain it also states Industry, Primary Domain, Territory, and Macro Domain anchor.

Do not repeat workflow rules, rating semantics, dependency semantics, approval status, or other generic instructions in SCOPE.
Those belong in the ontology, workflow, or research background.

*PROBLEM CARD*

Problem card = compact, structured record of a stand-alone ICO Problem.

- Controlled technical English for all explanatory fields, following ASD-STE100 principles.
- Use short declarative sentences, active voice, one term for one concept, only necessary synonyms, and simple sentence structure.
- Approved industrial, mathematical, and optimization terms are permitted as controlled ICO terminology.
- Formal identifiers, taxonomy values, tags, equations, complexity classes, and dependency notation are defined separately.
- Avoid information-free repetition across fields.

FORMAT

<ID> <PROBLEM SHORTHAND>.
DESCRIPTION <STE explainer>.
- Concise statement of what the Problem decides and the principal industrial conditions that define it.
- Do not describe solver choice here.

TEMPLATE <IE> | <OR>.
- Use the best established and defensible benchmark approach available.
- IE = established industrial-engineering framing, practice, or model class.
- OR = established formal optimization architecture, formulation family, or solution-method family.
- Record material differences among established industrial practice, strongest published method, and publicly reproducible benchmark when relevant.

REGIME <PRIMARY> [+ <SECONDARY> ...] | <STE explainer>.
- Primary required.
- Secondaries optional.
- Primary Regime values: #design #plan #allocate #operate #dispatch #adapt.
- #provision is allowed only after Primary #design.
- #reserve is allowed only after Primary #plan.
- #sequence is allowed only after Primary #operate.
- Explain why the Problem belongs primarily at the stated commitment stage or stages.
- Do not infer Regime only from planning horizon; use the type of commitment being made.

STRUCTURE <PRIMARY> [+ <SECONDARY> ...] | <STE explainer>.
- Primary required.
- Secondaries optional.
- Canonical Structure values: #select #map #link #place.
- Primary = combinatorial construction that most strongly defines the completed solution.
- Secondaries = additional constructions required to produce the complete solution.
- Explain what the solver constructs and identify important constitutive structural laws when needed.
- Do not infer Structure from supplied relations, formulation artifacts, feasibility conditions, objectives, or solver encoding.

PRIORITY <Critical | High | Medium | Low> | <STE centrality explainer>.

RELEVANCE <Critical | High | Medium | Low> | <STE value explainer>.

HARDNESS <Critical | High | Medium | Low> | <STE industrial explainer> [| <Formal class or supported span>].
- Industrial Hardness is required.
- Formal complexity is optional.
- Include formal complexity only when a defensible class or informative span materially sharpens the card.
- Use terms such as NP-hard only when supported.
- When meaningful variants have different classifications, state the supported span.
- Do not state that a formal class is unknown, unavailable, or not established.
- Do not infer industrial Hardness from formal complexity alone.

DEPENDENCIES <UPSTREAM IDs> → <THIS ID> → <DOWNSTREAM IDs>.
- Use "∅" when no dependency is specified.
- List only meaningful Problem-level dependencies.
- Dependencies = industrial decision sequence or information dependency, not mathematical reduction.

TAGS <Closed tags>, <Optional open tags>.
- Provide the compact canonical semantic signature of the Problem.
- Repeat Macro Domain, Domain, Regime, and Structure tags.
- For Industry Domain Problems, also include Industry and Territory.
- Include Parameters and useful Descriptors when they materially refine identity or retrieval.
- Duplication of dedicated ontology fields is intentional in TAGS.
- Prefer extensible ## tags for Industry, industrial specialization, mathematical form, structural laws, decomposition patterns, or established search terms.

COMMENTS <Optional STE explainer>.
- Preserve only material interpretation not captured adequately by the canonical fields or TAGS.
- Suitable content includes boundary conditions, exceptions, scope qualifications, and important variant distinctions.
- Do not restate DESCRIPTION, REGIME, STRUCTURE, ratings, or TAGS.
- Do not use COMMENTS to carry tags.
- Omit COMMENTS when no such information is needed.

EXAMPLE

PROC101 Process Network Synthesis & Topology Design. 
DESCRIPTION Selects and connects processing units, pipelines, headers, and storage to meet capacity and product-slate targets under capital constraints. 
TEMPLATE Process network synthesis / plant superstructure design | MILP/MINLP network design and decomposition. 
REGIME #design | Defines plant topology and capacity before detailed planning and operations. 
STRUCTURE #link + #select | Constructs the process topology and selects the units that participate; connectivity and flow topology are constitutive laws. 
PRIORITY Critical | Defines the physical structure that constrains major refinery and bulk-chemical operations.  
RELEVANCE Critical | Better designs can materially change capital cost, capacity, flexibility, and future operating limits.
HARDNESS High | Industrial instances can require decomposition because topology, capacity, and process constraints interact | NP-hard for general discrete network-design restrictions.
DEPENDENCIES ∅ → PROC101 → PROC102, PROC103, PROC106.
TAGS #TRANS #PROC #design #link #select ##network-design ##piping.
COMMENTS Long-term structural decisions constrain later physical operations.
