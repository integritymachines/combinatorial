HR owns decisions whose deliverable is workforce capability, personnel capacity, staffing, or coordinated organizational human activity. Human participation alone does not confer HR ownership. Education and sports scheduling are explicit HR territory in the foundational research. Healthcare employment is an industry context: a nurse-to-shift roster remains HR, while a patient-to-nurse assignment that depends on clinical acuity is HC. Airline pairings remain HR when the movement program is supplied. Integrated flight, aircraft and journey recovery belongs to LOG when that movement program is the main commitment.

Evidence codes S01–S56 identify retrieved primary research, author/institution records, implementation documentation and original benchmark specifications. Bibliographic titles are identifiers, not copied explanatory prose. Abstract-only evidence supports the stated decision and formulation family, not performance superiority or production deployment. Some publisher pages failed direct opening; indexed publisher abstracts were available and are identified below. Access was checked on 2026-10-07. No numerical experimental result was reproduced. No method is called universal SOTA. Benchmark names below establish problem identity and later research leads only; no Cases, baselines, instance selections or case IDs are compiled.

Each candidate has a temporary mnemonic. These are research keys, not permanent problem numbers or a final sequence. Classification, ratings, scope boundaries and the dependency graph are project analysis unless specifically attributed. Ratings concern the stated scope, not the most difficult imaginable extension. A method in a paper supports a defensible Template; it does not prove widespread industrial adoption. Formal hardness is stated only for an evidenced family or an explicit reduction below. For other scopes, the formal classification remains variant-dependent. Integer variables alone are not a hardness proof.

TERRITORY

Operating systems: corporate staffing; retail and hospitality; customer contact centers; aviation, rail and transit crews; telecommunications and utility technicians; professional services and software project teams; standalone manufacturing labor; public-service and contractor workforces; schools, universities and training organizations; sports leagues; conferences and coordinated meetings. Employment status, payment model and volunteer status do not create separate Problems.

Commitments: capacity and skill configuration; hiring and mobility plans; training and leave calendars; anonymous shift and duty construction; named personnel rosters; teams and work assignments; workforce-led service execution; educational and event calendars; immediate activation; recovery of existing commitments. Forecasting demand, predicting attrition and scoring applicants are inputs unless an actual discrete commitment is optimized.

Interfaces: HC supplies clinical service requirements but owns patient-specific integrated care; LOG supplies journeys, travel times and fixed transport tasks but owns movement-led routing; PROD and PROC supply production labor demand but own product/process feasibility; CIVIC supplies public-service work packages but owns asset and construction execution; INTEL owns protective or adversarial mission decisions; COMP supplies digital infrastructure but does not own assignments merely because a platform runs them; MKT owns commercial matching, bidding and labor-market mechanisms; FIN owns payroll settlement and financial claims. LAND and EXTR may supply seasonal or remote labor demand without changing the staffing contract. No cross-domain edge is invented from mere co-occurrence.

DISCOVERY AND IDENTITY REGISTER

CAP — Workforce Capacity and Contract-Mix Design
Decision: choose the numbers of positions or workforce units by skill pool, location and contract class before committing detailed schedules. Includes staffing service systems to given performance targets. Anonymous headcount differs from hiring named applicants and from allocating shifts. Aliases: manpower sizing, staffing mix, staffing establishment. Variants: full-/part-time mix, permanent/temporary mix, service-level staffing, reserve capacity and stochastic demand. Pure continuous FTE budgeting without a discrete choice is outside ICO.
Evidence: S01 develops integer staffing with simulation-estimated service levels; S02 studies recruiting and capacity allocation before demand recourse. Neither claims one staffing configuration works for all enterprises. Candidate Template: workload/service-level capacity planning; integer staffing or stochastic MIP, with queueing or simulation when waiting performance defines capacity. Analytical queueing approximations are near-equal screening tools, not substitutes for a multi-skill feasibility check.
Ontology analysis: #design configures deployable capacity. #select chooses position/contract units; #map assigns the chosen capacity to pools. Supplied demand scenarios and skill eligibility do not constitute #link. Output is an establishment, not a time roster. Candidate ratings: High priority, High relevance, High hardness. S01's coupled service evaluation and S02's recourse support the industrial difficulty judgment. Formal complexity depends on the exact staffing and performance model; no universal NP-hard claim. Descriptors: staffing, contract-mix, service-level, stochastic. CAP supplies limits to SKILL, FLOW and SHIFT. A separate firmwide enterprise-impact claim is unnecessary for High relevance.

SKILL — Workforce Skill-Mix and Cross-Training Design
Decision: select which additional qualifications each employee or employee group should acquire, subject to training resources and required substitutability. Deliverable: target worker-to-skill eligibility. Aliases: multi-skilling design, cross-training portfolio. Variants: hierarchical proficiency, limited cross-training, skill decay, demand/absence robustness. This is distinct from placing actual training sessions in a calendar. It does not assign workers to today's tasks.
Evidence: S03 explicitly chooses workers for training on particular machines using IP; S04 integrates training choices with service-category staffing. This supports an established integer-assignment Template with scenario or simulation evaluation where demand uncertainty matters. Chaining policies and limited flexibility are candidate industrial policies, not canonical Structures. Source models have different labor and training assumptions; retain both assignment and integrated scheduling formulations rather than declare a dominant algorithm.
Ontology analysis: #design; #map primary because new qualifications are assigned to people or pools. #select secondary for the optional training investments. Supplied skill hierarchy and substitution rules are feasibility data; they are not solver-created organizational links. Ratings: High/High/High. Breadth follows manufacturing, maintenance and service applications; investment and lost productive time create recurring value; scheduling interaction limits separability. Formal complexity is model-dependent. Descriptors: cross-training, skill-substitution. CAP provides the workforce envelope; SKILL feeds FLOW's targeted capability plan and TRAIN's course requirements. Later performance feedback can revise SKILL in a new design iteration, not in the current forward graph.

FLOW — Workforce Recruitment and Mobility Planning
Decision: plan integer hires, internal moves and exits across roles, sites and periods to reach a workforce target under lead times, attrition and budget limits. Aliases: manpower transition planning, workforce replenishment. Variants: internal promotion, redeployment, seasonal hiring, contingent personnel and retirement replacement. Named candidate selection is a POST variant when it assigns people to fixed positions; advertising and recruiting-market mechanism design belong to MKT.
Evidence: S02 supports recruiting/allocation with later recourse; S05 describes recruitment, internal mobility and training in a hierarchical manpower system. S06 has a workforce model with hiring, separation, training and overtime. Template: workforce supply-demand and transition planning; time-indexed MIP or stochastic MIP. Markov forecasts can supply transition rates but are not themselves ICO. Pure divisible linear-flow special cases may be easy; batch, qualification and timing restrictions must not be treated as interchangeable.
Ontology analysis: #plan prepares a staffing trajectory. #select chooses personnel actions, #map assigns moves to roles/sites and #place chooses their periods. Fixed transition eligibility is not #link. Ratings: High/High/High; interacting transitions, learning and uncertain availability justify more than routine matching effort, without inferring difficulty from integer notation. Formal complexity is variant-dependent. Descriptors: manpower-planning, workforce-transition. CAP and SKILL constrain FLOW; FLOW supplies employee availability to TRAIN, LEAVE, TEAM and POST. Training completions can feed a subsequent FLOW iteration; simultaneous optimization is possible but does not erase the two deliverables.

TRAIN — Employee Training and Certification Scheduling
Decision: assign employees to training sessions and place sessions in time while maintaining service coverage, instructor capacity and qualification deadlines. Aliases: training-course scheduling, recurrent certification scheduling. Variants: maintenance skills, recurrent crew training, classroom/simulator capacity and prerequisite courses. Certification rules are supplied; this file neither invents nor interprets legal rules. SKILL chooses the target capability; TRAIN decides how and when to obtain it.
Evidence: S07 separates skill-mix decisions from training timing in aircraft maintenance. S08 formulates integrated training calendars and multi-skilled employee deployment as IP, with a matheuristic. These support course-capacity planning with scheduling MIP; integrated employee scheduling is a defensible near-equal when service cover cannot be separated. Publication evidence is not a claim of a common public benchmark or universal deployed practice.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place primary for training time, #map secondary for attendance and training resources. Prerequisite arcs are supplied and therefore do not induce #link. Ratings: Medium/High/High. It recurs in certification-heavy operations and can release bottleneck capability; coverage loss, instructor limits and qualification timing couple choices. The unrestricted resource-constrained scheduling variant is NP-hard: fixing attendance and allowing arbitrary course precedences and renewable resource requirements embeds resource-constrained project scheduling. This is a scope-based inference, not a claim about every certification calendar. Descriptors: certification, precedence, skill-acquisition. SKILL and FLOW feed TRAIN; TRAIN supplies absences and qualification dates to LEAVE, ROSTER and TEAM.

LEAVE — Leave and Planned-Absence Scheduling
Decision: assign vacation and other planned absence periods to people under entitlements, requested periods, priorities and coverage limits. Aliases: vacation planning, annual-leave allocation. Variants: split leave, seniority bidding, carry-over and rotating holiday entitlement. Ordinary roster days off belong to ROSTER unless a separately approved absence calendar is delivered. An absence request is not a market bid merely because employees submit ranked preferences.
Evidence: S09 studies airline leave planning with IP/set packing and stochastic extension; S10 provides a recent column-generation framework for vacation awards. Template: leave quota and preference allocation; period-assignment MIP or column generation over admissible leave patterns. Seniority or lexicographic priorities are instance policies, not general recommendations.
Ontology analysis: #plan + #allocate; the primary commitment is the future absence calendar, with subordinate allocation of scarce leave windows. #place locates the absence and #map associates it with the employee. Ratings: Medium/High/Medium. Established formulations are practical for many institutions, while scarce qualifications and long patterns can require custom decomposition. Complexity varies with entitlement and pattern restrictions; simple quota matching is a tractable special case. Descriptors: vacation, coverage, preferences. FLOW and TRAIN provide available staff and fixed training absences; LEAVE restricts ROSTER. Mutual training/leave negotiation is an optional return to a later planning pass. No artificial requirement that all staffing applications have leave optimization.

SHIFT — Shift and Tour Generation
Decision: choose anonymous work patterns, their time boundaries and required multiplicities to cover a demand profile under allowable work/rest rules. Aliases: shift design, anonymous tour scheduling, work-pattern generation. Variants: split shifts, annualized hours, cyclic patterns and multiple activities. The word design in the literature does not determine Regime: selecting next month's work pattern is #plan, while changing the establishment is CAP. Output does not name employees.
Evidence: S11 defines selection of shift templates and daily numbers; S12 models compact continuous tour scheduling; S13 supplies multi-skilled tour models and column-generation alternatives. Template: demand-covering shift/tour construction; set-covering or compact MIP, with column generation for large pattern spaces. Breaks can be co-decided, but BREAK remains a standalone downstream decision when shifts are fixed. No latest-best claim follows from an older comparison.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place defines work/rest positions and #select chooses active patterns. A column in a solver is not independently a Structure: the delivered temporal pattern justifies #place. Ratings: Critical/High/High. Demand-covering patterns are core workforce infrastructure; fine time grids and pattern choices often require formulation engineering. General shift-design hardness is documented by S11, whereas consecutive-ones coverage restrictions permit easier cases. Descriptors: tour, coverage, cyclic. CAP supplies permitted labor capacity; SHIFT supplies ROSTER with anonymous patterns and BREAK with fixed shift bounds. The usual ROSTER-to-BREAK route applies when breaks are personalized.

PAIR — Crew Duty and Pairing Construction
Decision: form legal sequences of supplied transport work pieces into duties and multi-day pairings, including permitted connections and rest, while covering required crew work. Aliases: crew scheduling, duty generation, crew pairing. Variants: airline, rail and transit relief; base balance; rank/language substitution; robustness. Do not use the ambiguous phrase crew scheduling to merge pairing with named rostering. Flights, trips and departure times are supplied; constructing or recovering the integrated movement program is LOG.
Evidence: S14 describes pairing generation through set covering/partitioning and column generation; S15 explicitly distinguishes pairing from rostering and studies language constraints. Template: legal duty/pairing generation; set partitioning/covering with resource-constrained path pricing and branch-and-price. Compact network formulations are alternatives for smaller or more structured systems. The referenced research group's integration aim is not proof of completed deployment.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #link primary constructs successors between work pieces; #select chooses the duties/pairings that cover the work. Rest and connection feasibility constrain those selected relations. Fixed trip times do not justify an additional #place construction. Ratings: High/High/High. Material crew-cost and feasibility consequences are strongest in transport; large legal-pattern spaces require specialized pricing. NP-hard in the general set-partitioning pairing family; unrestricted legal-pattern selection includes set partitioning. This does not make every shortest-path pricing subproblem NP-hard. Descriptors: crew-pairing, succession, coverage. CAP supplies crew-pool/base capacity when it is available; PAIR supplies ROSTER. Roster infeasibility can return to PAIR as an iteration, not an unmarked permanent cycle.

ROSTER — Personnel Rostering
Decision: assign named people to supplied work duties and work/rest patterns across a roster horizon, satisfying qualifications, availability, coverage and labor rules. Aliases: employee rostering, shift assignment, crew rostering, nurse rostering. Variants: cyclic rosters, preference bidding, part-time work, weekend fairness, reserve/on-call rosters and multi-stage schedules. Overtime control, fairness and fatigue are criteria or restrictions here; they do not deliver a different industrial object.
Evidence: S16 specifies nurse-to-shift-and-skill assignments with history across weeks; S17 provides a primary implementation of static and dynamic formulations. S18 establishes crew-roster set-cover/partition approaches. Template: demand- and rule-based named rostering; assignment MIP or constraint programming, with pattern-based branch-and-price and large-neighborhood repair for richer instances. These are retained near-equals; no single family wins on all roster contracts.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #map assigns people to duties, #place constructs each person's duty/rest calendar. Rest-successor restrictions do not imply independently chosen #link relations when the output is a time-indexed roster. Ratings: Critical/High/Medium for generic workforce rostering, matching the normative calibration; large crew-specific or cross-unit variants can be High. Formal complexity varies; unrestricted individual pattern selection is NP-hard through set partitioning. An INRC nurse roster belongs to HR because patient acuity is not the delivered assignment. Descriptors: roster, coverage, preferences, rolling-horizon. SHIFT, PAIR, TRAIN and LEAVE constrain ROSTER conditionally. ROSTER feeds BREAK, PROJECT, FIELD, POST, TASK and REPAIR. Reserve provision and immediate reserve activation are different commitments.

BREAK — Break Scheduling
Decision: place meal and rest intervals within fixed shifts so that individual rules and remaining service cover are satisfied. Aliases: break placement, break assignment. Variants: multiple windows, split breaks and activity-specific availability. An integrated shift/break model is not a separate Problem; its outputs combine SHIFT and BREAK. If breaks are entirely fixed by a supplied template there is no separate BREAK decision.
Evidence: S19 gives implicit IP for flexible breaks; S20 supplies a multiple-break-window model; S11 compares decomposition and joint methods. Template: coverage-preserving break planning; implicit or time-indexed MIP, with constraint-based/local-search methods for large flexible instances. These approaches differ in treatment of break patterns and duty interaction.
Ontology analysis: #plan + #operate where break placement remains part of live shift orchestration. #place only: employees and shifts are supplied, so merely attaching an interval to its existing duty does not independently require #map. Ratings: Medium/High/Medium; frequent fine adjustments create service value but many bounded-window schedules are routine. General break-scheduling decision versions are NP-complete as discussed by S11; this does not establish hardness of every two-break instance. Descriptors: break-window, coverage. SHIFT or ROSTER supplies bounds; BREAK supplies actual on-duty intervals to TASK and DISPATCH. A requirement to redesign shifts after infeasible breaks is an explicit optional iteration back to SHIFT, not the usual downstream flow.

TEAM — Team Formation
Decision: choose people and allocate them to teams to meet combined skill, size and compatibility requirements. Aliases: project-team composition, multi-team formation. Variants: professional services, engineering, sports squads and volunteer teams. Supplied affinity scores are not selected communication links. Assigning one person to one fixed post is POST; coordinating timed work among formed teams is PROJECT or TASK. A team need not be a stable organizational department.
Evidence: S21 develops integer formulations for multiple teams with skills and affinity; S22 gives an exact algorithm for a quadratic team-formation model. Template: competency and compatibility team formation; assignment or set-partitioning MIP, with quadratic formulations and decomposition where pairwise effects matter. A social-network model is one variant, not a condition of admission.
Ontology analysis: #allocate; #map forms groups, #select chooses participants if the pool need not be exhausted. Input relationships define scores/eligibility; no #link unless actual reporting or communication edges are a separate output. Ratings: Medium/High/High. Joint skill coverage and interpersonal effects create specialized combinatorial effort; roster centrality should not be copied here. General team selection with arbitrary skill cover is NP-hard by set cover: choose the smallest people subset whose skill union covers the requirement. This reduction is project analysis for that admitted variant. Descriptors: team-formation, skill-cover, compatibility. FLOW and TRAIN supply available qualified people. TEAM feeds PROJECT and FIELD when those problems use stable or daily teams; endogenous team formation remains possible in their integrated variants.

PROJECT — Multi-Skilled Project Workforce Scheduling
Decision: assign people or teams to project activities and schedule their work under precedence, skill-specific effort, availability and project deadlines. Aliases: multi-project staffing and scheduling, professional-service resource planning. Variants: internal/external personnel, part-time sharing, resource-specific productivity and limits on team fragmentation. The scoped output is a human-resource work program; construction/project asset execution dominated by civic works is CIVIC, and product/process production remains PROD/PROC.
Evidence: S23 presents MIP for jointly scheduling IT projects and assigning internal/external skilled resources; S24 treats team scattering in project staffing. Template: resource-loaded multi-project workforce planning; multi-skill resource-constrained scheduling MIP, with decomposition or matheuristics for coupled project sets. Fixed project selection is an input; selecting commercial opportunities belongs to MKT when that is the contract.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place schedules effort and #map assigns human resources. Precedence is supplied, not a selected #link output. Ratings: Medium/High/High. Skilled personnel bottlenecks and simultaneous projects justify substantial solver engineering. General NP-hardness follows from ordinary resource-constrained project scheduling with one skill and fixed qualifications; continuous divisible effort special cases differ. Descriptors: project-staffing, precedence, multi-skill. TEAM and ROSTER supply capacity when decomposed. PROJECT feeds TASK for detailed execution and REPAIR after changes. Resource infeasibility can inform later CAP/FLOW plans without being a same-horizon dependency cycle.

FIELD — Skilled Field-Service Workforce Scheduling
Decision: allocate qualified technicians or teams to interventions and construct visit sequences and times under skill, availability, precedence and joint-attendance requirements. Aliases: workforce routing and scheduling, technician scheduling. Variants: telecom installation, utility service, repair contractors and synchronized multi-technician work. HR ownership requires the workforce/team/qualification commitment to define the deliverable. Ordinary fixed-crew vehicle routing belongs to LOG; integrated clinical home care belongs to HC; utility network control belongs to ENER.
Evidence: S25 specifies the France Telecom technician/intervention challenge, supporting skill-and-team scheduling. S26 supplies a separate service-technician routing benchmark with skill levels and travel windows. The two must not be conflated: ROADEF 2007 alone is not evidence for an arbitrary travel-routing model. Template: skill-constrained field-service planning; integrated assignment/routing MIP and decomposition or adaptive large-neighborhood search. S53 documents a constructive/large-neighborhood approach to the technician scheduling setting. S54 supports column-generation and constraint-programming alternatives for technician plans; S55 supports capacity/qualification scenario design coupled to routing. Exact and heuristic methods are instance-dependent near-equals.
Ontology analysis: #plan + #operate; #map is primary in this explicitly workforce-led scope, with #link for chosen visit successors and #place for service timing. Ratings: Medium/High/High. Qualifications, shared visits and geographic timing make industrial solutions difficult. The general routing variant is NP-hard because a single universally qualified technician serving all sites includes a traveling-salesperson tour as a restriction. That mathematical restriction does not reclassify the richer workforce contract as LOG. Descriptors: technician, route, synchronization. TEAM and ROSTER supply workforce; FIELD supplies live assignments to DISPATCH and committed work to REPAIR.

COURSE — Course Offering and Section-Capacity Planning
Decision: decide which courses and how many sections to offer in teaching periods under curricular demand, staffing and class-capacity limits. Aliases: elective-course planning, instructional offering planning. Variants: high-school electives, university semester offerings and minimum viable class sizes. Course catalog and degree requirements are inputs. The result changes the available teaching program; it is not merely placement of a fixed set of classes into weekly slots.
Evidence: S28 provides integer programming and branch-and-price for elective planning; S29 distinguishes a department course-offering plan from a student's course plan. Template: demand-led teaching-program planning; course/section activation and period-allocation IP, with column generation for complex student-choice combinations. Public implementation evidence is weaker here than for TIMETABLE and SECTION; no standard universal solver is claimed.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #select activates courses/sections and #place assigns offering periods. Permanent campus capacity is not being designed. Ratings: Medium/High/Medium. Program access and bottleneck courses matter, but this is an education specialization rather than a domain-wide core. Formal complexity depends on permitted prerequisite, demand and activation coupling; no single classification is asserted from IP alone. Descriptors: course-offering, curriculum, capacity. CAP provides instructional capacity only when that establishment is optimized within the same system. COURSE supplies offerings to LEARN and fixed events/capacities to TIMETABLE and SECTION. Enrollment feedback may revise later offerings.

LEARN — Student Course-Progression Planning
Decision: select courses for a learner and place them in study periods under degree requirements, prerequisites, workload limits and offered courses. Aliases: degree planning, academic progression planning. Variants: electives, transfer-credit restrictions and timely completion. This is a supplied educational-rule allocation problem, not automated assessment of learning or curriculum invention. SECTION instead allocates seats among parallel classes in an actual timetable.
Evidence: S29 formally separates student and department course planning; S30 provides an earlier ILP-based degree-completion approach. Template: prerequisite-constrained academic planning; binary course/period IP. Advising rules and transparent feasibility checks remain useful operational baselines, but their objectives need not match the optimization model.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #select chooses courses and #place schedules study periods. Prerequisite arcs are supplied, so #link is not assigned. Ratings: Medium/Medium/Medium. The parent decision is recurrent across education but the individual scope has localized value; systemwide coordination is not assumed. Formal complexity is variant-dependent; course alternatives and prerequisite coupling are the practical drivers. Descriptors: degree-planning, prerequisite. COURSE supplies offering periods; LEARN supplies requested course combinations to SECTION where institutions use explicit plans. It is not a mandatory upstream step for every timetable. No guarantee of timely graduation follows from solving a deterministic plan with uncertain future offerings.

TIMETABLE — Course and School Timetabling
Decision: place supplied teaching events in time and assign suitable rooms or other teaching resources under teacher, class, curriculum and availability constraints. Aliases: high-school timetabling, university course timetabling. Variants: curriculum-based or post-enrollment conflicts; repeated lessons; shared rooms; cross-campus travel. School and university formulations differ materially but deliver the same recurring teaching calendar, so retain variants without multiplying parent identities.
Evidence: S31 specifies combined class time/room and student assignment; S32 provides official course-timetabling solver documentation; S33 provides the XHSTT archive and format. S56 directly supports MaxSAT and local-search methods for ITC 2019. Template: conflict-aware educational timetabling; constraint programming/local search or MIP/MaxSAT, retaining resource and distribution constraints. An integrated SECTION solution is supported by S31 but the standalone student-allocation output remains distinct.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place primary, #map secondary for rooms/resources. Conflict graphs and curriculum relationships are supplied, not solver-created links. Ratings: High/High/High. Dense institutional conflicts and soft preferences can make obtaining high-quality timetables expensive. General NP-hardness follows from graph coloring when events sharing a person must have different periods and rooms are unrestricted; project inference, not a claim for every school model. Descriptors: timetable, conflict, curriculum. COURSE supplies events; TIMETABLE supplies periods and rooms to SECTION and teaching duties to POST. SECTION may return conflict information to TIMETABLE or be co-solved. The graph marks that as a feedback alternative, not an unexplained forward dependency.

SECTION — Student Sectioning
Decision: assign students to compatible sections of their requested courses under capacity, course structure and timetable conflicts. Aliases: student scheduling, enrollment sectioning, class allocation. Variants: batch registration, online registration, linked lecture/lab choices and high-school electives. Admissions and market allocation of educational rights are excluded when entitlement/market design, rather than class feasibility, defines the contract. Household or residence placement is not included.
Evidence: S34 documents a deployed student scheduling component; S31 specifies linked course configurations; S35 supplies IP and adaptive large-neighborhood methods for high-school sectioning. Template: capacity- and preference-aware student allocation; assignment MIP or constraint-based search, with large-neighborhood methods for combinatorial course bundles. Online versus batch is a parameter/commitment distinction, not a new Problem.
Ontology analysis: #allocate; #map only when timetable periods and class definitions are supplied. Evaluating conflicts in a chosen student's schedule does not imply that SECTION chooses event times. Ratings: High/High/High because joint course bundles and shared limited seats make this more than single-course matching. Simple independent seat allocation is a flow/matching special case; coupled section-bundle complexity is variant-dependent here. Descriptors: student-sectioning, course-bundle, capacity. COURSE, LEARN and TIMETABLE feed SECTION conditionally. SECTION supplies enrollment conflicts to EXAM and teaching-assistant availability to POST. S36 explicitly supports the latter ordering, preventing an automatic assumption that instructor assignment always precedes timetabling.

EXAM — Examination Timetabling
Decision: place examinations in periods and assign rooms under student conflicts, seating, duration and distribution requirements. Aliases: exam scheduling. Variants: split seating, different exam lengths, spacing and room sharing. Exam attendance follows enrollments; choosing who is admitted to study is outside this contract. Invigilator assignment belongs to POST when separately delivered.
Evidence: S37 documents the examination system, its student-enrollment dependency and room/period restrictions. The implementation offers an established constraint-based search Template. MIP conflict/room formulations are a defensible formal alternative through the direct binary assignment model; this is project modeling inference, not a documented claim that UniTime uses MIP.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place chooses periods, #map chooses rooms. Ratings: Medium/High/High. University-wide conflicts and seating constraints constrain exam access and schedule quality. The general feasibility problem contains graph coloring by making each shared student an exam-conflict edge and providing unlimited rooms; restricted easy cases remain. Descriptors: examination, conflict, spacing. SECTION supplies actual enrollments, EXAM supplies timed supervision duties to POST. It is distinct from TIMETABLE because examination participation, seat splitting, durations and spacing create a separate operational calendar and handoff. This distinction is supported by a separate operational module, not merely a different solver encoding.

SPORT — Sports Fixture and Venue Scheduling
Decision: place required matches into rounds or time slots and assign permitted venues/home-away positions under opponent, availability, rest and competition rules. Aliases: sports timetabling, league scheduling, tournament scheduling. Variants: single/multiple round robin, home-away breaks, travel cost and broadcast windows. Competing participants and match requirements are supplied; selling broadcasting rights is MKT, and optimizing team transport after fixtures is LOG.
Evidence: S38 gives the RobinX classification/data format and separates tournament format, constraints and objective; S39 illustrates game/referee coupling through MIP. The primary ITC2021 study registered with S38 supplies IP, CP and fix-and-optimize method evidence. Template: fixture and home-away planning; pattern-based MIP, constraint programming or local search. No algorithm dominates every competition rule set. Referee assignment is a POST variant rather than another fixture Problem.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place primary for rounds/time, #map for venues/home-away roles. A fixed round-robin opponent set is not selected #link structure. Ratings: Medium/High/High. Calendar feasibility and conflicting availability have substantial league-level value but the subdomain is specialized. Formal complexity depends on tournament restrictions and objective; travel-rich constrained variants must not be generalized to a bare circle-method round robin. Descriptors: fixture, home-away, venue. SPORT supplies fixed matches to POST. Integrated referee scheduling is possible; referee infeasibility can return to SPORT in a calendar revision. Revenue-maximizing objective alone does not change a calendar commitment into MKT.

EVENT — Meeting and Conference Scheduling
Decision: place meetings or presentations in time and assign rooms or sessions while respecting participant conflicts, capacity and event structure. Aliases: meeting scheduling, interview scheduling, conference timetabling. Variants: pairwise B2B meetings, parallel conference sessions, multi-day interviews and virtual time-zone availability. Scientific talk acceptance, commercial attendee matchmaking and ticket allocation are outside this fixed-event coordination contract.
Evidence: S40 compares CP, MIP and MaxSAT for supplied pairwise meetings; S41 gives a generic conference IP; S42 supplies a clustering-based alternative for conference sessions. Template: participant-conflict and session planning; CP/MIP/MaxSAT, with clustering and local search for grouped sessions. Session grouping does not require co-deciding which scientific submissions are accepted.
Ontology analysis: #plan; #place schedules events and #map assigns room/session membership. Supplied meeting participants are not chosen links. Ratings: Medium/Medium/High. Local event value is material but below enterprise staffing; grouping and attendee conflicts can still need custom formulations. General conflict-constrained timetabling includes graph coloring; simple independent room booking is easier. Descriptors: meeting, conference, conflict. EVENT supplies timed interview/panel/host duties to POST where staff assignment is optimized. It is not an obligatory downstream consumer of academic TIMETABLE or SPORT. This avoids a spurious chain among unrelated industries.

POST — Personnel-to-Position and Fixed-Duty Allocation
Decision: assign named people to supplied positions, roles or fixed-time duties under qualifications, availability, workload and compatibility requirements. Aliases: personnel assignment, internal placement, instructor assignment, referee assignment, invigilator allocation. Variants: applicant-to-vacancy selection after criteria are supplied; teaching assistants; officials; duty stations; fractional funded positions with temporal availability. Stable multi-person team composition is TEAM; constructing a complete intrashift task program, even with fixed task times, is TASK; making a whole work/rest calendar is ROSTER.
Evidence: S36 documents instructor allocation after timetable/sectioning where student assistants have class conflicts; S39 supports referee assignment; S43 documents position allocation implemented for an institution. These are differentiated instances of a common assignment deliverable. Template: qualification- and workload-based placement; min-cost matching/flow for separable cases, generalized-assignment or time-indexed MIP when loads, coupled roles or funding fragments matter. Pairwise preference matching does not automatically imply economic market clearing.
Ontology analysis: #allocate; #map primary. Selecting who occupies scarce posts is intrinsic to mapping a partial candidate pool and need not add #select in every fixed-slot contract. Fixed duty times do not justify #place. Ratings: High/High/Medium. Simple bipartite matching is polynomial; the variable-workload capacitated variant contains generalized assignment and is NP-hard. This explicit distinction prevents treating every staff allocation as difficult. Descriptors: assignment, qualification, workload. FLOW, ROSTER, TIMETABLE, SECTION, EXAM, SPORT and EVENT can supply people/slots. POST supplies responsibility assignments to TASK and DISPATCH. Industry-specific edges apply only to the matching instance.

TASK — Personnel Task Scheduling and Job Rotation
Decision: allocate workers to activities and choose their work intervals or rotation periods under demand, qualifications, task order and workload limits. Aliases: multi-activity staff deployment, task-level labor scheduling, job-rotation scheduling. Variants: service desks, production labor, ergonomic exposure, learning and fixed-time tasks. POST fills defined organizational or professional roles; TASK delivers a worker-by-activity program within the available work intervals, including task coverage and transitions. The fixed-time task restriction remains only a TASK variant, not another admission under POST. The task/resource interface dominates; physical product transformation or line balancing remains PROD when co-decided. An ergonomic score is an input/criterion, not clinical care.
Evidence: S44 provides a primary fixed-task benchmark whose tasks already have start/end times; S45 documents an integrated task/roster decomposition; S46 formulates cyclic job rotation. Template: skill-based task deployment; time-indexed MIP or pattern-based column generation, with local search for rotation and integrated instances. The fixed-task benchmark is not evidence that task times are always optimized; it is a #map-restricted variant of the broader delivered schedule.
Ontology analysis: #operate + #plan; #map primary assigns workers to activities, #place secondary where task or rotation times are chosen. Planned rotations are subordinate #plan instances; live coordinated work allocation supplies the primary operational contract. Ratings: High/High/High. Multiple activities, cumulative exposure and shift boundaries often couple otherwise easy assignments. Formal complexity depends on the temporal and labor restrictions; no hardness theorem is inferred from the benchmark size. Descriptors: task-scheduling, job-rotation, multi-activity. ROSTER, BREAK, PROJECT and POST supply personnel intervals, tasks and responsibilities. TASK supplies executing commitments to DISPATCH and REPAIR. Job rotation remains a controlled variant because its distinct exposure state does not require a separate parent decision object.

DISPATCH — Immediate Workforce Dispatch and Reserve Activation
Decision: choose available personnel and assign the next work or activate reserve staff using current queues, qualifications and availability. Aliases: skill-based service routing, intraday staffing dispatch, on-call activation. Variants: call centers, immediate technician jobs, relief workers and volunteer mobilization. Routing here may mean a customer-to-human assignment rather than physical travel; packet routing remains COMP. Published schedule revision is REPAIR when changing existing commitments is the main output.
Evidence: S47 gives dynamic skill-based routing and approximate dynamic programming; S48 studies reserve crew use within recovery. Template: skill-based queue and reserve control; priority policies or matching for the immediate assignment, with approximate dynamic programming/simulation for future opportunity costs. Predicting arrivals is an input. One-step priority dispatch remains a useful operational comparator rather than being presented as globally optimal.
Ontology analysis: #dispatch; #map assigns current tasks to people, #select chooses reserve activations. #realtime #online #closedloop apply when actual execution state drives short-latency decisions. Do not add #place for an immediate action whose start is prescribed as now. Ratings: High/High/High for coupled dynamic control; a single instantaneous matching can be polynomial. Full stochastic control depends on state/action representation and horizon, so no generic NP-hard claim. Descriptors: skill-based-routing, reserve, queue. BREAK, FIELD, POST and TASK feed availability/commitments. DISPATCH sends realized shortages or execution state to REPAIR. Staffing feedback to CAP is deferred to the next planning cycle.

REPAIR — Workforce Roster and Assignment Recovery
Decision: revise committed rosters, duties and work assignments after absences or changed demand, balancing restored cover with disruption and allowed changes. Aliases: rerostering, personnel rescheduling, crew recovery. Variants: staff substitution, overtime, reserves, duty reconnection and task retiming. A fresh rolling plan is not automatically #adapt; this Problem starts with commitments that are explicitly changed. Integrated flight/vehicle recovery is LOG when movement decisions dominate.
Evidence: S49 addresses retail rescheduling; S50 develops branch-and-price for personnel task recovery with retiming; S48 studies reliable reserve-crew recovery. Template: minimum-change legal workforce recovery; repair MIP/CP or neighborhood search, with branch-and-price for duty-path reconstruction. A small absence repair need not require the algorithm needed for network crew disruption.
Ontology analysis: #adapt; #map and #place revise people and calendars, #select activates permitted replacement/cover actions; #link is required only in duty-reconnection variants. The broad canonical card can retain this secondary construction with an explicit duty-reconnection qualification. Ratings: High/High/High. Tight response time, remaining legality and plan stability require tailored repair. Formal complexity inherits the admitted underlying scheduling variant when all changes are permitted; a restricted swap problem can be much easier. Descriptors: recovery, stability, absence. ROSTER, PROJECT, FIELD, TASK and DISPATCH feed prior commitments and live state. REPAIR has no current-cycle downstream planning edge. Its outputs update execution; persistent failures can trigger the next CAP/SKILL/FLOW/SHIFT iteration. This feedback is not a mathematical-reduction edge.

BOUNDARY AND EXCLUSION REGISTER

Organizational hierarchy/topology: searched explicitly, not silently equated with TEAM. Primary theoretical work constructs hierarchical organization structures (S51–S52). These are meaningful #design/#link candidates, but retrieved evidence did not establish a sufficiently general industrial Template and scope separated from mission-specific INTEL organization. Defer a canonical organizational-chart Problem in v0.1 rather than infer commercial prevalence from the Domain name. S52's integer hierarchy theory is not proof of practical enterprise chart optimization. This is a known coverage limit, not a claim that the topic is non-combinatorial.

Reserve rostering: retain in ROSTER and CAP; activation in DISPATCH; recovery in REPAIR. Separate papers or algorithms do not create four more Problems. Staff fairness, overtime, preference satisfaction, fatigue, workload balancing and skill substitution refine established contracts. Annualized hours and cyclic work patterns refine SHIFT/ROSTER. Crew base staffing refines CAP; crew positioning supplied as a movement choice interfaces LOG. Language/rank rules refine PAIR/ROSTER. Do not duplicate airline cabin, cockpit, rail and bus pairing merely by industry.

Recruiting analytics, performance scoring, employee retention prediction and attrition forecasts are excluded without a discrete allocation. Capacity-changing employment actions belong to FLOW; named placement belongs to POST; job interviews belong to EVENT; the design of the labor-market mechanism belongs to MKT. Compensation or benefit administration without a qualified discrete workforce contract is not forced into HR. Payroll reconciliation and financial settlement are FIN.

Construction execution, manufacturing line balancing, processing operations and machine scheduling remain with their owning Domains; standalone personnel deployment can be HR. Home-care patient continuity, nurse-patient acuity assignments and integrated clinical capacity allocation remain HC. Armed mission staffing with adversarial feasibility remains INTEL. Site patrol/security mission scheduling is not admitted solely because workers execute it. Sports referee, teaching and examination invigilator assignment are POST variants; their supplied calendars are SPORT/TIMETABLE/EXAM. Student admissions matching that allocates rights is a boundary with MKT and is not conflated with SECTION. School bus travel is LOG. School district/campus physical infrastructure is CIVIC. Room allocation attached to a teaching event is TIMETABLE, whereas physical space/tenant allocation can be LAND.

Educational curriculum/degree rules are inputs here. LEARN selects a compliant study trajectory; COURSE chooses the institutional offering. General pedagogical design, automated learning content generation and exam-question generation lack the present scope/evidence for admission. Sports tournament format design, player transfer markets and ticket pricing remain outside SPORT's supplied-format calendar. Conference talk acceptance and attendee matching are not hidden in EVENT. Organizational task-dependency design has no supported separate parent in this run; supplied task precedence must not be confused with #link.

STRUCTURAL AND COMPLEXITY INTERPRETATION

Constructions concern delivered decisions. Selecting an enumerated schedule column still delivers temporal placements; a graph-colored timetable delivers periods rather than selected links. PAIR selects actual successors, so #link is justified. TIMETABLE and SPORT receive conflict/opponent relationships and do not select them. SECTION and POST receive times and map people into fixed alternatives, so #place is not required. BREAK positions intervals within fixed duties and need not remap employees. FIELD uses chosen routes, qualifications and times; HR ownership must be maintained through the workforce-led contract, not through arbitrary tag preference. REPAIR chooses links only in its admitted duty-reconnection variant.

Complexity arguments are deliberately local. TRAIN/PROJECT include resource-constrained scheduling in the stated general variants. TEAM's arbitrary skill-cover variant embeds set cover. TIMETABLE/EXAM/EVENT embed graph coloring only when arbitrary shared-participant conflicts are admitted. POST distinguishes polynomial matching from capacitated variable-workload assignment. SHIFT/BREAK retain known hard general variants and easier pattern restrictions. PAIR/ROSTER's unrestricted pattern formulations retain set-partitioning hardness; a fixed small pattern family does not inherit a blanket industrial difficulty rating. FIELD has a routing restriction, but primary Domain is decided by its industrial contract rather than by that reduction. Dynamic control and recovery need their own state/change model before a formal class is asserted. These deductions support cautious card wording rather than a uniform NP-hard label.

TEMPLATE AND COVERAGE ASSESSMENT

The admitted set contains 23 research candidates with a defensible IE framing and OR family. Sources support distinct decisions rather than a separate parent for each method or industry. Public specification/implementation traditions are strongest for rostering, fixed personnel tasks, technician scheduling/routing, school/course timetabling, sectioning and sports timetabling. Source existence does not establish license, current best solver, production quality, reproducible uplift or a suitable industrial benchmark for every variant; those questions require the later Case layer. Staffing, training, project work, leave and organization design have different levels of public data support.

Search passes: (1) core workforce staffing, cross-training, rostering, pairing and technician work; (2) training, leave, teams, project staffing and call-center control; (3) school/course timetabling, sectioning, exams, sports and event coordination; (4) challenged omissions for organization topology, degree/course offering plans, job rotation, fixed-duty assignment and recovery; (5) cross-checks against primary specifications and adjacent repository Domains. The final pass chiefly yielded variants already covered: reserve crew, multi-stage rosters, instructor/referee allocation, ergonomic rotations and fixed-task assignment. Course progression/offering were retained when they added genuinely different deliverables. This is practical saturation within the searched territory, not proof of global exhaustiveness. Organizational hierarchy remains explicitly deferred for Template/industrial-scope weakness. No outstanding ambiguity is silently resolved into a second Primary Domain.

Priority judgments distinguish core workforce cover and rostering from narrower organizational calendars. High relevance is used for substantial recurrent service or staffing impact; no automatic Critical rating follows from healthcare or aviation industry labels. Medium relevance is used for individual progression and bounded events. Generic ROSTER uses normative Medium hardness; more coupled skill/route/project/timetable decisions use High. No Critical hardness is asserted without evidence that state-of-the-art industrial deployment intrinsically depends on extensive decomposition or aggregation across the whole stated scope.
