TERRITORY AND INDUSTRY CONTEXT

Consumer goods and retail: stocking coverage, buffers, coordinated purchasing, seasonal buys, shelf-life allocation, shortage protection and omnichannel sourcing.
Industrial distribution and global procurement: supplier portfolios, discounts/MOQ, internal-versus-external source allocation, time-phased purchasing, deployment, expediting and disruption recovery.
Capital goods and aftersales: multi-echelon repairable stock, level of repair, service availability, repair/buy provisioning, supersession and final buys. Technical repairs and technician routing remain interfaces.
Chemicals, food and pharmaceutical supply: availability by quality state, expiry and customer eligibility; bulk supply planning and sourcing. Plant blends, transformation lots, quality release and production schedules remain PROC.
Automotive, aerospace, electronics and discrete manufacturing supply: component readiness, discrete source capacity, BOM-constrained supply balance and promise feasibility. Factory capability, production loads and job sequencing remain PROD.
Reusable assets and circular supply: serviceable/unserviceable pools, incoming returns, stock disposition, repair versus replacement and replenishment. Empty movement units used to execute transport remain LOG20; collection and recovery production remain separate contracts.
Energy, construction, extraction, agriculture, public supply and health-related industries instantiate SCM when sourcing/stock availability defines the decision. Physical utility balance is ENER; public asset coordination is CIVIC; resource extraction is EXTR; land/water rights and harvest commitments are LAND; clinical feasibility is HC; adversarial mission feasibility is INTEL. Industry does not decide Primary Domain.
Useful open industry examples: ##RETAIL, ##CONSUMERGOODS, ##INDUSTRIALDISTRIBUTION, ##AUTOMOTIVE, ##AEROSPACE, ##ELECTRONICS, ##PHARMA, ##CHEMICALS, ##FOOD, ##CAPITALGOODS. They are contextual examples, not a closed industry register.

OWNERSHIP TESTS AND ADMISSION RULES

The delivered decision contract controls ownership. SCM delivers stockholding/supply configuration, inventory targets, supply assignments, replenishment orders, feasible supply promises or their repair. Cost terms and a formulation name do not determine Domain.
SCM versus LOG: stock availability/source/quantity is SCM; vehicles, trips, physical visits, handling order and warehouse travel are LOG. Transport costs/lead times may constrain SCM without transferring ownership. Internal stock policy is SCM; physical reserve-to-pick moves are LOG33. Item stocking entitlement is SCM; product-to-pick-face storage placement is LOG29.
SCM versus PROC/PROD: external and network supply commitments are SCM; transformation feasibility, production lots, factory loads and operation sequences are PROC/PROD. Repairable availability is SCM when repair capability is supplied. Technical repair/disassembly and recovery production are PROD. An integrated contract must identify its dominant delivered decision rather than acquire several Primary Domains.
SCM versus MKT/FIN: stock-feasible promises, fixed demand entitlements and stock-source allocation are SCM; offers, pricing, assortment, negotiated exchange and bid clearing are MKT. Working capital can constrain SCM; liquidity/financial claims remain FIN. Operational inventory verification is SCM; purely financial audit assurance is FIN.
Inventory-routing is an interface, not automatically a separate SCM Problem. If the complete artifact is vehicle journeys with delivery quantities, LOG15 owns the routing-led contract with SCM coupling. If the artifact is replenishment lots on supplied transport capacity, purchase/deployment owns it in SCM. A genuinely different dominant integrated contract requires renewed ownership assessment, not a duplicate card by name.
Every admitted ICO instance needs a genuine discrete decision: site/supplier/stock-position choice, categorical support or policy role, integer stock/lot, indivisible allocation, discrete source/date, count task, or recovery action. Pure continuous EOQ, aggregate LP, fixed-rule availability lookup and scalar safety-stock formula are valid techniques but not independent combinatorial problems.
Regime follows the commitment: footprint and available supply modes are design; target policies and future supply commitments are plan; entitlements and requirement assignments are allocate; stock-state disposition and redistribution are operate; immediate release/promises are dispatch; revision of prior commitments is adapt. Long horizon does not force design. Short horizon does not force dispatch.
Structure follows output: SELECT chooses actions/entities; MAP assigns stock, requirements or modes; PLACE chooses actual periods/epochs; LINK is used only if the industrial solution chooses connection topology. Supplied BOM, support tree, sourcing graph, substitution chain, road network and stock genealogy are not LINK evidence. Binary variables alone are not SELECT evidence; stock at a fixed site is not geometric PLACE evidence.

CANDIDATE IDENTITIES, ALTERNATIVES AND DECISION EVIDENCE

KEY buffer — Multi-Echelon Inventory Buffer Optimization
Decision and delivered scope: Assign discrete stock targets and service-time choices across supply echelons to meet service requirements within inventory and capacity limits.
Aliases: Inventory positioning; safety-stock allocation; base-stock allocation; service-parts stock sizing.
Material variants: Guaranteed-service versus stochastic-service models; repairable stock under METRIC/VARI-METRIC; discrete service times; location-inventory integration.
Identity and ownership boundary: Sizes buffers on a defined or jointly selected stocking system. It does not select commercial assortment, locate warehouse pick faces or sequence production jobs. Repairable demand is a material modeling variant, not a new domain problem.
Supplied substrate: Supply/support network, stocking entitlement, lead times, uncertainty, availability targets and allowed stock/service settings.
Chosen construction: Maps stock and service-time configurations to supply nodes and selects active buffers where that choice remains open.
Constitutive laws and feasibility: Material conservation, lead-time exposure and echelon service coupling define feasible configurations. Choosing a node stock level is MAP, not geometric PLACE.
Delivered artifact: Integer stock targets and node service guarantees or evaluated availability levels.
Template basis: Multi-echelon service and inventory positioning | Guaranteed-service optimization or stochastic-service models; METRIC-family allocation for repairables.
Evidence and competing approaches: S09 supports the guaranteed-service tree case only. S10 supports approximate repairable stock evaluation; S07 distinguishes these stocks from LORA. Neither dominates all systems. Pure continuous safety-stock formulas are not admitted ICO instances; discrete target units, stocking activation or service options must be present.
Source support: S09, S10, S07.
Ontology inference: Primary SCM; Regime #plan; Structure #map + #select.
Rating inference: Priority Critical — Inventory positioning is central to substantial supply networks. Relevance Critical — Better buffers can release working capital while preserving service. Hardness High — Service dependencies, shared capacity and uncertain lead times couple node targets.
Useful descriptors: ##multi-echelon ##inventory-positioning ##service-level.
Industrial upstream keys: coverage, repair_support. Dependency interpretation is defined below.

KEY counting — Inventory Count Selection
Decision and delivered scope: Select item-location inventory counts under record-accuracy targets and available counting capacity.
Aliases: Cycle count optimization; inventory verification selection; risk-based cycle counting.
Material variants: Fixed-period count programs; dynamic count priorities; discrepancy risk; discrete count tasks; availability verification before promising.
Identity and ownership boundary: Owns verification of physical stock availability. Financial assurance alone belongs to FIN; worker shift and skill rostering belong to HR; picker travel or count-tour routing is LOG. Predicting discrepancies alone is not ICO.
Supplied substrate: Inventory records, discrepancy history, accuracy targets, count effort, access eligibility and available count capacity.
Chosen construction: Selects item-location count tasks to perform.
Constitutive laws and feasibility: Discrete count effort, access eligibility and accuracy coverage constrain the selected observations. Count epochs, employee routes and staffing are external inputs.
Delivered artifact: Chosen inventory counts; corrected availability is subsequent observed information.
Template basis: Stratified and risk-based inventory cycle counting | Sampling-allocation optimization; constrained count selection.
Evidence and competing approaches: S33 supports optimized count effort subject to accuracy. S34 supports state-informed task prioritization. Restrict the admitted contract to selection of discrete count tasks; temporal scheduling is not established as its benchmark Template. Fixed-period program selection and immediate count activation explain the two Regimes. Statistical estimation and continuous effort allocation alone are not ICO.
Source support: S33, S34.
Ontology inference: Primary SCM; Regime #plan + #dispatch; Structure #select.
Rating inference: Priority Medium — Counting is a recurring support decision for inventory record accuracy. Relevance Medium — Better counts can reduce phantom-stock failures and unnecessary verification. Hardness Medium — Risk estimates and limited count capacity affect task selection.
Useful descriptors: ##cycle-counting ##record-accuracy ##verification.
Industrial upstream keys: coverage, policy. Dependency interpretation is defined below.

KEY coverage — Item-Location Stocking Coverage
Decision and delivered scope: Select item-location pairs to stock under supply eligibility, service coverage, space and investment limits.
Aliases: Stocking/destocking; SKU-location ranging; spare-parts stocking coverage.
Material variants: Consumables, repairables, shared or dedicated pools, stock/nonstock stability periods, omnichannel stock entitlement.
Identity and ownership boundary: Selects where an already defined product is held, not which products are offered to customers. Commercial assortment is MKT. Warehouse slotting is LOG29. Quantity targets and safety-stock placement belong to buffer optimization.
Supplied substrate: Product catalogue, active sites, service catchments, space limits and permitted supply/support modes.
Chosen construction: Selects the item-location stock positions that are maintained.
Constitutive laws and feasibility: Stocking coverage must meet supplied service and eligibility rules; simultaneous quantity decisions can be integrated but are not required to define coverage.
Delivered artifact: Stocked/nonstocked item-location set.
Template basis: Stocking and destocking with service coverage | Binary coverage optimization; joint location-inventory MIP.
Evidence and competing approaches: S08 establishes location-product stock/nonstock decisions with rule-table practice. S39 and S40 directly establish joint location/stock MIP and decomposition under service constraints. Binary item-location coverage is the narrowed architectural inference; joint zero/positive stock choices can combine it with sizing. This does not assert that a vendor stocking service implements a global optimizer. Exact nonlinear service models and linearized or relaxed stock-location models are material alternatives.
Source support: S08, S39, S40.
Ontology inference: Primary SCM; Regime #design + #adapt; Structure #select.
Rating inference: Priority High — Stocked-location choices recur across distribution and spare-parts networks. Relevance High — Better coverage can reduce idle stock and improve local availability. Hardness High — Many item-site choices interact with shared space and service targets.
Useful descriptors: ##stocking ##coverage ##stocked-location.
Industrial upstream keys: network, postponement, repair_support. Dependency interpretation is defined below.

KEY promising — Supply-Constrained Order Promising

Supplied substrate: Order requirements, protected supply, stock records, receipts, source alternatives, component/capacity envelopes and frozen confirmations.

MERGES, EXCLUSIONS AND INTEGRATED-CONTRACT DISPOSITIONS

Multi-echelon inventory, strategic safety stock, service-level optimization and spare-parts stock sizing share the buffer identity when the output is stock targets. Guaranteed-service versus METRIC is a material Template/assumption distinction, not a reason for duplicate IDs.
Stocking coverage is separate: its output is stock/nonstock entitlement. Warehouse physical slotting and market assortment are excluded to LOG/MKT. Product catalogue additions are not disguised as stock decisions.
Supplier selection, diversification and backup reservations merge into supplier portfolio. Single/multiple sourcing and sustainability constraints are variants. Multi-criteria scoring without discrete portfolio selection is preparatory analysis. Order allocation remains separate because it commits actual requirements against available sources.
Joint replenishment, seasonal first buys, cash-constrained replenishment, fixed MOQ and pack-size planning stay within purchase or policy according to the output. No separate card is created for a discount, shelf-life parameter, supplier count, or solver formulation.
Permanent end-of-life withdrawal is retained separately because future resupply is removed and final service liability must be covered. It is not only a short or long horizon.
Returns, excess and obsolete-stock retention/disposal merge into disposition where supplied condition and recovery modes define feasibility. Detailed disassembly/rework/remanufacturing execution remains PROD. Recovery-network facility choices are a network variant.
LORA is retained separately from both stock sizing and live repair/buy provisioning. It changes support roles/capabilities; repair_buy chooses restoration requests on that support system. Purely technical maintenance procedure design is excluded.
Product substitution, supersession and complete-kit support are integrated fulfillment/promising variants; supplied compatibility chains do not justify another Problem. Engineering product redesign remains PROD.
ATP/CTP and adaptive backorder reconfirmation merge as supply-constrained promising. Market-led order acceptance, dynamic pricing, promotion and assortment are excluded to MKT. Stock rationing remains separate from promise dates and pre-order class entitlements.
DRP/deployment and lateral transshipment remain separate: normal hierarchical replenishment from receipts versus redistribution of stock already held. Pull/push modes are variants. Inventory-routing, transport mode choice, empty containers, physical picking, freight consolidation and truck loading are LOG-led when movement is the complete solution.
VMI, consignment, vendor ownership and omnichannel are governance/context descriptors. They do not create new canonical Problems without a different decision artifact. Reusable transport-item purchasing/stock policies instantiate inventory families; repositioning transport units is LOG20.
Forecasting, demand sensing, ABC/XYZ segmentation, stock visibility, anomaly detection, traceability recording, supplier scoring and reporting are data/analysis inputs unless they contain a stand-alone discrete operational decision. Count selection is admitted; prediction alone is not.
Aggregate unconstrained S&OP LP, continuous EOQ, continuous stock-formula tuning, a predetermined FEFO list and fixed-source deterministic ATP checks lack a necessary combinatorial decision. They can be baselines/restricted components without becoming separate ICO cards.
Supply resilience is integrated into supplier/network design before commitments and recovery after disruptions. A generic resilience score or scenario analysis does not warrant a stand-alone card. Generic recovery does not duplicate normal stock control or order-only reconfirmation.
Shelf space/display choice and customer-directed stockouts are boundary-sensitive: physical handling/access is LOG; commercial exposure/assortment is MKT; replenishment stock availability is SCM. No new shelf-space SCM card is admitted from terminology alone.
