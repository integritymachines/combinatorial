PURPOSE AND TERRITORY

COMP owns decisions whose delivered service is computation, communication, storage or civilian sensing. The defining test is the decision contract, not the industry buying the service or the solver used. The domain includes discrete system configuration, executable computational plans, scarce-resource commitments, digital execution and state-preserving adaptation.

Cloud and data centers contribute capacity configuration, admission, temporal scheduling, host assignment, scaling, migration and planned updates. Telecom contributes installed network design, channel plans, service embedding, optical provisioning, packet routing and transmission grants. Storage and content delivery contribute durable placement, cache management and transfer scheduling. Database and compiler systems contribute physical access structures, query-plan construction, register allocation and instruction scheduling. AI infrastructure contributes execution-plan partitioning, heterogeneous job scheduling and online serving as distinct decisions; model fitting itself is not automatically ICO. Civilian sensing contributes deployment, observation and contact schedules.

Principal industries and contexts: ##CLOUD, ##DATACENTER, ##TELECOM, ##AEROSPACE; open additions ##AI, ##HPC and ##CDN denote industry/application contexts. Compiler and database decisions occur across these industries. Their software implementation does not make them solver algorithms for some other admitted decision: their output is itself the executable resource plan.

BOUNDARY RULES

- PROD owns semiconductor fabrication, chip physical design and production configuration. COMP owns the operation and executable use of installed digital resources. Register allocation is not chip-register circuit layout.
- CIVIC owns civil construction, buildings and civic asset maintenance. Communication topology and digital capacity remain COMP when communication or computing feasibility dominates.
- ENER owns utility production, grid balance and supply operation. Compute activation, workload deferral and energy-aware placement remain COMP when digital service feasibility dominates.
- INTEL owns adversarial, protective or strategic-security contracts. General availability after accidental failure remains COMP. Cyber attack-defense games, interdiction and ISR planning are excluded.
- MKT owns offers, pricing, rights, bids and commercial allocation. Technical admission, radio channel use and resource binding remain COMP. A revenue coefficient alone does not move a technical feasibility problem to MKT.
- HR owns stand-alone staffing, technician rostering and organizational calendars. This file schedules machines, programs and communication sessions.
- PROC, PROD, LAND and HC can consume digital sensing. A decision that chooses the sensing system itself remains COMP; a process, land-resource or clinical control decision remains with its dominant contract.

RESEARCH METHOD AND EVIDENCE STATUS

Search date: 2026-10-07. Search families covered cloud/HPC schedulers, AI infrastructure, edge deployment, telecom design and operation, distributed storage, CDN caching, database tuning, compiler optimization, civilian observation, downlink, migration and maintenance. Official implementation documentation and primary research were preferred. Sources are indexed below; retrieval depth and limitations are explicit.

This is problem discovery, not a contemporary performance leaderboard. Established practice, published optimization methods and evidence of accessible implementations are kept distinct. No numerical performance claim has been reproduced. No benchmark instance, case ID or definitive starter set is selected. Current implementations, licenses and executable baselines must be checked during the later case layer after human approval.

Ratings, Primary Regime, Structure and ownership are ontology judgments from the documented contract. Formal complexity statements are scoped to a specified restriction or generalization. Where a complexity claim uses a standard embedded problem rather than a theorem about an entire industrial system, that is a modeling inference. It is not evidence for industrial hardness by itself. Industrial difficulty follows interaction size, operational latency, state uncertainty and observed use of specialized methods.

STRUCTURAL INTERPRETATION

Resources, topology, interference, precedence, query semantics and visibility can all be supplied. They do not by themselves earn #link or #place. A host name is a target: assignment is #map. A frequency label is also a target: channel assignment is #map. An ordered spectral interval or time window is a position: #place. Routing chooses paths: #link. Query planning chooses an execution graph: #link. An optional demand set is #select. Candidate site selection is #select even though each candidate has supplied coordinates.

Every candidate below distinguishes the delivered choice from its environmental constraints. Models may integrate several candidates, but an integration is not automatically a new stand-alone Problem. A second card is justified only by a distinct decision artifact and usable operational interface.

[capacity] Compute and Storage Capacity Configuration

Decision contract. Selects digital-resource sites, server or storage configurations and discrete capacities under demand, latency, resilience and facility limits.
Identity and variants. Cloudlet placement; edge-server deployment; server-mix and storage-tier capacity configuration. Continuous facility engineering is outside this discrete contract.
Boundary. Owns digital service capacity. Civil construction belongs to CIVIC; energy plant design to ENER; supplier and delivery choices to SCM. Reuse an existing capacity plan as input when the decision is only workload placement.
Construction. #select + #map: Selects capacity configurations and maps demand regions to resource pools. Supplied constraints appear in the decision contract; they are not additional chosen structures.
Commitment. #design: Commits deployable digital capacity before service allocation.
Template evidence. S01 establishes capacity-constrained edge location and access-point assignment, including clustering and integer-programming alternatives. S02 establishes forecast and scenario practice. Extending the same contract to server and storage configurations is a modeling inference, not a claim that S01 evaluates every configuration technology.
Defensible IE basis: Workload-based capacity sizing and edge deployment. OR basis: Capacitated location-allocation MILP; scenario evaluation and clustering heuristics. These identify method families, not an unqualified strongest-solver claim.
Assessment (ontology inference). Priority High: Capacity configuration constrains cloud, edge and storage deployments. Relevance Critical: Capacity choices determine major infrastructure cost and service reach. Industrial hardness High: Demand uncertainty, resource compatibility and failure domains couple investment choices. Formal scope: NP-hard for discrete capacitated location-allocation variants.

Cycles and direction control:
- instructions ↔ registers: instruction order changes liveness; register assignment and spills revise order. Common pipelines and integrated models differ; neither direction is globally mandatory.
- parallel ↔ batch: a proposed execution plan supplies resource demand; feasible allocation can trigger plan revision. First-pass plan before schedule is the sequencing convention.
- scaling ↔ placement: desired capacity precedes binding; observed placement feasibility feeds scaling. First-pass desired capacity before binding is the convention.
- observations ↔ downlink: acquisitions create data; delivery windows and finite memory restrict acquisitions. Integrated planning is often appropriate. Acquisition before delivery is the first-pass convention, not a ban on feedback.
- maintenance ↔ migration: the outage plan requests evacuation; safe evacuation enables the outage. Put the intended maintenance plan before migration execution, retaining the readiness return.

Traffic management is also consulted while planning upgrades, but a full traffic-to-maintenance reciprocal edge is not required by every contract. The explicit maintenance → traffic edge records the chosen residual substrate. Restoration is an adapt variant of placement, routing or optical provisioning unless a separately evidenced integrated restoration contract is later admitted.

Dependencies intentionally omitted: no universal compiler → cloud scheduler edge; most schedulers consume a job resource profile rather than compiler allocation. No universal sensing-deployment → ground-contact edge because instrument deployments and space communication are not always one system. No blanket all-to-all cloud stack dependencies. Quota and fairness policy can be exogenous data without becoming a separate parent Problem.

MERGES, EXCLUSIONS AND INTEGRATED MODELS

MERGE as aliases or variants:
- VM placement, pod placement, container assignment, serverless host selection and edge offloading share the workload-to-resource decision when timing and routes are fixed.
- GPU, TPU and accelerator job scheduling remain temporal cluster scheduling. Heterogeneity, gang size and goodput models are material variants. A separate fractional fair-share optimizer alone is not an ICO Problem.
- RWA, RSA and modulation-aware provisioning share a lightpath/spectrum artifact; retain their different constraints for cases.
- Satellite and telescope observation schedules share optional time-window instrument commitments. Civilian ownership must be preserved in every case.
- Planned replica rebalancing and VM live migration share the state-preserving transition contract; protocols and feasibility differ and must not be conflated in cases.
- Failure rerouting, rescheduling, replacement placement and optical restoration are adaptation variants. Do not add a generic recovery card that duplicates their outputs.

RETAIN distinct:
- Capacity configuration vs autoscaling: installed/deployable envelope vs active configuration.
- Admission vs scheduling vs placement: accepted demand set vs resource-time reservations vs actual resource binding.
- Service embedding vs placement: embedding chooses substrate routes as well as endpoints.
- Durable data placement vs caching: mandated redundancy and persistence vs revocable performance copies.
- Database physical design vs query plan: persistent access structures vs an executable operator graph.
- Register allocation vs instruction scheduling: values-to-storage mapping vs issue order. Their integrated solution is a coupled model, not evidence of synonymy.
- Parallel execution planning vs cluster scheduling: internal program distribution vs competition among jobs for time and devices.
- Packet TE vs coflow scheduling: route/forwarding artifact vs time/order of application transfer service.
- Observation vs contact scheduling: production of information vs access to time-limited communication assets.
- Maintenance planning vs migration: allowed change sequence vs execution of state-preserving moves.

EXCLUDE from the admitted foreground:
- Pure continuous congestion control, bandwidth shares, power control and continuous sizing, absent discrete route, action, configuration or schedule decisions.
- Fairness, multi-tenancy, energy efficiency, carbon awareness, robust optimization and online operation as stand-alone identities. They qualify actual decisions.
- Hyperparameter tuning, numerical training, generic reinforcement learning and optimization algorithms as such. Their compute-resource plans are covered where discrete.
- Semiconductor physical design and fabrication configuration (PROD); spectrum auctions and cloud pricing (MKT); cyber interdiction and defensive security games (INTEL); utility supply (ENER); workforce rostering (HR).
- A generic integrated digital-operations superproblem. Existing integrated papers support coupling, not one necessary monolithic contract.

FOLLOW-UP TERRITORY AND COVERAGE LIMITS

The search rounds first recovered expected cloud and telecom families. The second pass added databases, civilian sensing and live transitions. The omission pass added compiler resource decisions and confirmed that several ostensibly new names were GPU, edge, serverless, recovery or LLM variants. Practical coverage is broad; this is not a proof of exhaustion.

The omission pass established instruction selection and tensor-kernel implementation configuration as additional supported contracts. They are retained separately from register allocation, issue scheduling and distributed parallel execution planning. General unrestricted program synthesis/superoptimization is not admitted as one industrial decision; bounded executable choices now have explicit homes. Memory layout is a kernel-configuration variant where it is an implementation choice, or data placement where storage-node assignment is decided. Repair transfer scheduling is a transfer/migration variant; choosing an entirely new error-correcting code is outside this operational resource-planning set. Quantum-specific resource scheduling remains a specialized follow-up territory: generic job and instruction scheduling are covered, but quantum-specific calibration, qubit routing and error-correction design are not claimed to be established by these sources. This limits claims of exhaustiveness without blocking the documented principal digital operating stacks.

Finite investigation scope does not justify a universal SOTA claim. Later case work must test the selected template families against current alternatives, reported scale and reproducibility. Research papers with only an inspected abstract support only the claims stated there. In particular, JASPER and optical restoration method internals are not treated as independently audited implementations.

COMPLEXITY AND RATINGS BASIS

Standard embedded-problem arguments used for compilation: fixed-charge activation with capacity contains network design; finite-site capacity configuration contains location-allocation; optional coverage contains maximum coverage; channel coloring contains graph coloring; constrained value-to-register assignment contains interference coloring; bounded-resource precedence schedules contain resource-constrained scheduling; multi-resource acceptance contains knapsack; vector host assignment contains vector packing; a chosen contiguous optical allocation contains RSA; unsplittable packet paths contain discrete multicommodity routing. These are conditional scope arguments, not proofs that every named implementation solves an NP-hard instance.

Database index selection requires a budget and competing benefits/interactions for the hard selection restriction. Join-plan complexity depends on the allowed plan space and cost model. General coflow completion scheduling is hard, while an individual fixed-path rate allocation may be an LP. Some fixed-resource matching restrictions are polynomial. Right-sizing has tractable restricted models, so no generic NP-hard label is assigned to autoscaling. Cloud placement retains normative Medium industrial hardness for its generic scope; integrated routing, temporal commitments and live transitions carry their own harder contracts.

Critical Priority is reserved for shared-compute job scheduling, workload placement and packet routing, which define major digital operating stacks. Specialized compiler, database, radio, optical and sensing decisions are Medium even where indispensable inside that subdomain. High covers recurring cross-platform decisions. Relevance is based on the affected resource/service, not on an invented uplift percentage. High hardness records specialized engineering; Critical hardness is not used merely to signal fashionable scale.
