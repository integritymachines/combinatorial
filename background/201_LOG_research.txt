EVIDENCE CONVENTIONS
Source keys resolve in the source register. Each source states the claim it supports and the retrieval depth. No source is treated as proof of every constraint in a broad industrial contract.
Candidate scopes, Primary Domain, Regime, Structure, ratings, admission boundaries and dependencies are ontology-based analytical judgments unless a specific literature claim is identified. Template architectures are supported families, not an asserted universal algorithm winner. Adaptations from a source model to a broader decision contract are identified as architectural inferences.
No published performance result was reproduced. No cases, baselines, final benchmark set or license assertions are compiled here. Publicly available package or paper evidence is only problem-level Template support.
Formal complexity belongs to the stated model or restriction. A supplied graph, precedence relation, capacity law or auxiliary binary variable does not establish the solver's Structure.
Candidate keys are temporary mnemonics. They are not permanent Problem IDs or a prescribed foreground sequence.

TERRITORY AND OWNERSHIP
LOG owns decisions whose defining artifact is a journey, vehicle duty, physical visit, load arrangement, handling plan or execution of movement. A facility or automation decision belongs here when it directly configures that movement/handling system. Public ownership or a digital solver does not by itself move the contract to CIVIC or COMP.
Industries and operating contexts: parcel/postal; LTL/TL road freight; grocery and retail fulfillment; cold-chain and temperature-controlled transport; industrial/tanker/tramp maritime; liner container shipping; ports and bulk terminals; freight rail and passenger rail; airlines and airport airside; public buses and ferries; ride-hailing and demand-responsive mobility; field-service travel; warehouse fulfillment; automated material handling; drone-assisted delivery. Reverse collections and returns share the movement contracts where recovery-processing or sourcing decisions are not optimized.
SCM interface: supplies demand, inventory availability, replenishment quantities, stock positions and supply commitments. A transport hub serves movements; a distribution node selected primarily to position stock is SCM. Internal stocked-product handling and empty-asset moves are LOG; deciding target stock or purchasing levels is SCM. Inventory-routing retains distinct replenishment and routing contracts unless an adequately specified integrated contract fixes a dominant owner.
CIVIC interface: supplies public assets, rights-of-way, infrastructure capacities and work restrictions. Carrier service topology, individual train/flight movements and fleet service execution are LOG. Public infrastructure investment, civic coordination and construction execution are CIVIC. Road-segment route service is LOG when the supplied required work is executed through optimized journeys; choosing civic service obligations is a different contract.
HR interface: supplies staff availability and legal/skill restrictions. Crew pairing, driver duty design, roster assignment, team formation and workforce capacity belong to HR when these define the decision. Vehicle duties and physical ground-service movement remain LOG. An integrated model does not create two primary owners.
HC interface: patient acuity, clinical precedence and direct-care feasibility can define HC transport problems, including ambulance relocation. Generic mobility or supplied medical freight routes can remain LOG. Clinical dispatch is not silently absorbed into the generic dispatch card.
PROC/PROD interface: industrial tanks and machines that define transformation remain PROC/PROD. Transit tanks, cargo handling, warehouse pick movement and transport loading remain LOG. Cutting/nesting for fabricated parts is PROD; geometric cargo packing is LOG. Process blending and bulk physical transfer must not be conflated.
ENER interface: electricity production, grid balance and charging-network operation belong to ENER. Vehicle journeys can include charge stops as movement constraints. A separately chosen vehicle charging calendar needs a contract-level ownership test.
COMP interface: digital workload, storage and communication-resource operation are COMP. Robots and digital control systems that execute physical cargo movement remain LOG.
INTEL interface: adversarial/protective feasibility can transfer the primary contract to INTEL; generic defense freight movement does not automatically do so.
MKT/FIN interface: cargo offers, fares, pricing, auctions, carrier procurement awards, compensation and financial leasing value are not movement decisions. Supplied accepted demand and physical service commitments are inputs to LOG.
EXTR/LAND interface: extraction, depletion, basin rights and land allocation are upstream. Mine haulage can be LOG if extraction choices are fixed; tightly constitutive extraction-haulage decisions need their specified dominant contract rather than a keyword rule.

DISCOVERY AND IDENTITY
Searches began from the normative territory, then independently sampled freight/network design, fleet investments, maritime operations, rail planning/yards, airlines/airports, warehouse design/picking, automated fulfillment, live mobility, road-segment service and cross-domain collisions. Source review dates span classical formal foundations through 2026 primary research and official documentation. Search results were used as seeds, not as a closed problem inventory.
Discovery rounds: (1) vehicle routing, service networks, warehouse picking and airline planning; (2) port, bulk, rail and airport operational functions; (3) dynamic mobility, crossdock, packing and robotic fulfillment; (4) hub/fleet design, arc service and intermodal forwarding; (5) districting, blocking, maintenance, live passenger control, sortation and internal replenishment; (6) independent checks for truck-drone synchronization, wave release, charging, model complexity and omitted physical movement stages; (7) pairwise aircraft separation with genuine discrete trajectory recovery and fixed-route vehicle platoon grouping/rendezvous.
Late searches primarily added variants and evidence to existing contracts: truck-drone and tanker routing; reverse collection; railcar empty distribution; gates and taxiing; sorter stations; scattered pod storage; wave versus waveless release. A separate targeted search established dedicated depot charging as an independent vehicle-resource/time commitment, now admitted as CHARGING.
Coverage is broad practical problem-layer coverage, not a proof of global exhaustion. Autonomous maritime navigation, cargo-specific securing procedures and detailed continuous vehicle-motion control may need later specialized evidence. Continuous control without genuine discrete choices is outside ICO. These residuals do not establish an additional supported general Problem at this stage. Pairwise airborne separation/recovery and fixed-route platoon coordination are admitted after targeted independent discovery; they have outputs absent from aggregate flow management and individual visit routing.
Merge rule: shared names, algorithms or formulations are insufficient. Merge only scopes with the same independent delivered industrial decision. Preserve explicit integrations through interfaces and multiple required constructions rather than creating a duplicate card for every pair of problems.
Split tests retained: physical hubs/service topology; fleet acquisition/type assignment/identified rotations; shipment itineraries/load grouping/physical packing/vessel stowage; appointment promises/dock work; initial yard placement/relocation moves; rail blocking/yard shunting/network traffic; warehouse slotting/batching/pick tours/station-pod work/release; request dispatch/live service regulation/disruption repair.

EXCLUSIONS, VARIANTS AND RETURNS
Crew pairing and driver rostering -> HR. Crew travel connections alone do not justify LOG ownership when employment legality defines feasibility.
Inventory positioning, supplier selection and replenishment policy -> SCM. Inventory-routing is a coupled ownership test, not an automatically LOG-owned umbrella.
Distribution-center network design -> SCM when stock availability dominates; transport depot/hub network -> HUB when journeys/transshipment dominate.
Airline revenue management, cargo pricing, tender award and commercial acceptance -> MKT. Passenger rerouting after an operating disruption can be RECOVERY when physical journey repair is the artifact.
Ambulance location/dispatch with clinical service conditions -> HC. Generic fleet relocation without those conditions -> EMPTY.
Security-constrained convoy/defense mission design -> INTEL when adversarial constraints dominate. Defense freight transport with fixed missions -> VRP/SHIPMENT.
Utility network operation and charging infrastructure electrical feasibility -> ENER; civic charging-site investment can be CIVIC. Route-integrated charge stops -> VRP/ROTATION variants. Independent vehicle-charger/time commitments -> CHARGING when transport duty readiness dominates.
Traffic signal control, civic mobility policy, highway construction and public-network investment -> CIVIC when shared public-service substrate decisions dominate. Journey timing/path allocation remains a separate LOG contract.
Warehouse staffing and picker rosters -> HR. Assigning supplied pick work to available stations/resources can be a LOG secondary MAP in the picking schedule.
Manufacturing lot formation, fabrication cutting/nesting and robotic production cycles -> PROD; LOG handles supplied products without constitutive transformation decisions.
Forecasting, queueing analysis, travel-time estimation, simulation and machine-learning prediction are supporting analyses. They are not stand-alone ICO decisions without a combinatorial action artifact.
CVRP, VRPTW, pickup-delivery, dial-a-ride, multi-trip, multi-depot, cold-chain, hazardous-cargo, electric-route and drone-synchronized tours -> VRP variants unless a distinct upstream or downstream output is independently committed.
Pure TSP, set covering, assignment, flow, bin packing and RCPSP are formal architectures, not automatically independent industrial problem identities.
Integrated location-routing, inventory-routing, loading-routing, batching-routing and fleet-crew recovery preserve constituent decision contracts and their coupling. Do not promote every integration as an extra generic Problem.

RATINGS AND STRUCTURAL INTERPRETATION
Ratings are explicit analytical judgments against 000_DOMAIN.txt, not survey popularity counts or quantified ROI. Domain-wide routing, service topology and live request commitment have Critical Priority; rail, maritime, airport and automation-specific functions generally have Medium Priority despite substantial value. Irreversible fleet/hub investments and network-wide disruptions may have Critical Relevance. High applies where repeated movement or handling cost and throughput mechanisms are substantial but domain-wide enterprise impact is not assumed.
Critical Hardness is reserved for large coupled service-network and multi-resource recovery stacks with strong decomposition needs. High indicates meaningful algorithm/model engineering. Medium retains routine polynomial restrictions and manageable local decisions even where integrated variants are hard. Formal hardness does not determine these ratings by itself.
SELECT means selecting operating entities/configurations/actions; MAP means assigning objects to supplied or constructed targets; LINK means choosing journey or service succession/connections; PLACE means choosing spatial/temporal/ordered positions. Capacity, balance, connectivity, nonoverlap, collision, precedence, stability and legality are constraints/laws, not additional structures by themselves.
Primary Structure describes the complete declared artifact. Restricted variants can omit a secondary when its decision is fixed externally. Candidate source methods and operational policies remain alternatives; no source's use of an auxiliary arc/column transfers to Structure automatically.
Complexity lower-bound inferences used here: general visit routing contains TSP; fleet design with route output contains capacitated routing; consolidation with unconstrained unit activation contains bin packing; physical multi-unit loading contains packing; unrestricted layout with fixed slots contains QAP; complete recovery with frozen work empty contains its routing/scheduling parent. These are stated as restriction arguments, not fabricated source theorems. Polynomial shortest path, bipartite assignment/matching, minimum-cost flow and DAG path cover are reference restrictions. Additional exact class claims require a model-specific source.

AIRCONFLICT â€” Airborne Conflict Resolution and Trajectory Recovery
Decision contract: Selects aircraft separation maneuvers or discrete recovery times and places trajectory changes under pairwise separation and waypoint-return limits.
Identity, variants, boundaries and evidence interpretation: Unlike AIRFLOW, this artifact explicitly resolves pairwise aircraft separation rather than aggregate sector-capacity occupancy. Discrete maneuver alternatives or recovery epochs must be genuine chosen actions; binary variables used only to encode continuous trajectory constraints do not establish a combinatorial contract. Pure continuous heading/speed control without such choices is outside this ICO card. The primary Dias-Rey study uses continuous speed and heading but discrete trajectory recovery time, which supplies a defensible admitted version. A separate disjunctive study supports speed, heading and altitude control architectures. Supplied initial trajectories and protected distances define the substrate; selected maneuvers and positioned changes form the artifact. Scheduled flight or path commitments supply targets and return points. Technical separation modeling is distinct from a claim of certified operational control. Safety-based Critical Relevance is an analytical judgment; published numerical solvability is not a deployment claim.
Ontology inference: primary LOG; Regime #dispatch + #adapt because issues immediate separation actions and revises committed trajectories. Structure #place + #select because places trajectory-change or recovery epochs and selects admissible maneuvers; pairwise separation constrains the trajectories.
Rating inference: Priority Medium â€” Airborne separation actions are specific to operating flight traffic. Relevance Critical â€” Feasible separation decisions protect physical flight operation. Industrial Hardness High â€” Pairwise conflicts, nonlinear motion and recovery commitments require rapid coordinated search. Formal restriction: No model-specific class is claimed.
Template basis: IE Air-traffic conflict resolution and trajectory recovery; OR Disjunctive separation MIP/MINLP; iterative avoidance and recovery optimization. These are defensible architectural references; scope-specific method dominance remains case-layer work.
Evidence: S01, S02. Descriptors: ##aircraft-separation ##trajectory-recovery ##conflict-resolution.
Admission: retain the declared independent physical-movement or handling decision; restricted and integrated versions remain variants as stated.

DEPENDENCY INTERPRETATION
Every edge below is an analytical industrial-flow inference tied to the declared scopes, not a mathematical reduction. The primary sources support the operating pipeline or coupled decisions; they do not prescribe this ontology's exact graph. Edges are conditional: a site need not deploy every Problem. The graph records direct decision artifacts used as inputs, not every possible common datum.
F = first-pass commitment flow. R = operational feedback or re-planning return. Both enter foreground dependencies. A supplied legacy system can provide an input without solving its upstream Problem anew.
Known cycles: rotation/maintenance; berth/handling; station-pod planning/robot traffic; replenishment/pick readiness; release/sortation and dynamic batching; runway/surface; empty repositioning/dispatch; recovery returns to issued plans. These represent coupled or rolling workflows, not errors to erase.
Compilation can topologically order F edges, then retain R edges as intentional backward returns. Cyclic dependencies do not admit a full topological order. The compiler must explain the first-pass/feedback convention and avoid claiming an exact global minimum-backward-edge result.

TEMPLATE AND EVIDENCE LIMITS
The problem layer establishes defensible IE/OR architectures. It does not rank all current algorithms across all industrial variants. Classic exact models, decomposition, specialized dynamic programming, constructive policies and modern neighborhood search are retained where their operating conditions differ materially.
Direct MILP/CP is a model architecture, not a claim that a commercial solver routinely proves optimality at full industrial scale. A source's benchmark or simulation supports that stated model, not every constraint in the proposed industrial scope. Published methods, deployed practices and public implementations remain distinguishable: wave release has official operational documentation and simulation-based policy evidence; vehicle routing has public solver evidence with release-dependent algorithms; robotic paths have strong simulation evidence; stowage has explicit industrial-representativeness limitations.
Known uncertainty remains about the strongest broadly reproducible method for full handling-system design, integrated fleet maintenance, sortation, internal replenishment and multi-resource recovery. Credible architectures exist, so these are not forced unsupported admissions. Method dominance, benchmark adequacy, licenses and reference results require separate case-layer research.
No ontology changes are proposed here. The known absence of a normative Case card schema does not block the requested Problem layer and is not resolved by inventing a local schema.
