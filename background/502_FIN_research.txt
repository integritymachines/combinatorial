v0.1 | Problem layer | 2026-10-07
Normative: 000_DOMAIN.txt v0.4 and background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt v0.4.

PURPOSE AND TERRITORY

FIN owns decisions whose delivered artifact commits or revises financial positions, claims, liquidity, settlement, accounting matches or insurance-risk rules. A bank, insurer or treasury department is an industry context, not sufficient evidence of FIN ownership. This research covers corporate finance; investment and pension management; bank credit; treasury; securities finance; payment and securities infrastructures; insurance and reinsurance; accounting assurance; and financial-system resolution. Public finance is included when the decision is financial claim or liquidity management.

The ICO admission test is a material choice over identifiable contracts, holdings, lots, obligations, transfers, matches, procedures or rules. Continuous quantities may accompany that construction. Discretizing a continuous control, adding scenario indicators to a probability constraint, or encoding a valuation model with integer variables does not by itself create an industrial combinatorial decision. Cash forecasting, credit scoring, reserve estimation and margin calculation normally supply inputs. Their predictions are not separate Problems here.

Principal interfaces:
FIN–MKT: FIN chooses positions, financial counterparties, credit commitments and post-trade actions. MKT designs offers, sets commercial prices, clears bids and assigns trading rights. A loan-selection contract constrained by credit risk is FIN; a pure loan-price offer is MKT. A CCP auction's winner determination is MKT, even though the defaulted inventory is financial. FIN may consume its results.
FIN–SCM: FIN finances existing receivables, schedules invoice payments and manages money. SCM chooses suppliers, orders, stock and replenishment. Working-capital constraints alone do not transfer an integrated supply plan to FIN.
FIN–LOG: FIN settles asset and money claims; LOG moves physical cash or commodities. ATM cash replenishment, cash-in-transit routing and physical delivery are excluded.
FIN–ENER/LAND/EXTR/PROC/PROD/CIVIC: FIN finances or hedges supplied exposures. Physical operating plans, site or block selection and engineering investment choices belong to their owning Domain when physical feasibility defines the decision.
FIN–HR: FIN selects accounting assurance tests or collection treatments; HR assigns and schedules accountants, auditors, claim handlers or collectors where skills and workforce capacity define the contract.
FIN–INTEL: FIN reconciles and verifies ordinary financial records. Adversarial fraud detection, anti-money-laundering investigation allocation and sanctions evasion interdiction require separate INTEL ownership analysis.
FIN–COMP: FIN defines financial actions; COMP defines compute, communication and ledger infrastructure. Cryptographic encoding and digital-asset implementation do not change the financial contract's ownership.

EVIDENCE CONVENTION

Sources are identified by S-numbers below. Retrieved paper abstracts establish problem and Template existence; an abstract is not evidence that the complete implementation is reproducible. Official product documentation establishes reported operational capability, not optimality or comparative performance. Patent disclosures establish an architecture, not production adoption or best performance. No proprietary solver is described as independently reproduced.

Candidate keys below are mnemonic research handles, not permanent problem numbers or final sequence. Candidate Regime, Structure, ratings and dependencies are reasoned ontology interpretations unless a source explicitly states the underlying industrial commitment. Priority and Relevance are analyst judgments against the normative scales. Hardness concerns the specified scope, not every continuous special case.

For structural analysis: supplied prices, exposures, eligibility edges, account permissions, dates and scenario trees are inputs. Selected financial actions, asset-to-obligation assignments, account allocations, chosen transition rules, execution dates and accounting matches are outputs. Balance, legal eligibility, risk budgets and atomicity constrain these outputs; they are not automatically additional Structures. Continuous amounts and scenario indicators are auxiliary or associated quantities unless they materially construct a discrete artifact.

CANDIDATE DOSSIERS

CAPITAL — Capital-Rationed Investment Selection
Decision: choose among supplied investment or acquisition commitments under multi-period financial budgets, exclusivity and financial return requirements. The physical engineering plan is fixed input. Corporate capital budgeting and capital-rationed project selection are aliases; industry-specific plant or infrastructure design is outside this scope.
Evidence: S01 reports an implemented corporate financial-planning MIP with investment and financing decisions. S02 establishes capital-budgeting models with discrete recourse. The stand-alone financial selection component is retained; the integrated investment-financing program is a coupling, not a third Problem.
Candidate Regime #plan; Structure #select. Candidate ratings High/Critical/High. IE Template: capital budgeting and financial investment appraisal. OR Template: binary or mixed-integer capital-rationing models; stochastic or robust recourse when uncertainty is material. Appraisal estimates and NPV calculation are inputs, not Templates for combinatorial selection. No universal best risk model is established.
Formal basis: the single-period restriction with indivisible commitments, independent values and one budget is 0–1 knapsack; NP-hardness follows for the stated family. Industrial difficulty comes from multi-period funding and mutually dependent commitments.
Boundary: eligibility of physical projects is supplied by their owning Domain. Descriptors: capital-rationing, project-selection, CORPORATE.

FUNDING — Funding and Debt-Issuance Planning
Decision: select financing instruments, facility activations, refinancing actions and their commitment dates to satisfy a supplied financing requirement. Issuance, borrowing and debt maturity planning are variants; floating-rate valuation and continuous capital-structure ratios alone are excluded.
Evidence: S01 includes debt creation and repayment in an implemented multiperiod financial program. S03 explicitly includes investment and borrowing alternatives, grace periods and piecewise yields in cash management. Separate funding planning is an interpretation of the decision layer, not a claim that S03 studies public bond underwriting.
Candidate Regime #plan; Structure #select + #place when the deliverable includes instrument commitment dates. Ratings High/Critical/High. IE: maturity-ladder and financing-program planning. OR: multiperiod cash-flow MILP with fixed activations and maturity choices; scenario or robust extensions. Continuous LP is a valid special case and a relaxation, not evidence that all funding is ICO.
Formal basis: minimum-cost coverage by indivisible financing commitments contains covering knapsack. Covenant, currency, refinancing and scenario coupling create industrial difficulty. Long-term funding commitments constrain later cash plans; cash forecasts return information without creating a mandatory reverse edge.
Descriptors: funding, debt-maturity, BANKING, CORPORATE.

ALM — Discrete Asset–Liability Planning
Decision: choose an asset and financing policy over future states to service supplied insurance, pension or bank liabilities under solvency, liquidity and transaction restrictions. Liability-driven investment, cash-flow matching and dedication with bond lots are variants. Selecting a liability-driven policy differs from selecting a single-period return-risk portfolio.
Evidence: S04 studies multistage mixed-integer ALM; S05 applies this architecture to pension funds. S06 studies asset-to-liability assignments with simulation and repeated integer optimization. Discrete assignments and instrument activations establish ICO scope; a purely continuous scenario portfolio does not.
Candidate Regime #plan; Structure #select + #place for the staged instrument-action policy. An explicit asset-to-liability assignment variant additionally requires #map; do not impose assignment on a cash-flow-pool formulation. Ratings High/Critical/Critical.
IE: liability-driven asset and funding planning. OR: multistage stochastic MILP and decomposition; simulation-supported integer search for explicit asset–liability matching. No single architecture dominates across banks, pensions and insurance portfolios. Deterministic lot-based cash-flow matching is a smaller variant.
Formal basis: discrete instrument coverage contains covering knapsack. Scenario branching, nonanticipativity and solvency recourse cause industrial difficulty. Dependencies on funding, risk transfer and target holdings are conditional; avoid a fabricated universal ALM workflow.

BONUS — Insurance Classification and Transition-Rule Design
Decision: design the allowed claim-history-driven transitions between insurance experience classes and, when coupled, class premium relativities. Bonus–malus design and experience-rating transition design are aliases. Applying a fixed rule to one policy is deterministic administration, not another Problem.
Evidence: S07 gives an IP for transitions with fixed premium scales. S08 jointly chooses transitions and the premium scale with MILP. These sources directly support a discrete insurance-rule artifact, unlike ordinary continuous price optimization.
Candidate Regime #design; Structure #link for the selected transitions. Existing classes and event labels are supplied; the solver constructs their successor relationships. Selecting class membership only after fixed transitions would describe another contract. Ratings Medium/High/High.
IE: actuarial experience-rating system design. OR: transition-rule IP or joint transition/relativity MILP. Markov-chain evaluation supplies long-run class occupancy and actuarial performance; it is not itself the combinatorial construction. The primary papers support a Template, not a claim of industry-wide use of their exact implementation.
Formal basis: finite constrained transition-rule design evaluated through Markov dynamics; no unsupported general NP-hardness claim. Industrial difficulty is the interaction of rule simplicity, loss histories, occupancy and premium fairness. Pure commercial discounts remain MKT. Descriptors: bonus-malus, markov-chain, INSURANCE.

REINSURANCE — Reinsurance Program Selection
Decision: choose a combination of offered treaty alternatives or discrete layers across insurance lines under premium, retained-risk and counterparty constraints. Quota-share, excess-of-loss, aggregate and retrocession are variants when they share this program-selection contract. Continuous indemnity-function optimization is outside the card unless paired with material discrete treaty choices.
Evidence: S09 explicitly supplies a finite treaty menu for each line, including no cover, and evaluates combined programs by simulation and Pareto filtering. S10 retains evolutionary multiobjective search as a competing method for articulated programs. The Template must retain this supported simulation-based architecture instead of asserting that every program is a standard MILP.
Candidate Regime #plan; Structure #select. Ratings High/Critical/High. IE: treaty-program comparison and retained-loss stress analysis. OR: scenario simulation with finite-menu enumeration and Pareto filtering; evolutionary search for larger nonlinear program spaces. Scenario MILP is admissible only where the contractual payoff representation supports it, not a universal replacement.
Formal basis: finite coupled menu selection; exhaustive alternatives grow multiplicatively across lines. No general complexity theorem is claimed from that growth alone. Industrial difficulty involves loss dependence, reinstatements and nonlinear risk evaluation.
Dependencies: supplied classification and underwriting assumptions influence risk-transfer design; selected treaties alter ALM liability cash flows. Descriptors: reinsurance, treaty-selection, INSURANCE.

PORTFOLIO — Discrete Investment Portfolio Selection
Decision: choose investable instruments and discrete holdings under risk-return targets, cardinality, minimum holding and concentration requirements. Cardinality-constrained portfolio selection and lot-constrained asset allocation are variants. Risk metric, robust model and solver family are not separate Problems.
Evidence: S11 establishes cardinality and holding bounds with several heuristic methods. S12 supplies the corresponding OR-Library datasets. S13 gives mixed-integer portfolio methods with tail-risk scenarios. The known source data are discovery pointers only; no Case has been selected or benchmark run.
Candidate Regime #allocate; Structure #select. Ratings Critical/Critical/High. IE: constrained portfolio construction. OR: convex MIQP for mean-variance objectives or scenario MILP for piecewise-linear tail-risk objectives; exact MIP and matheuristics are material alternatives. Continuous asset weights accompany the selected holdings and do not automatically add #map.
Formal basis: cardinality-constrained mean-variance optimization is NP-hard in general; S14 supports that qualification. Estimates, asset universes, scenario count and concentration restrictions drive practical difficulty. Initial target selection differs from revising held positions and from executing a supplied trade list.
Descriptors: cardinality, portfolio, ASSETMANAGEMENT.

INDEX — Sparse Index Tracking
Decision: choose a limited instrument subset and holdings to reproduce a supplied financial index under tracking, cost and investment rules. Enhanced indexation and sparse benchmark replication are variants. Index design itself is outside this contract.
Evidence: S15 provides related MILP formulations for tracking and enhancement. S16 records the public OR-Library tracking data and papers. S17 includes portfolio rebalancing across an investment horizon. These show a persistent benchmark-replication decision distinct from choosing a free risk-return portfolio.
Candidate Regime #allocate; Structure #select. Ratings High/High/High. IE: index-fund replication and tracking-error control. OR: cardinality-constrained tracking MILP or MIQP, with transaction-aware MIP for repeated tracking. Choice of error metric changes the formulation, not ownership or identity.
Formal basis: NP-hard for sparse index-tracking variants (S61); this claim does not cover continuous full replication. Industrial difficulty comes from correlated constituents, concentration rules and turnover. Author-repository data establish available instance families, not a current licensing or reproducibility assessment.
Boundary: fixed benchmark distinguishes INDEX from PORTFOLIO; existing holdings create a conditional interface with REBALANCE. Descriptors: index-tracking, benchmark-replication, ASSETMANAGEMENT.

CREDIT — Credit Commitment and Loan-Pool Selection
Decision: accept or acquire identifiable credit exposures to meet yield, expected-loss, concentration, budget and securitization constraints. Credit admission, loan portfolio selection and CLO collateral-pool selection share the exposure-selection contract; deal-specific waterfall evaluation is a variant.
Evidence: S18 includes explicit multiloan selection in a portfolio-risk-aware framework. Its price variable is a coupled MKT interface, not a reason to include pure pricing. S19 directly studies discrete credit selection for CLO portfolios and labels the loan-selection task NP-hard. S20 provides an additional primary loan-portfolio study.
Candidate Regime #allocate; Structure #select. Ratings High/Critical/High. IE: credit portfolio and collateral-pool construction. OR: risk-constrained MILP/MINLP with convex reformulations or linearized tranche-loss approximations. Pricing and acceptance-probability estimates are inputs where the credit commitments are the delivered artifact.
Formal basis: NP-hard for the discrete loan-pool scope supported by S19. Correlated defaults, tranche cash flows and borrower acceptance complicate industrial solutions. Reoptimization of a loan pool stays a variant; securities settlement and collection are downstream only where actual acquisition or repayment obligations exist.
Descriptors: credit-selection, securitization, BANKING. Consumer scoring without action selection is excluded.

HEDGE — Discrete Hedge Construction
Decision: select derivative instruments, contract lots and maturities to cover supplied currency, rate, commodity or freight exposures under risk, liquidity and transaction limits. Static option hedging and discrete industrial financial hedging are variants. The underlying physical production, procurement or dispatch plan is supplied.
Evidence: S21 explicitly uses implementable discrete hedges and mixed-integer mean-CVaR modeling. S22 supplies option-portfolio LP/MILP constructions for claims. S23 distinguishes operational and financial commodity hedging; only the financial action contract is retained here.
Candidate Regime #allocate; Structure #select. Ratings High/Critical/High. IE: exposure-to-instrument hedging with maturity alignment. OR: lot-constrained scenario MILP or mixed-integer convex risk optimization. Exposure labels do not imply #map unless indivisible hedges are actually assigned to individual obligations. Contract maturities are supplied alternatives; selecting a maturity is not placement into freely chosen execution dates.
Formal basis: exact exposure matching by selectable integer lots contains subset sum. Industrial difficulty comes from basis risk, correlated factors and margin consumption. Continuous hedge ratios are excluded unless a discrete implementation choice is material.
Descriptors: hedging, contract-lots, CORPORATE. Financial hedging consumes exposure plans from other Domains without assuming their ownership.

CASH — Treasury Cash and Liquidity Planning
Decision: choose and schedule cash transfers, short-term borrowing and investment actions across supplied accounts to maintain future balances under activation fees, minimum transfers, currency and availability constraints. Cash positioning, cash concentration and cash-pool operation are variants. Legal cash-pool architecture is supplied; no unsupported separate pool-topology optimization Problem is forced.
Evidence: S03 supports robust MIP cash management with investment and borrowing restrictions. S24 supports stochastic cash-flow models. S25 establishes actual cash-pooling practice; S26 supports an integrated cash-pool/supply-chain model, whose physical operating decisions remain SCM.
Candidate Regime #plan + #operate; Structure #place + #select. Ratings Critical/Critical/High. IE: balance projection, cash positioning and cash-pool sweeps. OR: multiperiod cash-flow network MILP with fixed actions; robust or stochastic extensions. Live sweeps can reuse a reduced model, while the card covers the committed treasury action plan.
Formal basis: fixed-charge cash transfer design contains fixed-charge flow. Industrial difficulty involves cross-entity eligibility, uncertain receipts and minimum balances. Funding instruments are upstream; reconciled actual balances feed the next planning iteration.
Descriptors: cash-pooling, liquidity, CORPORATE, BANKING. Currency conversion that is purely continuous is not a separate ICO Problem.

PAYABLE — Invoice Payment Scheduling
Decision: choose which approved invoices to pay and their dates under cash availability, due dates, early-payment discounts and penalties. Accounts-payable optimization and discount capture are aliases. This decision creates a payment instruction, not an actual RTGS settlement result.
Evidence: S27 models supplier-payment timing with integer programming for a supplied financial supply chain. S28 discloses a predictive invoice-payment worklist architecture; that disclosure is not a comparative industrial performance claim. Accounting approval and forecast models supply eligibility and timing inputs.
Candidate Regime #plan + #dispatch; Structure #place + #select. Ratings High/High/Medium. IE: approved-payables calendar and discount-window management. OR: time-indexed payment MILP with cash-balance constraints; rolling reoptimization when cash changes. Do not infer a sequence relation unless payment order within the same period materially affects balances.
Formal basis: one-period discount capture under a cash budget contains knapsack. Industrial difficulty arises from cash timing, invoice windows and payment bundling, though many bounded departmental models are routine MIP work. Supplier choice is SCM; pure commercial term design is MKT.
Dependencies: CASH constrains schedules; NETTING can replace gross payables with residuals; schedules feed PAYMENT. Descriptors: payment-scheduling, discount-windows, ACCOUNTING.

COLLECT — Receivable Recovery Action Selection
Decision: choose permitted recovery treatments or repayment-plan offers for delinquent financial claims under contact, cost and policy limits. Collections optimization and debt-treatment selection are aliases. Collector rostering and field visits are separate HR/LOG contracts.
Evidence: S29 directly discloses constrained optimization over debtors and collection strategies. It establishes action-selection architecture, not best-practice performance, and supplies no public operational benchmark. Expected recoveries must be estimated separately; the optimization consumes those estimates.
Candidate Regime #dispatch + #adapt; Structure #select + #map when a treatment is assigned to each claim. Ratings Medium/High/Medium. IE: treatment segmentation and recovery-policy constraints. OR: constrained treatment-assignment MILP or budgeted action selection. Statistical ranking alone is an input baseline, not an optimizer for shared action limits.
Formal basis: selecting one costed action per debtor under a shared budget contains multiple-choice knapsack. Industrial difficulty concerns response estimates, repeated decisions and rules that restrict treatment combinations. No inference is made that a patent's example collector assignments should be FIN-owned.
Dependencies: recovery estimates influence cash forecasts; actual reconciled payments revise claims for the next treatment cycle. Descriptors: receivables, collections, BANKING. Credible public case evidence remains a later research need.

LEND — Securities-Lending Inventory Allocation
Decision: admit locate or borrow requests and allocate available securities lots to the accepted requests under owner permissions, delivery availability and exposure limits. Locate approval and stock-loan inventory allocation are aliases. Quoting a loan fee or winning an exclusive auction is a distinct MKT contract.
Evidence: S30 explicitly proposes knapsack-based optimization of locate approvals and available inventory. S31 confirms that repo, securities lending and margin compete for the same institutional asset pool. S30 supports a formal method, while S31 supports operational integration; neither establishes a universally superior allocator.
Candidate Regime #allocate + #dispatch; Structure #map + #select. Ratings Medium/High/High. IE: securities-availability and locate-allocation control. OR: knapsack or generalized-assignment MILP with permitted owner/request combinations; bounded heuristics for short latency. Continuous allocation by fungible security is a simpler special case.
Formal basis: indivisible request admission contains knapsack. Cross-owner permissions, recall risk and live reservation states drive practical difficulty. Collateral delivery is a different allocation of assets to collateral obligations, not an alias.
Dependencies: accepted loans create collateral and delivery obligations; failed settlement or recalls return inventory-state information. Descriptors: securities-lending, locate, BANKING.

EXECUTE — Discrete Trade Execution Scheduling
Decision: place the lots of an approved trade list into execution periods under participation, risk and nonconvex transaction costs. Execution scheduling and gradual portfolio transition are aliases. Choosing the final desired holdings belongs to PORTFOLIO or REBALANCE; exchange-side order matching belongs to MKT.
Evidence: S32 explicitly optimizes a fund's rebalance schedule using 0–1 IP and nonconvex transaction costs. Its staged trade action is the scope here. Continuous liquidation models alone do not establish this discrete card; public execution data and market-response estimates need later case evaluation.
Candidate Regime #operate + #dispatch; Structure #place + #select for temporal lots and their active periods. Ratings High/High/High. IE: portfolio-transition scheduling and participation limits. OR: integer trade-schedule models with nonconvex cost; rolling mixed-integer execution under updated liquidity. Venue allocation is optional only if the actual contract requires it.
Formal basis: indivisible lot placement under period capacities includes bin packing. Nonconvex market costs, pathwise risk and live liquidity create industrial difficulty. Target quantities and securities available for sale are inputs.
Dependencies: selected target or revised holdings feed execution; executed trades feed account allocation and securities settlement. Descriptors: trade-execution, market-impact, ASSETMANAGEMENT.

REBALANCE — Portfolio Rebalancing
Decision: choose changes to held positions under revised targets, transaction activations, turnover, trading lots and liquidity. Portfolio transition and transaction-aware portfolio revision are aliases; generic changes in risk metric are variants. A revision contract has initial holdings and action costs, unlike fresh portfolio construction.
Evidence: S33 presents a quadratic MIP with funding changes and transaction costs; S34 models horizon-aware rebalance with a transaction budget. S35 models complex transaction-cost schedules. Templates retain MIQP and specialized search without treating one paper's method as universal SOTA.
Candidate Regime #adapt; Structure #select. Ratings High/Critical/High. IE: transaction-aware portfolio transition. OR: MIQP or mixed-integer convex rebalancing with lot and fixed-cost actions; matheuristics for larger universes. Execution dates belong to EXECUTE unless included in the revision contract.
Formal basis: the unrestricted held-position case includes discrete portfolio selection. Industrial difficulty comes from turnover, funding changes and path-dependent restrictions. Tax-aware lot disposal is a separate selectable accounting artifact when a target disposal quantity is supplied; integrated tax-aware rebalance is a coupling.
Dependencies: targets and cash limits feed revisions; selected trades feed tax-lot realization and execution. Descriptors: rebalancing, turnover, ASSETMANAGEMENT.

TAXLOT — Tax-Lot Disposal Selection
Decision: choose which acquired lots to dispose of to meet a supplied asset-sale requirement or loss-realization target under basis, holding-period and permitted-trade constraints. Tax-lot harvesting and lot realization are variants. Selecting a continuous sale amount without discrete disposal or buy/sell choices does not meet this scope.
Evidence: S36 constructs a tax-aware trade list with individual tax-lot sales and discusses mixed-integer convex optimization and a fast two-stage convex approach. It establishes an integrated tax-aware portfolio Template. The fixed-sale, indivisible-lot decision here is a restricted industrial interpretation; no claim is made that all lot-based tax accounting is mixed-integer.
Candidate Regime #allocate; Structure #select. Ratings Medium/High/Medium. IE: tax-basis-aware disposal and loss realization. OR: lot-selection MILP for supplied sale quantities; mixed-integer convex tax-aware portfolio models for coupled return-risk decisions. Preserve the source's convex approximation as a competing implementation when its conditions apply.
Formal basis: exact disposal of indivisible lot quantities contains subset sum; divisible per-lot sales with linear taxes can be LP. Industrial difficulty concerns trade direction, taxable gains and cross-trade restrictions, not tax-rate arithmetic.
Tax eligibility is a supplied jurisdiction-specific rule set. No tax advice or universally valid wash-sale rule is asserted. Descriptors: tax-lots, basis, ASSETMANAGEMENT.

MARGIN — Margin-Account Position Allocation
Decision: assign eligible financial positions or contract lots among allowed clearing accounts to reduce aggregate margin under account permissions and position-preservation requirements. Portfolio margin optimization and cross-margin account allocation are aliases. Computing a fixed account's margin is an input operation, not this Problem.
Evidence: S37 documents a CME optimizer that allocates futures and options into an OTC cleared account. S38 documents extension across segregated, IRS and FICC cross-margin accounts. S39 discloses inter-account search architecture. Sources establish the operational assignment contract; they do not establish a public solver implementation or global-optimality guarantee.
Candidate Regime #allocate + #adapt; Structure #map. Ratings Medium/High/High. IE: cross-margin position alignment. OR: constrained integer account allocation with a portfolio-margin evaluation oracle; local or derivative-free search and surrogates where margin is black-box. A generic MIQP is not asserted to implement the commercial optimizer.
Formal basis: finite integer allocation with a portfolio-dependent margin oracle; arbitrary oracle hardness is not converted into an unsupported theorem about the actual service. Nonadditive offsets and account restrictions drive industrial difficulty.
Dependencies: held or executed positions feed account assignment; resulting margin obligations feed COLLATERAL. Descriptors: cross-margin, account-allocation, BANKING.

COLLATERAL — Collateral Allocation and Substitution
Decision: assign eligible asset lots to margin, repo and secured-credit obligations and select substitutions as eligibility, values or obligations change. Cheapest-to-deliver allocation, collateral rebalancing and collateral mobilization are variants within the asset-to-obligation contract. Contract creation is upstream funding or lending.
Evidence: S40 reports deployed integrated MIPs for tri-party repo collateral and settlement. S41 provides LP/MILP under-allocation and movement-control formulations. S31 establishes the enterprise asset-pool context. Large historical figures in S40 describe that deployment and are not current market totals.
Candidate Regime #allocate + #adapt; Structure #map + #select. Ratings Critical/Critical/High. IE: eligibility and haircut-based collateral delivery with controlled substitutions. OR: collateral-allocation MILP; LP relaxations, staged allocation and movement penalties. Fungible divisible assets with simple coverage can be LP; lot indivisibility and movement activations define the ICO scope.
Formal basis: assigning indivisible assets to coverage obligations contains bin covering or generalized assignment restrictions. Industrial difficulty comes from eligibility, haircuts, concentration, custodian partitions and substitution limits.
Dependencies: financing, lending and margin account choices define obligations; allocations supply securities for settlement; updated settlement states change next-iteration availability. Descriptors: collateral, repo, haircuts, BANKING.

NETTING — Integral Obligation Netting
Decision: select compatible whole invoices or obligations for multilateral offset and assign a feasible offset date under participant consent, cash requirements and legal netting rules. Integral B2B clearing and invoice-cycle netting are aliases. Fixed deterministic bilateral subtraction is a calculation, not a separate optimization.
Evidence: S42 studies whole-invoice integral settlement on a directed business network with financing trade-offs. S43 is a bibliographic discovery pointer for further temporal research, not primary method evidence. S44 supplies reference code and invoice data, retained as discovery leads only; no case validity or access rights are presumed.
Candidate Regime #operate; Structure #select + #place when common-day offsets are chosen. The debt graph is supplied. #link is unnecessary unless the contract explicitly permits construction of replacement claim relations. Ratings High/High/High.
IE: consent-based multilateral netting and residual-payment calculation. OR: whole-obligation subset optimization with cash-balance constraints; cycle/path search and integer programming where atomic invoice constraints apply. Continuous cycle cancellation and deterministic account netting are simpler restrictions.
Formal basis: fixed financing for indivisible obligations can include knapsack-type selection; no general NP-hardness claim is inferred from the phrase netting. Temporal intersections and consent constraints drive industrial difficulty.
Dependencies: confirmed claims feed netting; selected offsets define residual payables and settlement instructions. Descriptors: multilateral-netting, atomic-invoices, CORPORATE.

COMPRESS — Derivative Portfolio Compression
Decision: cancel or replace derivative contracts to reduce gross exposure while preserving agreed net risk and respecting bilateral tolerance, permitted counterparties and contract granularity. Trade tear-up and constrained portfolio compression are aliases. Compression differs from invoice netting because the delivered artifact revises future outstanding contracts rather than settling current payable claims.
Evidence: S45 studies compression and its network conditions using CDS transactions. S46 shows that conservative divisible compression has an LP formulation and that systemic-risk effects require separate analysis. Discrete whole-contract selection or contract-count restrictions define the ICO variant admitted here; the sources do not establish all compression as NP-hard or all compression as safer.
Candidate Regime #adapt; Structure #select + #link when replacements are constructed. Selection chooses cancellations; linking specifies permitted replacement counterparties. Pure conservative tear-up uses selection alone. Ratings Medium/High/High.
IE: risk-preserving post-trade portfolio compression. OR: network-flow LP for divisible reference models; whole-contract mixed-integer network models when indivisibility or activation restrictions apply. The latter is an explicit formulation inference, not a claimed implementation of S45.
Formal basis: exact whole-contract cancellation includes subset sum; divisible conservative compression is LP. Industrial difficulty concerns risk tolerances, participant consent and contract attributes.
Dependencies: existing positions feed compression; replacement positions alter margin and collateral demands. Descriptors: compression, risk-preservation, BANKING.

PAYMENT — Payment Settlement Queue Optimization
Decision: select payments from a live queue for atomic release under available account balances, priorities and liquidity-saving rules. RTGS queue optimization and gridlock resolution are aliases. The payment instructions exist before this decision; payable scheduling creates them.
Evidence: S47 distinguishes conventional fixed-liquidity queue optimization from a newer auction-based liquidity-sharing mechanism. S48 explains the integer selection bottleneck for indivisible payments and the partial-payment relaxation. S49 studies competing queue designs. Auction preference elicitation is a MKT interface, not proof of routine deployment of the new mechanism.
Candidate Regime #dispatch + #operate; Structure #select. A simultaneous release set does not construct a temporal schedule or new interbank links. A sequence-sensitive implementation can additionally require #place; that variant must state its sequential contract. Ratings Critical/Critical/High.
IE: RTGS liquidity-saving queue and priority control. OR: binary payment-release optimization with account-balance constraints; bounded cycle or offset search. Proportional partial-payment LP is a relaxation when all-or-none release is compulsory.
Formal basis: S47 reports NP-hard constrained payment-subset selection; S48 confirms the whole-payment integer bottleneck. A one-account restriction with independent payments and one cash budget is knapsack. Individual unconstrained releases can be trivial. Queue sizes, timing and liquidity coupling drive industrial difficulty.
Dependencies: cash plans and residual obligations supply liquidity and queues; settled payments supply reconciliation and future balances. Descriptors: settlement-queue, gridlock, BANKING.

SECSETTLE — Securities Settlement Batch Optimization
Decision: select and, where sequence matters, order securities deliveries and cash legs for delivery-versus-payment under asset availability, money balances and settlement deadlines. DvP batch selection and securities settlement sequencing are aliases. Portfolio ownership changes and collateral allocations are inputs.
Evidence: S50 establishes DvP's cash–security coupling. S51 reports an INDEVAL LP settlement architecture; S40 supports integrated collateral and settlement MIPs. The Template therefore retains both continuous or divisible reference models and atomic-discrete extensions instead of mislabeling S51 as MILP.
Candidate Regime #dispatch + #operate; Structure #select + #place for a sequential deliverable. Simultaneous atomic batches need selection alone. Ratings High/Critical/High.
IE: DvP eligibility, linked-leg coordination and fail prevention. OR: cash-and-security balance optimization with binary atomic transfers or integer lot sizes; LP when permitted settlement fractions are continuous. Not all delivery matching is a commercial order-matching Problem.
Formal basis: one cash resource and indivisible valued deliveries includes knapsack; fungible continuous settlement can be LP. Cross-security dependencies, atomic linked legs and available inventory drive industrial difficulty.
Dependencies: executed trades, loans and allocated collateral generate eligible transfers; securities settlement supplies reconciliation and next-round collateral inventory. Descriptors: delivery-versus-payment, atomic-settlement, BANKING.

RECONCILE — Transaction Matching and Reconciliation
Decision: choose disjoint matches or record groups across ledgers, statements, invoices and settlements under identity, date, amount and tolerance requirements. Bank reconciliation, cash application and one-to-many transaction matching are variants when the result associates existing records. Correction posting is an authorized subsequent operation, not silently created by a match.
Evidence: S52 documents one-to-one and grouped matching, evaluation order and ambiguity controls. S53 discloses clustering plus MIP for many-to-many balances. S54 reports high-volume grouped matching capability. Official rule engines are an established operational baseline; global optimization is a defensible competing Template, not evidence that a rule engine globally optimizes.
Candidate Regime #operate; Structure #map when records are assigned to common match groups. Pairwise bipartite matching may be represented by #link, but that encoding is not a reason to switch the canonical grouped contract. Ratings Critical/High/High.
IE: rule-based reconciliation with explicit tolerances and exception approval. OR: weighted bipartite matching for pairwise cases; subset/group selection MILP or set packing for aggregated matches. Monetary precision and tolerances are controlled input rules.
Formal basis: weighted bipartite matching is polynomial; grouped exact amount matching contains subset sum and disjoint group selection contains set packing. Ambiguous groups and auditability drive practical difficulty.
Dependencies: settlement supplies records; reconciled balances and claim states feed the next cash and recovery decisions. Descriptors: reconciliation, grouped-matching, ACCOUNTING.

ASSURANCE — Accounting Assurance Procedure Selection
Decision: select financial audit tests and control checks to meet assurance requirements under cost and evidence dependencies. Audit program design and assertion coverage are aliases. Scheduling audit staff belongs to HR; adversarial investigation coverage can belong to INTEL.
Evidence: S55 treats accounting audit planning as sequential covering with reliability of internal controls. S56 is a primary disclosure of an integer procedure-selection tradition; publication metadata alone is retained as corroboration, not detailed method evidence. The card admits ordinary financial assertion coverage, not an all-purpose audit industry bucket.
Candidate Regime #plan; Structure #select. Ratings Medium/High/Medium. IE: financial assertion/control coverage and reliability-based audit planning. OR: covering optimization with sequential evidence updates; binary set-cover models for deterministic independent tests. Bayesian or reliability inference estimates assurance; it is not a replacement for selecting a cost-constrained procedure set.
Formal basis: deterministic assertion coverage by costed selectable tests is weighted set cover and NP-hard. Industrial difficulty comes from dependent evidence and assurance thresholds; modest bounded test sets can be routine MIP work. No claim is made that a 1985 architecture is universal current audit SOTA.
Dependencies: reconciled exceptions can direct a subsequent audit plan. Financial close deadlines are inputs; workforce organization is separate. Descriptors: assurance, assertion-coverage, ACCOUNTING.

RESOLVE — Financial Network Rescue and Debt-Relief Selection
Decision: choose institutions or debt obligations for cash support, guarantees or negotiated debt removal under a rescue budget and modeled clearing/default effects. Systemic bailout selection and debt-relief intervention are variants; calculating a clearing fixed point for supplied interventions is an evaluation subproblem.
Evidence: S57 establishes complexity of bailout and debt-removal optimization and distinguishes default-cost assumptions. S58 studies minimum-cost rescue under overlapping financial cycles. These support decision existence and formal methods, not an established public-sector deployment or a claim that one rescue objective is normative.
Candidate Regime #adapt; Structure #select. Ratings Medium/Critical/Critical. IE: resolution scenario planning with financial contagion stress evaluation. OR: intervention-selection MILP coupled to a clearing model; network-aware greedy policies and bounds. Exact encodings must state limited-liability, priority and default-cost assumptions. A fixed ordinary proportional clearing calculation is not promoted to ICO.
Formal basis: NP-hard for supported default-cost or network rescue variants; S57 has a polynomial LP case without default costs. Industrial difficulty comes from contagion, equilibrium assumptions and uncertain claims. The financial network is supplied, so #link is not inferred from its existence.
Dependency: current obligations and reconciled positions feed stress models; intervention choices revise funding and liquidity limits in a new commitment cycle. Descriptors: resolution, financial-network, PUBLICFINANCE.

ALIASES, EXCLUSIONS AND OPEN COVERAGE

Merge: mean-variance/CVaR/VaR/robust/cardinality/lot-size portfolio models under PORTFOLIO; enhanced indexation under INDEX; mortgage/CLO loan pools under CREDIT when credit admission dominates; all financing instrument choices under FUNDING; corporate sweeps and cash-pool operation under CASH; tri-party repo and margin collateral under COLLATERAL; gross/net queue and gridlock variants under PAYMENT; one-to-one/one-to-many/cash-application variants under RECONCILE; quota-share/XoL/aggregate/retrocession offered menus under REINSURANCE.
Retain splits: PORTFOLIO/INDEX by free investment objective versus fixed replication target; PORTFOLIO/REBALANCE by fresh target construction versus costly revision of held commitments; REBALANCE/EXECUTE by approved trade list versus temporal action placement; REBALANCE/TAXLOT by aggregate sales versus tax-basis lot realization; MARGIN/COLLATERAL by position-to-account assignment versus asset-to-obligation delivery; NETTING/COMPRESS by settlement of current claims versus revision of outstanding risk contracts; PAYABLE/PAYMENT by instruction creation versus live balance-feasible release; PAYMENT/SECSETTLE by money-only claims versus atomic cash–security coupling. ASSURANCE selects evidence-producing procedures; RECONCILE associates already-existing records.
Do not create generic design/plan/operate/adapt duplicates. Integrated portfolio-funding, ALM-reinsurance, tax-aware rebalancing and collateral-settlement models remain coupled versions with explicitly different outputs. A solver class, QUBO, scenario generator or risk metric is not a Problem.

Excluded or retained only as leads:
- Continuous Markowitz allocation, unconstrained cash-flow matching, continuous reinsurance indemnity, valuation, reserve estimation and capital allocation without discrete commitments: lack a material combinatorial artifact.
- Insurance portfolio/underwriting optimization: S59/S60 support risk and volume models, but scenario indicators alone do not prove selectable policy admission. Distinct individual policy acceptance remains an evidence lead rather than a forced card; generic pricing/offer selection is MKT. Discrete offered risk-transfer contracts are covered by REINSURANCE; invested assets and liabilities by ALM.
- Contract deductible/limit selection without evidence of a discrete financial-risk artifact: do not invent a stand-alone Problem from a discretized actuarial curve. Offered treaty menus are covered; broader primary-insurance menu design needs stronger contract evidence and an MKT boundary check.
- Accounting close task schedules, claim handler assignment, audit staff allocation and call-center staffing: HR where workforce legality defines feasibility.
- Repairer networks, home-repair scheduling, catastrophe field response and cash-in-transit: physical/clinical/workforce decisions belong elsewhere despite insurer or bank ownership.
- CCP default auction design and winners: MKT. Evaluating default funds and fixed waterfalls is normally continuous/rule-based. Selecting whole rescue interventions is RESOLVE. Ordinary deterministic corporate actions and fixed netting are not ICO.
- Insurance model-point compression and scenario selection: statistical/model-representation choices rather than commitments over actual financial claims; do not confuse with derivative contract compression.
- Bankruptcy claim matching: RECONCILE; intervention selection: RESOLVE. Legal case scheduling and unsupported tax-election optimization are not admitted from analogy alone.
- Autonomous accounting posting, financial-statement classification and account hierarchy redesign: recurring accounting functions, but current retrieval did not establish a sufficiently distinct ICO Template. Retain as search gaps.
