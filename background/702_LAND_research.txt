TERRITORY AND ADMISSION
LAND owns the decision when land, geospatial assets, renewable resources, water rights, basins or land-use allocation dominate. The primary test is the promised industrial output and its feasibility contract. A GIS representation, agricultural industry tag, public owner, or mathematical assignment formulation does not establish ownership.

Principal territories are: urban/rural land zoning and readjustment; physical real-estate development and occupancy; conservation and ecological connections; forestry and renewable biomass; annual/perennial crops and protected cultivation; pasture and biological livestock stocks; fisheries and aquaculture; basin resource access and irrigation turns; catchment intervention and ecological recovery. Forestry, fisheries, real estate, retail and water use established industry contexts. Agriculture, orchard, livestock and protected cultivation use open lowercase refinements rather than inventing a closed Industry registry entry.

ICO admission requires a genuine discrete commitment: candidate inclusion, physical assignment, constructed connections, or spatial/temporal placement. Continuous water, nutrient, biomass, flow, financial and yield quantities may accompany a discrete contract. Purely continuous balances, regression, forecasting and simulation alone are not admitted. A binary approximation variable is not proof of an intrinsically combinatorial industrial decision. Conversely an MDP action choice does not become continuous because an occupation-measure LP can represent its policy.

Neighboring-domain ownership follows 000_DOMAIN.txt:
EXTR: prospecting, extraction/depletion, mine blocks and well withdrawal. LAND: resource access rights, basin rationing, renewable harvesting and ecological land recovery. Mine waste emplacement remains EXTR; a separate ecological restoration portfolio is LAND.
ENER: installed utility production/distribution and pump/tank/valve operation. LAND: basin rights/resource access and agronomic irrigation-turn commitments. Mere reservoir imagery does not determine ownership; a basin rationing policy and utility dispatch are distinct contracts.
CIVIC: shared service-asset operation, public works and construction execution. LAND: permitted land uses, physical geospatial development phases and ecological recovery. Integrated forest access design is LAND only when renewable-resource access defines its value and feasible output.
LOG: vehicle journeys, visits, field coverage routes, equipment movement and harvest haulage when movement defines the service. LAND: agronomic or forest work where crop, soil, maturity, stand or renewable-stock conditions define feasibility. Travel constraints do not automatically transfer an integrated biological work calendar to LOG.
SCM: feed/input sourcing, availability, harvested inventory and replenishment. LAND: crop-land, pasture, herd/cohort and resource-harvest commitments. Biological growth-state decisions are not inventory policy merely because states are carried between periods.
PROC/PROD: process recipes/material transformation and manufacturing/assembly respectively. LAND: the renewal and harvesting of biological resources. Food/fish processing stays outside LAND; feed formulation can be PROC.
MKT: pricing, offers, auctions, commercial rights allocation and trading mechanisms. LAND: physical tenant-space allocation and resource-use commitments under supplied rights. FIN: financing structure, property-backed claims, settlement, liquidity and risk positions. Financial limits and discounted objectives do not redefine physical land development as FIN.
HR: standalone workforce skills/capacity/rosters. LAND: integrated field or forest resource execution where biological conditions define the work. HC: clinical/veterinary direct-care conditions when care is promised. COMP: sensing/computation/storage operation; forecasts and GIS supply information. INTEL: adversarial/protective response where those conditions dominate, not every natural threat.

IDENTITY AND STRUCTURAL METHOD
For each candidate, distinguish: supplied substrate; chosen construction; delivered artifact; constitutive laws; feasibility conditions; objectives; auxiliary variables. Priority, Relevance and Hardness are separate judgment axes. Ratings below are synthesis from recurrence, stakes, model coupling and documented solution methods; sources do not themselves award ontology ratings. Evidence-based existence of a Template is required for admission, but a unique global SOTA method is not required. Industrial work-window budgeting, forestry prescriptions and systematic conservation practice can differ from the strongest formal method or a public implementation.

Selection of a treatment subset can accompany assignment of those treatments and their timing when the output explicitly includes all three commitments. Link selection is not duplicated with #select merely because a link variable is binary. Supplied adjacency does not make zoning, crop rotation, reserve selection or fuel scheduling #link. A corridor or access network explicitly promises chosen connections. Scheduling is #place; assignment is #map. The baseline Structure is not a demand to add every Structure used by every integrated variant.

Regime follows the commitment. System configuration and permitted-use patterns are #design. Future crop, stand, cohort, recovery and work calendars are #plan. Physical occupancy allocation is #allocate. Ongoing pasture/irrigation coordination can add #operate. Immediate reassignments add #dispatch where a rolling work-calendar variant is explicitly included. Material revision can add #adapt. Long horizon alone does not imply #design, and repeated solution alone does not imply #realtime.

COMPLEXITY AND RATINGS BASIS
Formal statements apply only to supported variants. General integer programming is not a blanket complexity proof for a particular industrial Problem. A source-specific NP-hard result is not inherited by every special case. For RESERVE, the budgeted coverage subclass contains ordinary budgeted maximum coverage: features are elements, sites cover element sets, costs constrain the subset, and covered feature count is the objective. This is a first-principles classification argument, not a claimed theorem about all ecological models. For FIELD, nonpreemptive renewable-resource task scheduling with precedence contains RCPSP; the formal result applies to that flexible-resource scheduling variant. Restricting resource needs or tasks can yield easier models.

High Hardness denotes recurring custom formulation/search burdens, not merely NP-hardness. Spatial neighborhood interaction, long biological state propagation and expensive hydrological evaluations provide practical reasons. Medium denotes standard MILP/DP architectures that provide defensible routine starting points, with scope-dependent formulation and forecast issues. Where deployment adoption is unverified, that limitation remains explicit. No runtime, optimality gap, financial uplift, crop-yield increase or hazard reduction is invented.

DEPENDENCY EVIDENCE CONVENTION
Edges below are applicable handoffs, not universal prerequisites. E denotes a handoff directly reflected in the described source model or source scope. I denotes industrial/information-flow synthesis across sourced contracts. N denotes a normative ownership or decision-boundary basis. Combined codes preserve inference. No edge is asserted as a formal mathematical reduction. External inputs, including EXTR release dates and SCM demand, remain named interfaces rather than unvalidated external Problem IDs.

CANDIDATE RESEARCH

ZONE — Spatial Land-Use and Resource Zoning
Admission basis: One spatial allocation contract covers urban, rural, coastal and marine units. The solution changes the permitted or committed use of identified spatial units. Assigning uses is distinct from computing suitability scores or predicting uncontrolled land change.
Decision scope: Assigns land parcels or spatial units to permitted uses under suitability, area, adjacency and environmental limits.
Aliases: Land-use allocation; spatial zoning; marine spatial planning; master-plan allocation.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: GIS polygons/cells, allowable uses, incumbents, suitability scores, area targets and neighborhood relations are supplied. The delivered artifact is a unit-to-use assignment. Compactness and neighborhood compatibility constrain or value it; the solver does not create neighborhood edges.
Candidate Regime: #design | Commits the spatial use pattern before development and resource operations.
Candidate Structure: #map | Assigns spatial units to use classes; supplied adjacency constrains the resulting pattern.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Raster and irregular parcels; conversion costs; single and multiple objectives; compatible multi-use zones; land-sea interfaces. Optional land acquisition remains a secondary selection when the promised output includes it.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Urban land allocation is LAND. Construction tasks are CIVIC. Generic facility service coverage follows its operating domain. Marine energy operation is ENER. A geospatial layer does not make all downstream decisions LAND.
IE and OR Template basis: Spatial master planning and land suitability analysis | Multiobjective integer programming and spatial search.
Template evidence: S01: Raster land-use assignment with cost and compactness objectives supports integer programming. Test scales do not establish universal industrial scalability. S39: Multiobjective zoning ILP supports marine-space allocation as a zoning variant, without introducing another Domain.
Priority inference: High | Spatial zoning coordinates competing uses across urban, rural and marine resource systems.
Relevance inference: Critical | Use patterns determine durable resource access and development opportunities.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; large spatial grids and competing compactness and suitability goals require tailored formulations.
Useful descriptors: ##land-use ##spatial-allocation ##adjacency
Evidence limits and uncertainty: Some zoning is qualitative or regulatory rather than ICO. Admit only explicit discrete allocation contracts with a credible Template. No law or planning approval procedure is assumed universal.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Marine spatial zoning shares this contract. Pure continuous acreage balances are excluded.
Source references: S01 S39.

PARCEL — Land Consolidation and Parcel Readjustment
Admission basis: Reallocating physical holdings differs from assigning permitted uses. Owners' entitlements, parcel value and resulting geometry define feasibility. A land market may supply eligible exchanges without changing the physical reallocation contract.
Decision scope: Reassigns discrete land units or ownership interests to consolidated holdings under entitlement, value, access and parcel-shape conditions.
Aliases: Land reallocation; plot consolidation; cadastral readjustment.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Original parcels, owners, value/area entitlements, candidate blocks and access conditions are inputs. The output allocates discrete units or interests to resulting holdings. Valuation is supplied evidence, not the combinatorial output.
Candidate Regime: #design | Defines the land holdings available to later development and cultivation.
Candidate Structure: #map | Assigns land units to resulting holdings or entitled owners.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Rural consolidation; urban reallocation after public-space deductions; discrete whole parcels; divisible ownership interests assigned to indivisible destination lots; owner preferences and shape objectives.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Commercial brokerage alone is MKT. Mortgage or land-backed claim portfolios are FIN. Survey data processing is COMP support. Constructing access roads is CIVIC unless subordinate to a consolidated-holding design.
IE and OR Template basis: Cadastral land consolidation and readjustment | Mixed-integer allocation and geometric optimization.
Template evidence: S02: LP and MIP allocate cadastral interests into subdivision lots after public-use deductions. Supports reallocation identity, not every legal system. S03: Binary parcel choices extend consolidation beyond divisible area balances.
Priority inference: High | Fragmented agricultural and urban holdings require recurring reallocation.
Relevance inference: High | Consolidation can reduce access costs and improve usable land value.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; indivisible parcels, owner preferences and geometric conditions complicate standard assignment.
Useful descriptors: ##land-consolidation ##readjustment ##entitlement
Evidence limits and uncertainty: Entitlement and deduction rules vary by jurisdiction. A purely divisible area-balancing LP lacks the required discrete contract. Geometric land partition design can need further research if the optimizer chooses new boundaries rather than using candidate units.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Transfers of financial land-backed claims belong to FIN.
Source references: S02 S03.

RESERVE — Conservation Area Selection
Admission basis: The commitment is which sites receive conservation protection. Species targets, management budgets and opportunity costs distinguish it from a real-estate investment portfolio. Selecting connected sites is not automatically corridor network construction.
Decision scope: Selects conservation sites under habitat-representation targets, resource budgets and required spatial conditions.
Aliases: Reserve design; protected-area selection; conservation prioritization; marine reserve planning.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Planning units, habitat-feature amounts, costs, incumbents and optional adjacency are supplied. The chosen protected subset is the artifact. Auxiliary network flows in a connectivity formulation do not automatically add a #link construction.
Candidate Regime: #design | Commits the protected-site portfolio before management implementation.
Candidate Structure: #select | Chooses planning units from a supplied landscape.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Minimum-cost representation; budgeted coverage; boundary penalties; connected sites; multiple protection zones; staged acquisition. A temporal acquisition variant can add #place without changing the core protected-site identity.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Price-setting and auctions for rights are MKT. Adversarial exclusion zones are INTEL. Habitat conservation retains LAND when a public agency implements it. Forestry and fishing plans receive protection constraints rather than become conservation Problems.
IE and OR Template basis: Systematic conservation prioritization | Binary planning-unit optimization, prioritizr ILP and Marxan-style spatial search.
Template evidence: S04: Binary planning-unit prioritization, alternative objectives, spatial penalties and solver interfaces establish a conservation ILP Template. Marxan-style inputs are supported. S40: Budgeted connected-site acquisition across periods is a staged reserve-design variant. S41: Reserve selection trades habitat, area, compactness and foregone fishing revenue. It is conservation rather than vessel routing.
Priority inference: High | Reserve selection recurs across terrestrial and marine resource management.
Relevance inference: Critical | Site choices can determine enduring biodiversity protection and resource-use limits.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | Budgeted coverage variants are NP-hard; multiple features and spatial requirements require strong formulations or spatial search.
Useful descriptors: ##conservation ##coverage ##FORESTRY ##FISHERIES
Evidence limits and uncertainty: prioritizr ILP and Marxan-style search are defensible alternatives. Documentation distinguishes hard target constraints from soft penalties; solutions must be compared under matched target semantics. Feature uncertainty can matter more than small objective differences.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Connected and staged acquisition are variants; explicit corridor construction is separate.
Source references: S04 S40 S41.

CORRIDOR — Habitat Corridor Network Design
Admission basis: An explicit connection system is the promised output. A reserve with only a compactness penalty is not equivalent. Corridor geometry and redundant connection requirements can remain material even after habitat cores are fixed.
Decision scope: Connects habitat patches through feasible corridors under conservation budgets, habitat quality and corridor geometry requirements.
Aliases: Wildlife corridor design; ecological network design; least-cost corridor system.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Habitat cores, parcel geometry, quality, costs and candidate connections are supplied. The solver constructs the corridor system. A single-corridor implementation may select parcels on a supplied graph; the corridor-system foreground contract emphasizes the delivered interconnections.
Candidate Regime: #design | Defines ecological connections before land acquisition or restoration work.
Candidate Structure: #link | Constructs the corridor connections between habitat patches.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Single corridor; multiple habitat cores; width/length conditions; redundant corridors; joint corridor geometry and land selection. Land acquisition is a supporting commitment when sites are not already controlled.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Physical wildlife crossing construction is CIVIC when project execution dominates. Ecological connection design remains LAND. Freight and passenger connectivity belong to LOG; supplied GIS proximity is supporting data.
IE and OR Template basis: Landscape connectivity and wildlife corridor planning | Connectivity-constrained MILP and corridor-system decomposition.
Template evidence: S05: MILP designs corridors with habitat quality, resource and geometric requirements. A corridor is distinct from generic reserve coverage. S06: Two-stage corridor generation and least-cost corridor-system MILP support chosen connections among habitat patches.
Priority inference: Medium | Connectivity design is essential in fragmented habitat systems.
Relevance inference: High | Better connections improve feasible movement between protected habitats.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; corridor geometry, overlapping parcels and network requirements complicate large designs.
Useful descriptors: ##connectivity ##corridor ##network-design
Evidence limits and uncertainty: Connectivity is not proof of biological success. Habitat and movement models remain assumptions. The 2023 two-stage construction establishes a network Template, not dominance over every integrated corridor model.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Fixed-path parcel selection is a corridor variant; supplied landscape adjacency alone does not imply #link.
Source references: S05 S06.

ACCESS — Forest Harvest Access Network Design
Admission basis: Admit integrated renewable-resource access design whose feasible output and value depend on harvest availability. The forest industry tag does not move all its road work into LAND. A previously fixed network creates a downstream schedule substrate.
Decision scope: Connects harvest areas to exit points through logging roads, landings and access spurs under terrain, access and cost limits.
Aliases: Forest road network design; logging access design; road and landing planning.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Candidate road/spur links, terrain, landing options, harvest volumes and exit points are supplied. The delivered topology determines reachable timber. Accessibility flow variables do not create another independent construction.
Candidate Regime: #design | Defines the access topology that enables later timber harvesting.
Candidate Structure: #link | Constructs the road and spur connections that give access to harvest areas.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Fixed harvest set; joint stand activation; landing selection; road opening calendars; smallholder cooperation. Selecting optional landings can add #select in an integrated variant, while choosing road edges is already #link.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Road engineering and public-road asset management are CIVIC. Truck journeys are LOG. Integrated access and timber activation stay LAND when the resource decision defines feasibility and benefit.
IE and OR Template basis: Integrated forest harvest-access planning | Fixed-charge network MILP and joint road-harvest optimization.
Template evidence: S07: Joint logging-road and skidding-spur design establishes harvest-access topology. Pure truck routing is separate. S08: MILP couples road decisions, landing selection and timber access. Joint optimization is an alternative to fixed-access planning.
Priority inference: Medium | Access design is fundamental in road-constrained forest operations.
Relevance inference: High | Access topology changes recoverable timber and development cost.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; terrain, discrete road costs and harvest-value coupling require tailored models.
Useful descriptors: ##FORESTRY ##network-design ##landing
Evidence limits and uncertainty: Permanent topology and temporary opening are different commitments. Joint access/harvest optimization creates feedback rather than a universal one-way sequence. The foreground records only the practical access-to-harvest handoff.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Standalone road construction is CIVIC; timber-transport routing is LOG.
Source references: S07 S08.

PERENNIAL — Perennial Crop Establishment and Replacement Planning
Admission basis: Long-lived productive blocks incur establishment expenditure and delayed yield. This irreversibility distinguishes renewal from annual crop allocation. Replacement includes preserving existing blocks as an alternative.
Decision scope: Selects crop-block establishment or replacement and assigns varieties and planting options under land, investment and yield-development limits.
Aliases: Orchard reconversion; perennial planting plan; variety replacement; plantation renewal.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Block ages, varieties, feasible density options, candidate replacements, capital limits and yield-age curves are supplied. The delivered block configurations and renewal dates change physical productive potential.
Candidate Regime: #design + #plan | Defines perennial block configuration and commits its establishment or replacement calendar.
Candidate Structure: #map + #place + #select | Assigns varieties and planting options to selected blocks and schedules establishment or replacement.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Fruit orchards; vineyards and plantations when the same block contract applies; minimum reconversion units; staged funding; disease-driven replacement; variety change and density options.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Plantation securities are FIN. Crop inputs and product inventory are SCM. Crop-processing recipes are PROC. Biological block configuration remains LAND, with downstream field work supplied by the new block plan.
IE and OR Template basis: Orchard and perennial plantation renewal planning | Multiperiod MILP and investment-policy optimization.
Template evidence: S09: MILP reconversion decisions include varieties, planting densities and minimum land units. Supports perennial replacement rather than annual crop rotation.
Priority inference: Medium | Perennial crops require recurring establishment and reconversion decisions.
Relevance inference: High | Renewal choices determine productive capacity over many seasons.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: Medium | No general complexity claim; juvenile yield and block indivisibility couple multiple years, while standard MILP provides a defensible starting point.
Useful descriptors: ##perennial-crops ##orchard ##replacement
Evidence limits and uncertainty: The retrieved pome-fruit model establishes Template existence. It does not establish a current universal best deployment. Maturation and disease/yield forecasts require sensitivity checks, not a new Problem for every forecast model.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Annual crop rotation is separate; purely financial plantation holdings belong to FIN.
Source references: S09.

DEVELOP — Real-Estate Development Phasing
Admission basis: Master-plan phases have indivisible openings and use-release dates. The primary output is physical site availability, rather than financial claim selection or construction-worker task scheduling.
Decision scope: Schedules the development and release of property phases under site-use commitments, capacity, investment and market-absorption limits.
Aliases: Property development sequencing; building opening schedule; phased land development.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Planned buildings/subdivisions, durations, capacity, development costs and absorption forecasts are supplied. The artifact is a phase opening/release calendar. Construction start dates can be implied by fixed durations; this is not detailed construction scheduling.
Candidate Regime: #plan | Commits when physical property phases become available for use.
Candidate Structure: #place | Positions development phases and space releases in time.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Office parks; residential subdivisions; industrial parks; dependent enabling phases; optional phases; changed absorption forecasts. Phase capacities and use classes can be chosen in broader integrated variants.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: CIVIC owns activity-level construction resource and precedence networks. MKT owns pricing and offers. FIN owns debt structure, taxes and claims. LAND owns geospatial asset development timing.
IE and OR Template basis: Property master-plan phasing and absorption planning | Multiperiod mixed-integer development scheduling.
Template evidence: S10: Integer opening-year decisions interact with absorption and building capacity. Its financing simplification must not be generalized to complete financial optimization.
Priority inference: Medium | Large property developments require coordinated phase commitments.
Relevance inference: Critical | Phasing controls major capital exposure and usable-space availability.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: Medium | No general complexity claim; commercial MILP can handle standard phase calendars, while uncertain absorption requires scenario analysis.
Useful descriptors: ##REALESTATE ##development-phasing ##absorption
Evidence limits and uncertainty: The classical source simplifies financing. Investment limits are admissible instance inputs but its economic model is not a full financial optimizer. Scenario analysis is justified; no present market forecasts or price assumptions are imported.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Detailed construction-task schedules belong to CIVIC; financing structure belongs to FIN.
Source references: S10.

Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; tenant synergy and unit compatibility complicate otherwise simple matching.

WATER — Basin Drought Rationing Policy Planning
Admission basis: The stand-alone commitment is a drought rationing or hedging policy with genuinely discrete service levels or candidate actions. S43 directly supports resource policy under uncertain inflows. Future policy preparation is #plan; material revision is #adapt. Purely continuous water-right or volume allocation is context, not an admitted discrete Problem.
Decision scope: Selects rationing policies and user-group service levels under basin water availability, entitlement priorities and environmental flow requirements.
Aliases: Discrete water allocation; basin rationing; drought hedging policy; service-priority allocation.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Rights/priority inputs, user groups, inflow states, water stocks, environmental flows and feasible rationing levels are supplied. The output commits policy or service modes. Continuous volumes accompany these choices but are not the core discrete construction.
Candidate Regime: #plan + #adapt | Prepares rationing commitments and revises them after material changes in water availability.
Candidate Structure: #select + #map | Chooses rationing policies and assigns service levels to user groups.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Single or multiple reservoirs as resource stocks; discrete hedging modes; user-group levels; multistage policies; climate/inflow uncertainty; post-drought policy revision. Installed hydraulic utility dispatch retains ENER ownership.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: MKT owns water trading and auction mechanisms. EXTR owns well activation and withdrawal. ENER owns installed pump, valve, tank and hydraulic distribution operation. LAND owns basin resource access and rationing policy.
IE and OR Template basis: Basin resource rationing and drought hedging | Discrete hedging MILP, multistage stochastic MIQP and policy optimization.
Template evidence: S13: Mixed-integer basin allocation exists. A formulation label alone does not prove that any water-allocation contract is intrinsically discrete. S14: Mixed-integer reservoir drought planning supports discrete hedging. Resource-rationing commitments differ from installed utility switching. S15: Priority-preserving multiperiod network allocation is a strong continuous comparator. Legal priority alone does not create a combinatorial decision. S43: A multistage stochastic mixed-integer quadratic model chooses hedging rules with different user rationing levels at reservoir trigger volumes. Supports the discrete resource-policy contract; continuous rights balances remain excluded.
Priority inference: High | Drought rationing is material across agricultural, municipal and environmental basin uses.
Relevance inference: Critical | Allocation changes agricultural output, ecosystem flows and essential supply.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; discrete rationing choices interact with uncertain inflows and resource balances.
Useful descriptors: ##WATER ##water-rights ##basin ##drought
Evidence limits and uncertainty: The broad basin allocation literature includes mostly continuous models. Admission remains conditional on intrinsically discrete choices. Jurisdictional water-law rules are supplied parameters, not universal normative claims.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Continuous water-right balances alone are excluded. Utility pump switching is ENER; groundwater withdrawal is EXTR.
Source references: S13 S14 S15 S43.

WATERSHED — Watershed Intervention Selection and Allocation
Admission basis: The contract changes land management to meet catchment outcomes. Each selected intervention has a type and eligible site. This is distinct from protecting an existing reserve and from controlling installed urban drainage equipment.
Decision scope: Selects catchment interventions and assigns them to eligible land units under budgets, land compatibility and water-quality or runoff targets.
Aliases: Best-management-practice placement; catchment treatment targeting; riparian buffer allocation.
Supplied substrate and delivered artifact: Hydrological units, eligible actions, existing uses, costs and modeled runoff/pollutant responses are supplied. Output allocates interventions to units. Hydrological topology and quality balances are evaluation structures, not necessarily constructed links.
Candidate Regime: #design | Defines the catchment intervention portfolio before implementation.
Candidate Structure: #map + #select | Assigns selected intervention types to land units.
Variants and integrated alternatives: Buffers, nutrient management, erosion controls, wetlands and retention practices; target versus budget objectives; uncertainty; multiple intervention types. Action implementation generates restoration or field-task requirements.
Primary ownership: #LAND. Material interfaces: Urban sewer/stormwater asset configuration can be CIVIC; installed utility operation is ENER. Farm agronomic calendars remain LAND. Cross-domain ownership is decided per promised output, rather than by the acronym BMP.
IE and OR Template basis: Catchment best-management-practice targeting | Hydrological simulation-optimization, multiobjective search and surrogate MILP.
Template evidence: S16: SWAT-linked multiobjective genetic search selects catchment practices for nutrient and sediment reduction. Simulation cost and response estimates matter. S17: Official watershed decision support establishes cost-effective intervention selection and optimization. Urban service-asset implementations need an ownership check.
Priority inference: High | Catchment resource management repeatedly requires spatial intervention choices.
Relevance inference: High | Targeting can reduce pollutant loads and protect water-resource function.
Industrial Hardness and formal qualification: High | No general complexity claim; response simulation and interacting intervention effects make portfolio evaluation expensive.
Useful descriptors: ##WATER ##watershed ##nonpoint-source ##simulation-optimization
Evidence limits and uncertainty: Simulation response is uncertain and repeated evaluations can be expensive. Published multiobjective search and mixed-integer surrogate formulations are alternatives. No global solver ranking or precise pollutant reduction is inferred.
Admission condition / boundary conclusion: Urban drainage asset decisions belong to CIVIC or ENER when that service-asset contract dominates.
Source references: S16 S17.

CROP — Seasonal Crop-to-Plot Allocation
Admission basis: This contract decides current-season physical crop assignment. Historical soil and prior crop states can be fixed inputs. Chosen succession across seasons is the rotation contract. Integrated crop/rotation models need not be represented as duplicate industrial decisions in an instance.
Decision scope: Assigns crops or crop plans to land units for a season under suitability, acreage, demand and farm-resource limits.
Aliases: Crop selection; seasonal crop mix; whole-farm plot planning.

EXCLUSIONS, MERGES AND OPEN QUESTIONS
Urban, rural and marine spatial zoning share the unit-to-use contract; keep one Problem with context variants. Cadastral consolidation has owners and entitlements and remains distinct. Generic land-change simulation is not an optimization commitment.
Reserve selection, explicit corridor connections and ecological restoration differ by artifact: protected subset; connection system; action/recovery calendar. Connected-site acquisition is a reserve variant. Wetlands and riparian restoration are recovery/intervention variants, not separate Problems merely because the ecosystem changes.
Seasonal crop assignment is distinct from chosen multi-season succession. Integrated models can instantiate both, but an industrial instance should not be counted twice when it promises one integrated calendar. Greenhouse/vertical-farm occupancy needs a separate contract because biological space capacity changes during growth. Nursery stock allocation without biological capacity decisions is SCM or a continuous allocation context.
Plantation renewal is a perennial block replacement variant. Forestry thinning/regeneration are stand-prescription variants. Biomass harvesting under a forest/crop-resource calendar uses the corresponding harvest/field contract; standalone biomass supply networks are SCM and transport LOG.
Forest long-range stand prescriptions and near-term harvest-resource execution are distinct commitments. Green-up, maximum opening areas and even flow are constraints. A road/harvest joint model can couple ACCESS and FOREST; it does not create a new solver-method Problem. A routing-only harvester path belongs to LOG even when performed inside a field.
Crop harvest timing is retained in FIELD or ROTATION when it is part of the timed crop contract. Protected batch harvest and aquaculture cohort harvest remain biologically capacity-defined calendars. Fishery harvest allocation is not the same as quota auction/trading or vessel routing.
Pure water quantities and water-right priority balances are outside ICO without discrete policy/service commitments. Detailed groundwater abstraction is EXTR. Installed reservoir/pump/valve balance and distribution operation are ENER. Basin rights auctions are MKT. A proposed dam portfolio requires an asset/infrastructure ownership test and is not forced into LAND merely because it influences water resources.
Generic farm capital machinery selection is not automatically LAND. Hardware fabrication configuration may be PROD, financial investment FIN, and physical service capacity follows the relevant operational contract. Feed blending is PROC; feed inventory SCM. Animal mating-network design is a potential distinct biological resource contract; current replacement sources do not justify silently adding chosen mate pairs to HERD.
Mine extraction/backfill/waste emplacement retain EXTR. Geotechnical landform construction alone is CIVIC. Ecological post-use recovery is RESTORE only when recovery states and land function define feasibility. The mine-land LP/simulation source supplies industrial context, not a validated discrete scheduling benchmark.
Insurance, carbon-credit trading, land-backed claims and rent pricing remain FIN/MKT according to contract. Habitat or forest-use allocation under a supplied carbon target is a LAND variant, not a financial claim decision.
Public-service emergency coordination and active-fire resource dispatch are CIVIC/INTEL by contract. Pre-event fuel modification is LAND. PEST separately owns landscape biological treatment portfolios because endogenous spread and continuing action choice define a stand-alone contract. Fixed crop protection execution remains FIELD; ecological recovery after abatement remains RESTORE.

Known gaps: geospatial parcel-boundary generation rather than candidate-lot allocation; species-specific mate-pair selection; wildlife culling/translocation portfolios; detailed biological disease-control deployments; highly integrated basin/energy/market systems. None is given a fabricated Template or permanent ID. These are future discovery targets unless adequate distinctness, commercial materiality and ownership evidence is supplied. Source access gaps are recorded, not hidden. Background Control must decide whether a gap blocks the current canonical set.

FEEDBACK, CYCLES AND CONDITIONAL DIRECTIONS
ZONE <-> PARCEL: desired uses constrain holdings, while feasible readjustment can revise zoning. The commitment pass records ZONE -> PARCEL.
RESERVE <-> CORRIDOR: selected habitat cores define connection demand; infeasible/expensive corridors can revise the reserve portfolio. Joint models are valid; the initial protection-to-connection handoff is recorded forward.
ACCESS <-> FOREST: roads enable stands; chosen harvest value and dates can revise access investments. Integrated sources support the coupling, not a universal acyclic system. Initial access availability feeds stand planning.
PERENNIAL <-> FIELD: block renewal creates operations, while machine/weather capacity can revise renewal timing. Initial configuration feeds execution.
DEVELOP <-> TENANT: phase openings supply units; tenant demand and anchor commitments can revise development. Future commercial forecasts are inputs, not automatically backward dependencies to MKT IDs.
WATER <-> CROP/ROTATION/IRRIGATE: resource policy limits crop choices and delivery, while crop demand can revise policy. Initial resource access feeds the calendar. A coupled policy/crop solve can remove the one-way decomposition without changing Problem identity.
CROP <-> ROTATION: when separately scoped, an already committed season supplies the rotation's initial conditions. In integrated planning, the rotation instead determines annual crops. This is conditional rather than a universal precedence law.
FOREST <-> FUEL/RESTORE: harvest and recovery states limit compatible treatments; environmental treatments can revise harvest choices. The initial harvest/state commitment is shown forward.
HERD <-> GRAZE: herd composition supplies forage demand, while pasture capacity can revise herd size. Initial herd commitments feed the rotation schedule.
FORESTOP/FIELD/IRRIGATE observations can revise upstream plans, especially after disruption. Such execution feedback is not rewritten as an acyclic initial-commitment edge. No continuous feedback cycle is claimed absent merely because the foreground sequence is forward.

EVIDENCE LIMITS AND CASE HANDOFF
Source dates refer to publications or official records, not to reproduced experiments. Indexed primary abstracts were used where direct retrieval failed; they establish stated scope and model family but cannot support unseen equations, licenses, implementation details or result reproduction. Official repositories demonstrate method availability, not production adoption. Stronger formulation evidence and open implementations are retained as near-equals rather than ranking methods across incomparable datasets.
Case research must follow human approval of the controlled foreground. It should test physical-resource ownership, genuine discrete action, exact Template match, data access/license, baseline validity, objectives and constraints, scale and reproducibility. No final Case IDs, selected benchmark sets, performance reference values or uplift calculations appear here. A public nearby LP or logistics dataset is not a substitute for an unverified LAND benchmark.
