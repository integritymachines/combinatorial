TERRITORY AND ADMISSION CONTRACT

Primary EXTR means that prospecting, extraction, depletion, wells, resource blocks or nonrenewable-resource activation defines the decision contract. Optimization must construct a discrete solution; a continuous reservoir simulator, geological estimate or pumping-control problem alone is not admitted.

Industries investigated: ##MINING (metal, coal, underground industrial minerals), ##QUARRYING (limestone, aggregates and raw materials), ##OILGAS (onshore/offshore, conventional/unconventional), ##WATER (groundwater prospecting and extraction). These are open industry contexts, not additional Domains. Brine and solution-mining well systems can instantiate the wellfield contracts subject to validating their dissolution and processing interfaces. Fisheries, forestry and agricultural harvest planning belong to LAND. Water withdrawal is expressly within EXTR even where the resource is renewable.

Coverage spans exploration selection, information acquisition, extraction geometry, underground access, field configuration, extraction-unit scheduling, well construction, depletion, selective grade control, material allocation, waste emplacement, aquifer withdrawals, producing-well operation and corrective intervention. Open-pit, underground and well-based systems are not interchangeable: their different delivered artifacts and physical feasibility justify separate families.

OWNERSHIP AND INTERFACE RULES

EXTR / LOG: geological extraction decisions remain EXTR; vehicle journeys, truck dispatch and fleet/handling capacity under fixed extraction requirements belong to LOG. S52 chooses a truck/loader fleet for a supplied mine plan; S53 dispatches trucks. They support commercially material exclusions, not omission of haulage territory. Integrated mine shift decisions stay EXTR only when extraction-cycle feasibility defines their output. LOG supplies mobility and transport capacities.

EXTR / PROC: ore-unit destination and recovery decisions linked to mine availability are EXTR. Standalone blend recipes, crushers/mills/flotation process design, smelter campaigns and transformation settings belong to PROC. Pure continuous blend ratios are not forced into EXTR ICO. Surface oil separation alone does not make every petroleum problem PROC: an integrated well-activation contract remains EXTR.

EXTR / SCM: extraction and depletion create physical availability. Inventory position, replenishment, procurement and supplier allocation are SCM. A stockpile state in a mine schedule does not create a second SCM primary domain.

EXTR / LAND: land access, concession footprint as land use, basin rights, resource entitlements, recharge allocation and reclamation belong to LAND. EXTR consumes these limits. A mine-waste placement contract is EXTR only when extraction provenance, progressive dump availability and material compatibility define it; land restoration is not included. Conjunctive-use research can contain LAND-owned decisions and is not imported as a whole into EXTR.

EXTR / ENER: reservoir or aquifer extraction defines EXTR; installed utility pump, valve, tank and distribution operation defines ENER. Mine ventilation and dewatering can be constraints in an extraction schedule. An independently scoped utility-control or power-supply problem belongs to ENER.

EXTR / CIVIC: resource access layout is EXTR; generic construction calendars, civil structure design, roads and asset maintenance follow CIVIC when those decisions dominate. Platform dismantling does not become EXTR merely because it occurs at an oilfield.

EXTR / HR / COMP / INTEL: standalone crews and rosters are HR; sensing/data-resource operation is COMP; adversarial protection is INTEL. Geological sampling remains EXTR when its output is an extraction-information programme. A research paper's industry label does not determine Domain ownership.

EXTR / MKT / FIN: rights auctions, commodity offers and allocation mechanisms are MKT; financing, hedges and financial asset positions are FIN. A budget or discounted cash-flow objective does not displace an extraction decision. Physical exploration project selection is EXTR.

EVIDENCE INTERPRETATION

A source supports the existence of a decision or method family only to the extent stated in its record. Indexed abstracts do not support detailed implementation, runtime, licensing or industrial adoption claims. Vendor documentation supports the existence of industrial functionality, not independent optimality or superiority.

IE Templates identify defensible operational framings. OR Templates retain meaningful alternatives where model scope matters. No single universally strongest algorithm was established for this domain. Published stochastic, integrated and simulation-based methods are not silently labelled routine industrial practice. MineLib and FieldOpt are reproducibility leads for the later, separately authorized Case phase; no Cases or performance baselines are selected here.

Priority, Relevance, Hardness and ontology annotations are analyst judgments under 000_DOMAIN.txt, supported by the decision scope and cited mechanisms. They are not ratings quoted from papers. Formal complexity statements are limited to an identified classical problem or a containing standard hard special case. General mixed-integer or nonlinear formulations alone do not prove NP-hardness.

Structure records the chosen solution: block membership = select; destination or resource assignment = map; new access connections = link; trajectories and activity calendars = place. Supplied slope arcs, reservoir connectivity, task precedence, spatial adjacency and mathematical auxiliary variables do not justify link. Continuous volumes and rates accompany discrete choices without creating another Structure.

PROSPECT — Exploration Prospect Portfolio Selection

Decision evidence and identity. Contract: fund or defer physical exploration prospects, not rank geological pixels or trade asset shares. Inputs are candidate prospects, conditional discoveries, costs and access conditions; output is an exploration subset. Alias: exploration project selection. Sequential drilling with information updates is a variant; appraisal-hole geometry is a separate contract. S01 compares exploration portfolio approaches; S02 gives binary exploration-project decisions in a stochastic formulation, but is a 2026 preprint. Neither establishes a universally superior industrial method. The Template is a defensible model family, not a claim of commercial deployment. Budgeted independent rewards reduce to knapsack; this is the limited complexity inference. High centrality across exploration-intensive industries and enterprise-scale relevance are analyst assessments. Geological risk alone does not change ownership to FIN. Commercial licence allocation is excluded.

Candidate scope. Selects resource prospects for exploration under exploration budgets, geological dependence and access limits.
Template conclusion. Risked prospect evaluation and exploration portfolio planning | Binary stochastic or multiobjective portfolio optimization.

Rating assessment. Priority High | Prospect choice initiates major mineral and petroleum development programmes. Relevance Critical | Choices determine discovery exposure and the use of exploration capital. Hardness High | Budgeted subset variants are NP-hard; correlated geological outcomes and staged information complicate portfolio evaluation.
Boundary note. Physical exploration commitments define this scope; financial holdings and licence auctions belong to FIN and MKT.

PIT — Ultimate Pit Limit Selection

DEPENDENCY GRAPH: INDUSTRIAL HANDOFFS

An edge means that the upstream delivered artifact supplies an applicable downstream input, not that every installation must instantiate both Problems. These are analyst reconstructions from the cited integrated models and industrial contracts. They are not mathematical reductions. Mining, petroleum and groundwater chains are alternative deployment paths.

Prospect to sampling: the selected opportunity set constrains appraisal locations (S01-S04). Existing deposits can enter directly at sampling or design.
Sampling to method, pit, stope, field, wellfield and grade-control design: geological information updates candidate values and feasibility (S03-S05, S11-S12, S18-S21). These are information handoffs, not promises that uncertainty disappears.
Method to pit/stope: method restrictions determine permitted extraction units (S05-S06).
Pit to pushback to surface plan: membership bounds practical phases, which restrict extraction progression (S07-S10).
Stope to access and underground plan; access to underground/cave schedules: units and access feasibility constrain extraction timing (S11-S14, S24-S28). The ACCESS-to-CAVE edge applies only to cave-access infrastructure; a stope layout is not a cave-design prerequisite.
Field to well and depletion; well to depletion/drilling: field capacity and well configuration constrain activation and campaigns (S15-S19).
Wellfield design to drilling/withdrawal: installed candidate locations and capacities constrain implementation and extraction (S20-S21, S45).
Depletion to drilling and well operation; drilling to well operation/withdrawal: target activation dates drive campaign needs; completed wells supply operational availability (S15-S17, S29-S31, S45-S49).
Surface plan to grade/shift/destination/waste; underground and cave schedules to shift/destination: production targets and available extraction units define local execution and material choices (S22-S28, S32-S43).
Grade to shift/destination: selective-mining polygons determine executable cuts and material identity (S32-S34).
Shift to destination: realized available lots and extraction states inform updated destination allocation (S35-S40). A prescriptive destination plan can also guide the shift; that reverse direction is feedback.
Destination to waste emplacement: assigned waste units provide quantities and material classes (S41-S43).
Well operation to workover: observed loss and operating status generate eligible intervention demand (S46-S51).

CYCLES, COUPLINGS AND ORDERING LIMITS

Sampling informs CUTOFF; selected cutoff policies constrain surface/underground schedules and destinations. Schedule valuations feed back into CUTOFF (S55, S59). These policies need not be separately instantiated where direct block optimization determines ore/waste decisions.

Design is iterative: ACCESS -> STOPE and WELL -> FIELD are economic/feasibility feedback, while the first committed layout proceeds in the reverse direction. The Compiler may retain the forward handoff while explaining the feedback here.
GRADE/DEST -> SURFACEPLAN and DEST -> TASK feed grade/plant targets back into plans. DUMP -> SURFACEPLAN is capacity feedback. TASK -> UGPLAN revises near-term accessibility and execution assumptions. CAVE draw outcomes revise drawpoint schedules internally.
WORKOVER -> WELLOP returns restored capacity; WELLOP -> DEPLETION updates deliverability. DRILL -> DEPLETION returns completion delays. WATEROP -> WATERDESIGN can trigger additional capacity or revised wells. These adaptation paths do not reverse the initial dependency sequence.
The phase, stope, access and well design/schedule models may be solved jointly. Joint solution is a coupling architecture, not evidence for a catch-all integrated extraction Problem that duplicates its components.
No cross-domain permanent IDs are asserted: supplied interfaces are contract boundaries rather than unresolved references to future LAND work.

ALIAS, EXCLUSION AND OPEN-QUESTION LEDGER

Mine scheduling, block scheduling, extraction sequencing: resolve to the surface, underground, cave or shift contract according to the delivered artifact; a change of horizon alone is insufficient for a new Problem.
Quarry/coal/metal-specific schedules: variants unless a distinct output/feasibility contract is established. Quarry chemical limits refine SURFACEPLAN and DEST; quarry plant transformations remain PROC.
Deterministic/stochastic/robust mining: uncertainty variants and descriptors, not independent Problems.
Pit shells/Lerchs-Grossmann/minimum cut: solution approaches or inputs for PIT, not separate Problems.
Stope boundaries versus mine access: distinct selection and network artifacts; retained separately.
Cutoff grade policy: admit CUTOFF when the output is a reusable zone-level policy. Per-unit destinations remain DEST; implicit threshold effects in a block schedule remain SURFACEPLAN. Continuous break-even-grade calculation alone remains outside ICO.
Drill/blast pattern parameters and fragmentation simulation: pure continuous engineering settings excluded. Discrete extraction-task scheduling is TASK. A separate discrete blast-pattern selection Problem lacks adequately distinct Template evidence in this pass.
Surface/underground transition timing: METHOD plus production-planning coupling; no duplicate transition scheduler.
Grade estimation, resource classification statistics and reservoir history matching: analytical inputs; no chosen combinatorial construction demonstrated.
Truck/shovel fleet purchase, salvage, generic transport infrastructure and truck dispatch under fixed mining requirements: LOG (S52-S53). Excavation-cycle choices remain TASK.
Mine ventilation/dewatering: extraction feasibility refinements, or ENER when separately scoped utility operation; S54 supports integration without requiring another EXTR card.
Process blending, mineral processing, refinery planning: PROC. Destination allocation does not claim the process-recipe contract.
Mine closure/reclamation: LAND for resource/land restoration; CIVIC for independent civil works. Field retirement linked to remaining extraction is a DEPLETION variant supported by S29.
Mine waste versus dump construction: DUMP retains extraction-stream-specific cell progression; geotechnical earthworks design itself is not included.
Tailings process handling, impoundment civil design and basin reclamation: PROC, CIVIC and LAND according to their decision contracts. No generic tailings card is added here.
Geothermal wells: prospective EXTR configuration/activation interface only when subsurface extraction dominates; installed heat/power dispatch belongs to ENER. A dedicated industry-specific split is deferred.
Solution mining, salt/brine and in-situ leaching: potential WELL/WATERDESIGN/DEPLETION variants; dissolution chemistry may instead make the primary contract PROC. No false equivalence or dedicated Problem is asserted.
Marine seabed mining and dredged aggregates: likely extraction-sequence and LOG interfaces; separate coverage not established. Revisit if subsequent industry research supplies distinct commercially material decisions.
No Case identifiers, instance sizes, selected benchmark suite, numerical reference results or uplift claims are included.
