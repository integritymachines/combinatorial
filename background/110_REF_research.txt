TERRITORY

REF covers petroleum refining and process-compatible alternative-feed conversion within refining operations. It includes crude and feed preparation; atmospheric/vacuum distillation; conversion, upgrading and treating; intermediate storage; product blending; process heat, hydrogen and water integration; and process-coupled maintenance. Hydroskimming and conversion refineries differ in installed units, not in ontology. Gasoline, diesel, jet-fuel, fuel-oil, asphalt, LPG and base-oil operations are contexts for these contracts, not automatically separate Problems. A wholly distinct chemical synthesis or biorefinery industry identity is not created here.

Ownership follows material transformation, process capability, recipe validity or material-state feasibility. Plant design remains #PRODUCTION Territory when it configures the means of producing refinery output. A #design Regime does not imply #DEVELOPMENT. No new-fuel development or standalone scientific experiment contract is silently included. Supplied identity is consistent with the foundational research example of integrated refinery crude movement and blending under PROC. No identity split is established.

BOUNDARIES

REF/LOG: independent tanker routing, port service, trunk-pipeline batch scheduling and finished-product movement deliver journeys or transfers. Integrated receipt-to-CDU scheduling stays REF only when settling, mixing and feed-state feasibility define the contract. A loading window can be a supplied interface to BLEND without importing all delivery routing.
REF/SCM: procurement contracts, inventory targets, supply-position allocation and replenishment belong to SCM. Process-feasible feedstock selection is FEED; material availability and price are inputs. Feedstock classification follows the decision artifact, not the word procurement in a paper title.
REF/ENER: standalone boilers, turbines, electricity/steam commitment and utility network operation belong to ENER. Process-stream heat recovery stays REF. Hydrogen reuse is REF when reaction purity, pressure and process recovery define the material contract; energy-carrier supply is not automatically included.
REF/LAND/EXTR: basin allocation, water rights, crude extraction, wells and depletion are outside REF. Contaminant-based industrial water treatment and reuse remain REF.
REF/HR/CIVIC: standalone workforce rosters and construction programs belong to HR/CIVIC. A turnaround dominated by process isolation, connected-unit downtime and restart readiness remains REF even if workers are scarce.
REF/FIN/MKT: commodity trading, hedging, pricing, credit and commercial offers remain outside. Profit is an objective, not evidence of FIN ownership.
REF/COMP/INTEL: digital infrastructure, predictive analytics and cybersecurity are separate contracts. A prediction of a yield or fault is an input, not a discrete refinery solution artifact. Process safety constraints do not convert ordinary process design into adversarial INTEL.

DISCOVERY AND EVIDENCE METHOD

Read generic PROC and foundational research as seeds, then searched independently across configuration, tank design, equipment layout, heat/water/hydrogen networks, production planning, crude selection, crude handling, blend operations, mode transitions, catalyst life, cleaning, turnaround and reactive recovery. A second coverage sweep searched coke-drum cycling, process oil-movement paths, utility integration and contemporary planning/scheduling methods. Sources are primary publisher abstracts, author institutional records, official industrial pages, technical reports and an official model repository. Most paper access is abstract-level; full technical report pages were retrieved for S03 and S23. No numerical result or implementation has been reproduced.

Sources establish decision/model existence and plausible Templates. Analyst judgments under 000_DOMAIN.txt establish ownership, identity, ratings, Structure and the modular dependency graph. Those judgments are not claimed as classifications supplied by the papers. There is no universal SOTA ranking: commercial systems, published algorithms and reproducible public models differ. MILP/MINLP is a supported architecture, not evidence of superiority by itself. Public repository existence does not establish a refinery benchmark fit.

The second sweep mostly returned integrated versions, formulation improvements and material-specific variants of the retained contracts. It also found coker modeling without sufficient standalone combinatorial scheduling evidence. This supports practical coverage for the present problem layer, not mathematical exhaustiveness. Known gaps remain explicit below. No Problem is admitted simply to fill a Regime band.

CONFIG — Refinery Process Configuration Design
Decision: Selects refining technologies, unit capacities and material connections to deliver a target product slate under feedstock, investment and process limits.
Aliases and variants: Grassroots configuration; conversion-route selection; capacity expansion; process retrofit.
Boundary and identity: Capacity selection is retained inside configuration when it delivers the same installed process capability. This is production-system design, not research into a new fuel molecule. Standalone construction execution is CIVIC.
Construction analysis: Supplied substrate: candidate technologies, feed assays, allowed connections, capacity choices and investment data. Chosen artifact: an installed processing topology. Capital and yield equations constrain that artifact; they do not create additional Structure tags. Grassroots and retrofit retain one identity. Residue upgrading, naphtha conversion and renewable-feed co-processing are configuration variants when the refinery product contract is unchanged. Continuous sizing with a fixed topology and no discrete equipment choices is outside the ICO subset.
Candidate Regime: #design | Commits installed transformation capability before production planning.
Candidate Structure: #link + #select | Constructs material-processing connections and selects participating technologies and capacity options.
Rating judgments: Priority Critical | Process configuration defines the refinery's feasible transformations. Relevance Critical | Configuration commits capital and long-lived conversion capability. Industrial Hardness High | Technology alternatives, recycles and nonlinear yields require careful model reduction.
Template basis: Refinery flowsheet synthesis and retrofit screening | Superstructure MILP or MINLP with technology-selection logic.
Evidence: S01 S02. See source register for access and support limits.
Descriptors: ##process-synthesis ##retrofit
Input dependencies: none specified. Output dependencies: TANKDES, LAYOUT, HEAT, H2DES, WATER, PLAN.

Aliases and variants: Tankage sizing; storage expansion; tank-service configuration.

Unlike TANK, these decisions alter installed capability rather than assign current lots. The published multiproduct MINLP provides a transferable formal Template, not a verified refinery benchmark. The industrial refinery report provides application evidence for tankage alternatives and simulation. Industrial Hardness High | Capacity choices must remain feasible across variable receipts and production schedules.

DEPENDENCY GRAPH AND INTERPRETATION

The edges describe modular industrial decisions, not reductions between mathematical problems. CONFIG supplies installed capability. Tankage precedes physical layout and operational assignment. Heat and hydrogen designs constrain cleaning and hydrogen operating choices. Water-system capability can constrain production plans. PLAN sets target envelopes for feed selection, turnarounds and detailed campaigns. FEED supplies selected materials to tank allocation and crude scheduling. TURN supplies available outage windows to condition-based interventions and production calendars. CLEAN and CATALYST return availability to MODE. MODE supplies unit-feed requirements, intermediate availability and hydrogen demands. TANK supplies resource commitments to CRUDE and BLEND. These directions are analytical inference from the delivered contracts and the integration evidence S15, S17, S18, S25, S30-S34 and S40.

Detailed scheduling feasibility can feed back to production targets. Catalyst or fouling forecasts can force changes to the turnaround envelope; crude feasibility can force mode changes; hydrogen limits can revise unit schedules. These are feedback in iterative planning, not evidence that upstream plans are meaningless. The canonical graph records the usual modular handoff; it does not require serial optimization or claim the graph is acyclic in practice. Explicit REPAIR cycles retain the delivery of an old schedule to repair and its revised calendar back to the affected scheduling modules. Direct TANK-to-CRUDE/BLEND edges cross backward from #allocate to #plan; they remain because commitment type takes priority over topological numbering. Optional integration absorbs those handoffs inside one solver.

No edge asserts that every refinery has every unit or must solve every Problem. For example, a refinery without fixed-bed catalyst changeouts has no CATALYST instance. Simultaneous solution of a full refinery can include PLAN, FEED, MODE, CRUDE, TANK and BLEND without creating a seventh integrated Problem. Industry-specific specialization is distinct from duplication within this inventory.

DISPOSITIONS, ALIASES AND GAPS

1. Equipment selection, discrete capacity expansion, residue route and grassroots/retrofit technology choice: retained as CONFIG variants, not repeated capacity cards. Their common output is installed transformation capability.
2. Tank sizing and tank service design: TANKDES. Physical equipment coordinates: LAYOUT. Named-lot occupancy: TANK. A service assignment at design time differs from one day's tank use.
3. Crude assay evaluation and yield prediction: analytical inputs. FEED admits actual discrete material selection; an assay model alone is not ICO.
4. Continuous refinery LP, pooling NLP, blending ratios, distillation cut points and severity optimization: exclude when no industrial discrete decision remains. Mathematical nonconvexity alone does not satisfy ICO.
5. Product recipe determination: inside BLEND for scheduled production. No separate recipe card is admitted without evidence of a distinct, commercially recurring discrete-only contract.
6. Gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, asphalt, LPG, fuel oil, lubricants and renewable co-processing: retained as product/feed variants subject to appropriate property models. Product names do not establish separate Problem identities.
7. Refinery-wide planning/scheduling and multi-site integration: compositions or scale variants of retained contracts. Generic intersite sourcing and inventory remain SCM.
8. Tank-to-CDU grouping: TANK assignment; its timed switching: CRUDE. Standalone valve-lineup or transfer-path allocation: insufficient independently checked recurring refinery Template in this run. Do not infer link from supplied piping or from auxiliary flow variables.
9. Coke-drum sequencing: discrete operational candidate, but the located primary industrial modeling source S43 does not establish a separately defensible scheduling Template. Retain as a gap; relevant cyclic-unit calendars can fall under MODE when discrete choices are present. Do not call simulation/control alone ICO.
10. Catalyst regeneration/replacement: CATALYST only for discrete interventions. Continuous circulation or online severity control is excluded by itself.
11. Planned shutdowns: TURN; state-dependent exchanger cleaning: CLEAN; state-dependent catalytic activity: CATALYST. Shared crew usage is coupling, not a reason to collapse all three physical contracts.
12. Startup/shutdown procedure synthesis and refinery restart sequencing: operationally credible; dedicated refinery evidence not sufficient here to retain separately. Unit-state transitions remain MODE; emergency procedures require separate safety-validity research.
13. Laboratory sample sequencing, tank certification and off-spec rework: potential future discovery. Quality release is currently a constraint on TANK/BLEND, and reblending is a BLEND variant. No unsupported separate Template is supplied.
14. Live process dispatch: no separate #dispatch card. Rolling scheduling remains #plan unless it releases a next action; repairing an invalid commitment is REPAIR. No #operate + #sequence card is created from the mere presence of sequencing variables.
15. Independent marine, pipeline and finished-product movement: transfer to LOG. Material quality may be an interface; ownership needs the whole contract.
16. Crude procurement, supply positions and replenishment: transfer to SCM where transformation feasibility does not define the delivered artifact.
17. Standalone steam/power generation and balancing: transfer to ENER. Fuel-gas system commitment belongs there when utility output defines the contract.
18. Workforce rosters: HR; non-process construction: CIVIC; crude extraction: EXTR; basin allocation: LAND; trading and hedges: FIN/MKT; compute deployment and security: COMP/INTEL. These are domain exclusions, not invented refinery Problems.
19. Desulfurization, sulfur recovery and effluent process capability: CONFIG/WATER or MODE as appropriate. A standalone sulfur-unit continuous setpoint problem is not admitted solely because sulfur limits are economically important.
20. Facility decarbonization: technology additions are CONFIG, process heat recovery is HEAT, water integration is WATER. Emissions caps are constraints or objectives. Carbon allowance purchase is a commercial/financial interface, not a new refining Problem.

EVIDENCE LIMITS AND LATER CASE WORK

This is Problem-level research. Source examples are evidence leads, not selected Cases. Later work must check actual instance data, licenses, model equations, parameter provenance and baseline reproducibility. Configuration costs and assays may be proprietary. Property models must be valid for the product and feed ranges. Reported computational success does not imply a universal industrial solve-time bound. Local nonconvex solutions do not establish global optimality. Formal complexity claims are deliberately omitted from foreground because the present sources do not establish a precise informative classification for each complete industrial contract.
