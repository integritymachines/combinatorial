INTRODUCTION AND NORMATIVE INPUT

ENER covers decisions that configure or operate physical utility supply. Electricity, gas, heat, cooling and installed water systems share a service obligation: deliver a feasible utility state through assets whose availability, connection or mode can be chosen. The domain includes design, future operating commitments, resource assignments, live control and physical recovery. Industry membership alone does not establish ownership.

TERRITORY, INDUSTRIES AND OPERATING SYSTEMS

Bulk electricity: vertically integrated utilities, ISO/RTO physical operations, generation/storage investment, transmission expansion, outage calendars, thermal commitment, hydro, nuclear reload, topology control, voltage/security actions, shedding, islanding and restart.
Distribution and renewable plants: network reinforcement, discrete DER connections, wind layout/collection systems, phase assignments, installed switch/tap/shunt controls, flexible loads, charging service and feeder restoration.
Gas and hydrogen utilities: pipelines and compression, expansion/conversion, stationary nomination feasibility, linepack and transient control, valve and station modes. Fuel extraction, procurement and truck movement remain separate contracts.
District heat/cooling and industrial utilities: utility supply technology configuration, thermal distribution, CHP/boiler/chiller scheduling, electrical and thermal storage, local resilience and multi-carrier balance. The same equipment can support a PROC contract; ownership follows the delivered utility or transformation decision.
Water: installed distribution pipes, pumps, tanks, isolation/pressure/quality devices, pumping calendars and failure response. Utility-owned drainage/sewer pumping is a scoped operational variant where installed hydraulic service dominates. Catchment/runoff allocation, rights and withdrawal are LAND/EXTR; treatment chemistry is PROC; public works are CIVIC.
Useful industry context includes ##POWER, ##WIND, ##NUCLEAR, ##OILGAS, ##WATER, ##DISTRICTENERGY, ##DATACENTER, ##CHEMICALS, ##REFINING, ##STEEL and ##REALESTATE. This is an open contextual registry, not a new closed axis.
Renewables, batteries, microgrids, virtual power plants, energy hubs and hydrogen are technologies or operating contexts. They do not automatically become separate Problems. An integrated Problem is retained only when the delivered industrial contract adds distinct obligations beyond naming a combination.

OWNERSHIP, IDENTITY AND DISCRETE ADMISSION

ENER versus PROC: standalone utility supply/production, physical balance and utility distribution are ENER. Process reaction, recipe, material quality, treatment/reuse and hot-to-cold process-stream synthesis are PROC. The current PROC04 explicitly assigns standalone utility-generation design to ENER. Utility quantities supplied to a process schedule do not transfer ownership.
ENER versus PROD/COMP: utility service schedules with supplied production/computation obligations are ENER. Product-operation sequencing, machine assignment, server placement and compute work scheduling remain PROD/COMP even when power is a severe constraint.
ENER versus LOG/SCM: charging-station energy service on supplied arrivals is ENER; LOG11 owns charging/refueling integrated with transport duties. Pipeline utility pressure and delivery are ENER; vehicle journeys are LOG. Physical fuel balance may constrain ENER, while purchasing and supply-position commitments remain SCM.
ENER versus MKT/FIN: physical commitment, dispatch, balance and grid-feasible physical clearing belong to ENER when those obligations define the contract. Mechanism design, bids/offers and rights belong to MKT; hedges, settlements and financial exposure belong to FIN. A supplied tariff or bid is an objective input, not evidence for reclassification.
ENER versus CIVIC/LAND/EXTR/HR/INTEL: installed utility operation is ENER; construction/public asset work is CIVIC; basin/land/right allocation is LAND; extraction/withdrawal/depletion is EXTR; standalone crews are HR; adversarial defense/interdiction is INTEL. Land and water restrictions may be supplied utility feasibility conditions.
Admit genuine project/equipment choice, discrete capacity, loading/phase/port assignment, mode transition, selected active connection, interruption block, partition or physical recovery action. Exclude forecasting, simulation, estimation, unconstrained arithmetic and pure continuous dispatch/control as standalone ICO Problems. Nonconvexity alone does not imply combinatorial construction. Binary solver encodings alone do not prove SELECT.
Distinguish reusable policy configuration from event action; investment from use of installed assets; normal service from fault recovery; power restart from ordinary generator commitment; system-level projects from within-plant layout. Preserve technology variants rather than proliferating source/formulation-specific cards.

STRUCTURE, RATINGS AND TEMPLATE EVIDENCE

Each record separates supplied substrate, chosen construction, constitutive laws and delivered artifact. Given networks, reservoir cascades, core slot geometry, customer profiles and weather scenarios do not by themselves establish LINK or PLACE. Actual active network connections are LINK; role/configuration assignment is MAP; actual time or spatial arrangement is PLACE; membership/actions/projects are SELECT.
Regime follows the committed artifact rather than horizon: assets and protective rules are design; operation/outage calendars are plan; service or connection assignments are allocate; ongoing utility coordination is operate; immediate activation is dispatch; interrupted-service recovery and material commitment revision are adapt.
Priority/Relevance/Hardness below are explicit ontology judgments from the decision scope and stated industrial burdens, not source-reported rankings or measured uplifts. Formal complexity is supplied only for supported restrictions: general UC and DC-OTS. No empty full-contract complexity disclaimer is repeated in foreground.
Templates identify established engineering framing and a defensible optimization family. They are not assertions of a uniquely strongest 2026 solver. Retain material alternatives: linearized screen versus nonlinear validation; exact model versus relaxation; physics-evaluated search versus learned assistance; deterministic versus scenario planning; hierarchy versus joint co-optimization. Public examples do not establish commercial deployment share, detailed license terms or reproducibility.

Constitutive laws and feasibility: Each restoration component needs suitable energized support; candidate selection is constrained by reachable restoration plans.
Delivered artifact: Selected physically available black-start resources.
Template basis: Black-start readiness and capability placement | Restoration-coupled allocation MILP; flow and cut formulations.
Evidence and competing approaches: S15 establishes optimal black-start allocation with grid restoration considerations. SELECT records the actual capability portfolio; auxiliary island variables do not imply that the delivered design is a partition.
Source support: S15.
Ontology inference: Primary ENER; Regime #design; Structure #select. Commits restoration capability before a blackout.
Rating inference: Priority Medium — Black-start readiness is central to blackout recovery planning. Relevance Critical — Better capability can reduce the duration of widespread outages. Hardness High — Selected resources must support feasible energization across several outage conditions.
Useful comments/descriptors: Service procurement terms belong to MKT; restoration execution is downstream. ##black-start ##readiness ##resilience.

ALIASES, INTEGRATIONS, EXCLUSIONS AND OPEN LIMITS

UC/SCUC/fast-start and energy-reserve co-optimization: retain commitment. Separate reserve allocation requires genuine discrete provider/action assignments; ordinary divisible headroom allocation is continuous. No separate reserve card is invented from a constraint inside UC.
SCED/ACOPF/continuous congestion redispatch: exclude standalone continuous restrictions; retain genuine discrete-action security coordination as remedial. Nonconvex AC physics alone does not change this admission.
VPP/DER/microgrid orchestration: technology context, not a separate industrial identity. Portfolio configuration maps to local_design; unit availability to commitment; battery modes to storage; load actions to flex; installed network controls to feeder/voltage/remedial. S51 shows that physical DER disaggregation can be convex. A distinct mixed-integer VPP contract would require specific service obligations not already represented.
Power-market clearing, reserve auctions, congestion rights and price bidding: MKT when market mechanism/rights dominate; physical grid-feasible commitment remains commitment/remedial under supplied commercial inputs. Settlement/hedging is FIN. No financial profitability baseline is imported into physical Templates.
Long-term hydro water values and reservoir releases: upstream continuous/stochastic inputs unless there are real discrete unit/action choices. LAND owns basin and rights allocation. Pumped storage with reversible modes is hydro; electrical batteries are storage.
Renewable forecasts, continuous wind yaw/pitch and continuous inverter power allocation: exclude. Discrete wind site/collection design is wind; block curtailment may be a remedial/flex action. Solar panel/string topology is a local electrical-plant design specialization; no distinct commercially general parent is established by a technology name alone.
Capacity screens with divisible MW, scalar storage arbitrage with exact continuous relaxation, continuous dynamic operating envelopes, continuous gas blending and water pressure/chemical setpoints: not standalone ICO. Their physics can support an admitted discrete asset/mode/action decision.
Reactive-device placement, valve/booster placement, utility resilience hardening and asset renewal: retain as design options when they alter utility network service capability. Generic risk-ranked capital or maintenance portfolios remain CIVIC where utility balance is merely an evaluator.
Gas/hydrogen conversion: retain gas_design as a phased expansion/repurposing variant. Carrier conversion does not create a new Primary Domain. The flow abstraction in S07 does not establish pressure-feasible transient transition.
Nuclear refueling outage timing belongs to outage. Core loading belongs to nuclear. Fuel purchase inventory belongs to SCM; fabrication is PROD/PROC. Spent-fuel canister packing is a narrow specialized downstream contract not established here as a commercially general utility-operation parent.
Thermal-storage scheduling is integrated with multi_operation; a purely thermal fluid-state controller is continuous. Standalone thermal storage with genuine discrete modes may be a scoped storage-type variant, but electrical and hydro-specific feasibility must not be silently copied.
Emergency protection settings belong to shedding_policy; actual block disconnection to emergency; planned fair interruptions to rotation. Generic relay setting optimization is not admitted without discrete policy/action choices and supported coordination evidence.
Distribution fault restoration and bulk restart have different supplied service states and delivered obligations. Bottom-up feeder black start remains distribution_restore when feeder service defines the contract; system-wide source startup and synchronizing islands belong to bulk_restore.
Pipe/asset physical repair sequencing, construction staging and workforce travel remain CIVIC/HR/LOG interfaces. water_restore selects hydraulic isolation/recovery; it does not manufacture a crew-scheduling card.
Rolling schedule recomputation, robust/stochastic formulations, reinforcement learning, quantum optimization and decomposition are methods or qualifiers. They do not justify new Problem identities. A separate frozen-commitment utility schedule-repair parent is not established by simply rerunning UC/SHOP.
Water treatment/reuse chemistry and hydrogen/ammonia process synthesis stay PROC when transformation feasibility dominates. Installed utility desalination pumping can couple water_operation and multi_operation; reaction/treatment control is not silently subsumed.

DEPENDENCY GRAPH AND EVIDENCE CONVENTION

An edge A -> B records a meaningful industrial output handoff when both contracts occur in one deployment. It does not assert that every B instance must solve A first; an existing asset/issued plan can be supplied externally. Edges are neither mathematical reductions nor citations of authors' taxonomy. The source evidence supports physical coupling; the partition into project keys and ordering implications are explicitly our industrial inferences.
Design -> operation: generation supplies installed power capability; transmission/distribution supply connection/control limits; gas/thermal/water designs supply hydraulic assets; local_design supplies installed local fleets. Their downstream calendars and controls consume these outputs.
generation -> wind: selected wind projects supply capacity/site requirements for within-plant design. generation -> transmission -> distribution: staged hierarchy uses supply footprint and upstream transfer constraints. Joint planning is a co-optimization alternative, not a universal sequential requirement (S01, S04, S05).
generation/transmission/local_design -> black_capacity -> shedding_policy/restoration: installed candidates constrain black-start readiness; restored-source availability constrains supported islands and protective scenarios (S15, S43).
outage -> nuclear/commitment/hydro/multi_operation/storage/water_operation: outage outputs constrain when installed utility assets are available (S18, S19). Refueling outage/cycle windows constrain the loading-plan commitment; the nuclear physics pattern supplies allowable next-cycle operating conditions (S49, S50).
commitment -> hydro/multi_operation/storage/flex/trans_switch/feeder/rotation: issued power availability and residual supply requirements constrain coordinated subplans where a hierarchy is used. Joint optimization of these schedules is explicitly retained (S20, S22, S24, S26, S27).
multi_operation -> gas_operation: conversion/CHP schedules supply gas nominations and timing needs. Supplied upstream nominations can replace this edge (S25, S30, S31).
commitment -> water_operation: issued electrical supply/availability can constrain electricity-dependent pumping. The source supports energy-aware scheduling; the named handoff is a deployment inference (S32, S33).
distribution/local_design/storage -> charging: connection capability and issued local/storage support constrain station power availability; vehicle requests arrive as external inputs (S29).
phase -> feeder/voltage: phase assignments change the unbalanced network data evaluated by controls (S17, S37).
trans_switch/feeder -> voltage/remedial: issued topology supplies the network state on which voltage/security action selection operates (S34, S36, S37, S39).
commitment/hydro/storage/flex/trans_switch/feeder/voltage -> remedial: current schedules and controls are the commitments/actions available for security correction (S39, S40).
commitment/storage/flex/outage/distribution -> rotation: issued supply and connection/service capacity define sustained residual shortage (S41).
shedding_policy/commitment/remedial -> emergency: configured protection and current feasible action availability constrain actual block disconnection (S16, S42). rotation is not a mandatory input to emergency; both may occur independently.
remedial/emergency -> islanding: previous measures and resulting state constrain intentional separation when containment is still needed (S43). Black-start/protection capability supplies island support and safeguards. Not every intentional islanding event follows shedding.
feeder/voltage/emergency/islanding -> distribution_restore: resulting or previously issued network states are inputs to recovery where such events preceded the fault/restoration state (S36, S44). Restoration may instead start directly from observed fault data.
black_capacity/commitment/hydro/storage/islanding/distribution_restore -> bulk_restore: available startup sources, survivor islands and distribution pickup feasibility constrain whole-system restart (S45, S46). Last operating commitments are historical state, not an obligation to preserve failed power balance.
water_design/water_operation -> water_restore: installed valves/bypasses and current tank/pump state constrain hydraulic recovery (S47, S48).
All candidate-specific upstream-key lists below define the compile graph. Reciprocal recourse is possible between outage and unit schedules, generation and transmission expansion, topology and dispatch, and restored states and subsequent operation. Those are iteration feedback interfaces, not extra unconditional edges in this release. The graph records a directed deployment handoff abstraction; it is acyclic. Alternative integrated models do not force backward dependencies by sharing variables.
Cross-domain inputs/outputs remain narrative interfaces, not unsupported references to unseen ENER/CIVIC/LAND cards. Existing PROC04/PROC05/LOG11 IDs are used only to identify already visible boundary contracts.

SEARCH COVERAGE AND SATURATION

Pass 1: normative ENER territory — bulk power SCUC/dispatch, renewables, hydro, outage, storage, gas, district heat/cooling, microgrids and installed water pumping. Retrieved FERC, laboratory planning models, SINTEF and EPA.
Pass 2: construction and identity — expansion and equipment choices; distribution reconfiguration versus restoration; gas configurations/combined controls; discrete taps/shunts; water pump and pipe decisions. Retrieved author papers, GasLib and model documentation.
Pass 3: independent omission search — internal wind plant/collection layout; phase swapping; UFLS policy; rotating outages; black-start readiness; nuclear core loading; water isolation/bypass response; EV charger sessions. These searches added material identities beyond the original territory seeds.
Pass 4: integrated/boundary stress — DER/VPP disaggregation, multi-utility generation, hydrogen conversion, demand response versus production/computation, station charging versus LOG11, utility controls versus basin/treatment/civic work. New results largely mapped to existing keys, continuous subcases, technology specializations or other-domain decisions.
Coverage is near-exhaustive at the retained industrial-contract granularity within the principal territories, not a proof over all possible utility systems. Known limits: no exhaustive worldwide vendor deployment census; specialist nuclear/water/gas cases may require stronger instance-specific template verification; detailed sewer optimization evidence is weaker than potable-water pumping; several source pages are abstract-only. These do not justify invented parent Problems or claims of uniquely strongest practice. Case research must resolve actual data access, baseline, fidelity, reproducibility and current strongest instance-specific methods after approval.
