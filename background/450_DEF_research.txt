IDENTITY AND TERRITORY

450 and DEF are supplied identity inputs. Industry ##DEFENSE is present in the research registry. #INTEL and #INFO follow the normative protective/adversarial ownership test. #PRODUCTION follows the delivered operational defense service, not whether a decision is called design or planning. These omitted identity axes are explicit scope inferences, not a new territory split. The operational service scope permits design of an installed force or security configuration. It excludes the design of a new weapon/platform product and new-capability development portfolios. No Industry Domain is renamed, recoded, split or merged in this run.
This is a defense-specific Industry Domain within INTEL, not a replacement for the generic INTEL catalog. Civilian security, generic privacy release, conservation enforcement and commercial cybersecurity contexts remain in the generic Domain or other Industry contexts. Shared problem families may recur across Industries without duplicating Primary Domain ownership.

EVIDENCE AND INFERENCE

Primary publications and official institution descriptions establish decision existence and method families. Ownership, Regime, Structure, ratings, dependencies, admission and variants are project synthesis under 000_DOMAIN.txt, not source-authored ontology claims. Defense applicability of general cyber and security methods is stated as an inference when no military deployment is documented. Historical deployment, research evaluation and current commercial practice are distinguished. No classified access, live defense instance, performance reproduction or universal SOTA ranking is claimed.
Source-register inspection records distinguish fresh retrieval from inherited supporting references and failed direct opens. Freshly inspected sources carry the core existence and Template claims; inherited references preserve near-equals and context without being promoted to current deployment evidence. The 2025 exact WTA method supersedes the older exact-only seed as a principal static comparator; learning alternatives are retained without a blanket dominance claim.
Priority measures centrality within operational defense services. Relevance measures improvement value, not institutional importance alone. Industrial Hardness is qualitative engineering judgment from coupling, uncertainty, scale, latency and demonstrated method architecture. Formal complexity is not necessary to the cards and is not inferred from model appearance. Prototype performance does not justify universal operational hardness. Restricted inspection and assignment models remain easier than rich coupled contracts.

Templates are sufficient at Problem level; benchmark accessibility and fit remain Case-layer work after human inspection.

STRUCTURAL AUDIT

Supplied networks, adversary models, precedence, observations and effect estimates are substrates. The chosen artifact determines Structure: selection of interventions is select; assignments and groups are map; chosen route or permission relations are link; independent temporal or spatial positioning is place. Feasibility conditions, scenario indices, linearization, dual variables, search trees and attack-graph representations do not automatically add Structure.
Discrete policies and schedules enter ICO when their realizations choose finite actions, visits, configurations, assignments or transitions. Pure continuous effort, signal design, prediction and simulation without a combinatorial commitment are excluded. A selected probabilistic mixture does not erase the executable discrete construction. Secondary Structures are used only when the delivered contract requires them.

Aliases and retrieval terms: force mix; modular force design; capability-based force configuration.
Supplied substrate: Available component types or candidate configurations, scenario requirements, aggregation rules, budgets and force-size limits.
Chosen construction and delivered artifact: Groups components into formations and selects the component configurations included. The returned artifact is the committed decision above, not the auxiliary optimization representation.
Ownership and neighboring contracts: The delivered contract is an operational service configuration. New equipment architecture, research portfolios and procurement transactions are separate contracts.
Variants and identity control: Army formations, naval fleet mix and air-force composition are service-specific variants when they deliver the same operational force configuration.
Evidence and Template basis [S01]: S01 optimizes the assignment of modular building blocks to larger formations under uncertain adversary capability. Its military application supports operational force design; it does not establish deployed production software. Expert configuration and simulation remain useful comparators.
IE/OR reference architecture: Capability-based force design | Robust integer allocation of modular force components; simulation-supported configuration evaluation.
Candidate Regime: #design | Commits the composition of the operational force before readiness and mission assignments.
Candidate Structure: #map + #select | Groups components into formations and selects the component configurations included.
Rating synthesis: Priority High because force composition constrains readiness and major defense service commitments. Relevance Critical because better configurations can change long-lived capability sufficiency and resource commitments. Industrial Hardness High because scenario requirements and interacting component capabilities require tailored configuration models.
Dependency candidates: upstream none specified; downstream readiness, units. Edges are conditional industrial information or commitment flow, not mathematical reduction.
Useful refinements: ##force-design ##capability ##modularity ##robust-optimization.

DEPENDENCY EVIDENCE AND QUALIFICATIONS

The directed edges below are project synthesis. None is claimed as a universal source-reported operating sequence. Sources establish the consuming contracts; the supplied committed artifacts explain each edge.
force → readiness, units: configured operational formations supply eligible units and capability classes [S01, S14, S15].
layout → collection, patrol, weapons, electronic, alarm: installed protective resources supply availability or eligibility to tasking, coverage and response [S02, S03, S17, S33, S41]. A purely fixed sensor footprint does not constrain all effect resources; edges apply only to the integrated protective configuration.
protection → patrol: selected protection changes the residual exposure and protective coverage requirement [S04, S20].
controls → roles, segments, deception, remediation, mtd, response: selected safeguard requirements constrain detailed installed policies, recurring protection and allowed response alternatives [S06–S13, S29, S38, S42]. These are conditional configuration interfaces, not a requirement that every system implements all controls.
roles → segments, response: authorized access requirements constrain zone policies and service-preserving response [S08, S10, S42].
segments → deception, mtd, response: permitted access and zone membership constrain where deception or platform changes operate and which response actions preserve access [S10–S13, S38, S42].
deception → alerts: installed defensive observation can produce alerts for triage [S13, S39]; only deployments that emit investigation evidence use this edge.
readiness → collection, patrol, units, mission: readiness commitments supply resource availability [S14–S17, S20, S26, S28].
collection → search, interdiction, mission, weapons, electronic: collection supplies permitted information updates to the consuming decisions [S17–S19, S24–S27, S30, S33]. Collection is not the exclusive possible input source.
patrol → alarm: the issued patrol program supplies responder positions and availability to alarm-conditioned response [S20, S41].
search → mission: search results can update mission task eligibility [S24, S26].
interdiction → mission: an admitted denial plan can supply tasks to an integrated mission program [S25, S26].
units → mission: committed force-to-task responsibility can constrain detailed platform timing and support feasibility [S15, S26, S28].
mission → weapons, electronic: committed platform participation and activity windows constrain effect and electronic assignments [S26, S27, S30, S33]. These later allocations can also inform a new mission revision, but that feedback is a new decision episode.
remediation → response: planned controls and remaining known exposure constrain allowed containment and secure recovery alternatives [S29, S42].
alerts → forensics, response: triaged alert evidence initiates investigation or protective response [S37, S39, S42].
forensics → response: investigation evidence updates the known compromise and allowed response choices [S37, S42].
Inspection has no necessary within-set dependency. It can consume external treaties and verification requirements.
Potential feedback cycles: collection/search/mission; mission/effect allocation; triage/forensics/response; response/installed-policy revision. The compiled graph represents the initial or current commitment pass, not the entire feedback history. Feedback is not silently treated as an acyclic universal prerequisite. Direction and presence depend on integration; no mathematical reduction is used as an industrial edge.
Regime bands will govern compilation first. Fixed-task unit allocation precedes a richer mission program industrially but belongs to a later numbering band. That backward inter-band reference cannot be removed by in-band reordering. Alert triage and evidence-triggered forensic action selection share the dispatch band, where triage precedes investigation. Within-band design configuration and search/interdiction/mission flows have a defensible order.

MERGE, EXCLUSION AND TERRITORY LEDGER

Readiness crisis rescheduling → merge with readiness. Same returned unit-cycle artifact [S14].
Satellite and airborne collection; ad-hoc ISR tasking; disrupted retasking → collection variants, not Territory splits [S17–S19].
Installation, maritime and moving-asset escort protection → patrol variants. Alarm-conditioned response remains distinct because an observed alarm commits a contingent route [S20, S21, S41].
Air/maritime/joint mission packages and disruption repair → integrated mission variants. Do not create one Problem per vehicle or military service [S26, S27].
Static, immediate and rolling effect-resource assignment → weapons variants. Dynamic time-linked models require specific Template validation; ordinary matching is a restricted case [S30–S32].
Electronic allocation → retain separately from WTA because shared interference and electronic resource compatibility define a distinct professional contract [S33, S34].
Cyber control portfolios, roles, segmentation, deception, dynamic platforms, remediation, triage, investigation and response → retain distinct delivered protection artifacts and decision triggers. A single tool may integrate them; that does not make their contracts synonymous [S06–S13, S29, S37–S43].
CBRN protection, mine countermeasure service, counter-drone protection and space defense → no unsupported specialty cards. Test protective deployment, search, collection, screening, effect assignment and mission contracts first. Specialized operating constraints are background variants; material missing contracts may be revisited during human inspection.
General access anomaly detection, threat scoring, image fusion, adversary behavior prediction and combat simulation → input analytics or validation, not ICO without a discrete commitment.
Continuous waveform synthesis, trajectory control and fractional search/inspection effort alone → outside discrete ICO.
Standalone privacy release and civilian enterprise security → not automatically defense service Problems. Authorization and cyber defense enter DEF only for the specified defense safeguarding contract.
New weapon/platform architecture, product validation and equipment production → PROD or PROC by contract; outside DEF operational territory.
Modernization/acquisition capability program portfolios → potential INTEL × DEFENSE development or administration territory at ontology level. CPAT establishes a credible candidate [S44]. Do not silently widen #PRODUCTION or invent a new Industry Domain code.
Generic force-closure cargo movement, connectors, ordinary airlift and replenishment journeys → LOG [S45]. Security constraints must define the delivered mission before ownership can change.
War-reserve stock positioning, military spare-parts availability and replenishment → SCM under the normative contract test. Protective availability may be an interface but military ownership is insufficient.
Individual personnel shifts, training rosters and generic workforce allocation → HR. Mission-capable unit readiness remains DEF because it commits the force service [S14, S15].
Equipment repair task scheduling and manufacturing → PROD. Carrier withdrawal windows can be a readiness input; this does not transfer shop work to DEF.
Installed radio, downlink, compute and ordinary network traffic resources → COMP unless adversarial protection defines the chosen contract.
Military medical response → HC when clinical or patient conditions define feasibility; ordinary casualty transport needs a LOG/HC contract test.
Base construction and routine public-asset upkeep → CIVIC; utility balance and dispatch → ENER; financial claims → FIN; market rights and procurement mechanisms → MKT.
