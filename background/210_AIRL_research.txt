AIRL — Airline Operations
v0.1 | 8 October 2026 | Problem layer

IDENTITY AND RESEARCH BASIS

Code 210; mnemonic AIRL; Industry ##AIRLINE; Primary Domain 201 #LOG; Territory #PRODUCTION; Macro Domain anchor #DISP.
The user fixes code, mnemonic and industry territory. The remaining classification follows 000_DOMAIN.txt and background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt: airline flight, fleet, journey and integrated disruption operations belong to LOG. Providing airline transport is the core service, so Territory is PRODUCTION even though transport can be DISTRIBUTION in a manufacturer’s value chain. AIRL is the industry mnemonic, not a logistics suffix. No territory split is proposed.

Normative inputs: 000_DOMAIN.txt v0.5; background/000_DOMAIN_research.txt v0.5; background/000_PROBLEM_marketplace.txt; workflow/0_workflow.txt through workflow/5_output.txt. Existing generic LOG, HR and MKT coverage supplies boundary context, not an exhaustive airline inventory.
The requested published version remains v0.1. R/B/C/F/O stage progression is recorded in commit history without changing that user-specified label. No Cases are researched or compiled.

Scope: scheduled passenger and cargo carriers; hub-and-spoke, point-to-point, regional, long-haul, combination and dedicated freighter operations; charter flights as schedule/routing variants; airline-controlled aircraft stands, ground service and cargo-terminal movement contracts. Outsourced execution can instantiate the same movement decision, but an unrelated airport, forwarding or workforce contract does not become AIRL merely because an airline is a customer.
Ownership follows the delivered artifact: airline movement capability, flight program, aircraft rotation, journey assignment, aircraft load, airline-controlled handling calendar or revised movement program.
Material interfaces: HR supplies crew availability, qualification and legality; MKT supplies demand, prices, booked claims and commercial opportunity values; SCM supplies fuel, spare parts and ULD availability; CIVIC supplies shared airport assets and capacity; PROD supplies completed maintenance and aircraft engineering; COMP supplies computation and communications; FIN supplies budgets and financial conditions.
These inputs can be coupled constraints or Secondary Domains. They do not individually determine AIRL ownership.

EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE

Source keys below identify retrieved primary papers, author/institution pages, official operational descriptions or original challenge specifications. Abstract-only access is sufficient for the limited problem/method claims identified; it does not verify every constraint or implementation. Some publisher opens failed while indexed primary abstracts remained available. Such sources support Template existence, not a claim that full text, code or datasets were inspected.
Candidate classification, ratings, granularity and dependency edges are ontology applications and engineering inference unless marked as a reported result. No solver was run on industrial instances. No claimed savings, reproduced reference results, global SOTA winner or certified operational approval is inferred.
Templates distinguish practical framing from published algorithms. MILP, network flow, branch-and-price, CP, decomposition, simulation evaluation and search are alternatives at different scales, not a universal performance ranking.
Formal complexity is omitted from the proposed foreground unless an exact restriction is useful and evidenced. Industrial Hardness reflects coupled constraints, representation scale and time available for a decision. Polynomial matching or shortest-path restrictions do not imply that full integrated contracts are easy.
Public benchmark support is uneven. The original ROADEF 2009 specification establishes a public simplified integrated aircraft/passenger contract and explicitly omits crews. It is evidence for a later benchmark investigation, not a compiled Case or a complete industrial baseline.

TERRITORY MAP AND DISCOVERY

Strategic movement capability: service-network topology and hub choices; aircraft composition; ground-service vehicle capability.
Flight program: flight selection, frequencies, times, connectivity and buffers; maintenance availability calendars; fleet-level rotations; named-aircraft assignment; physical route/level choices.
Transport claims and loads: cargo paths; operational cabin seating; ULD packing; aircraft stowage and balance.
Ground movement: gates/stands within airline control; service-vehicle routes; terminal truck appointments and dock use; ULD build-up calendars; turnaround task calendars; boarding release order.
Execution: dispatch of available ground vehicles; connection-protection holds.
Disruption: aircraft program recovery; passenger reaccommodation; cargo itinerary recovery; integrated flight/resource/journey recovery; airline reprioritization of supplied ATFM opportunities.
Other aircraft repositioning, ferrying, spare-tail deployment, diversions and technical stops are routing or recovery variants unless an independent contract can be established.

Search rounds:
A. Airline scheduling hierarchy, network/fleet integration, load planning and turnaround/ground routing.
B. Hub network, fleet composition, robust schedule buffers and passenger connections.
C. Cargo paths, boarding/seating and discrete flight planning.
D. Aircraft reserve deployment, UDPP/slot substitution, baggage vehicles and passenger recovery.
E. Tail assignment, ULD palletization, ground fleet sizing and maintenance availability.
F. Cargo disruption, maintenance checks, ULD circulation and ground recovery.
G. Terminal throughput, integrated handling and aircraft recovery representations.
H. Official recovery specification, ULD batching/Benders and fuel-management boundary.
I. Independent omission check: truck appointments/dock coordination and ULD build-up scheduling.
New substantive additions in the final omission check were terminal truck scheduling and ULD build-up scheduling. Later hits mainly supplied alternate algorithms, robustness/energy variants, source access or neighboring contracts. This supports broad practical coverage, not a proof of exhaustiveness.

CANDIDATE INVENTORY

Symbolic keys are research labels. Permanent Problem IDs and within-band ordering are reserved for compilation.
Ratings below are proposed Priority / Relevance / industrial Hardness, with their reasoning. All retained candidates have the fixed AIRL identity.

NETWORK — Airline Service Network Design
Decision: choose served airport pairs, service connections and airline hubs under travel demand, airport access and operating capability. The artifact is the carrier’s movement network rather than a set of commercial offers.
Aliases: airline network planning, hub-and-spoke design, route-network design. Variants: point-to-point, gateway hubs, freighter feeders, reliability-aware topology, service frequencies coupled to network decisions.
Supplied substrate: candidate airports and connections, travel demand, access and fleet envelopes. Construction: choose hubs and service arcs; deliver connected movement opportunities. Primary #design; primary #link with #select. Connectivity concerns delivered service, not every airport necessarily reaching every other airport.
Boundary: construction or enlargement of the airport is CIVIC; purchase/allocation of access rights is MKT; setting flight departure times on the accepted service network is TIMETABLE. Commercial demand and profit can remain inputs/objectives of this physical service design.
Template: hub/network planning | capacitated hub-location and network-design MILP, decomposition or constrained network search. Joint fleet/network models are legitimate integrated variants. No single hub formulation covers all carrier types.
Evidence: S01 supports airline hub/network models; S02 supports joint global network/fleet planning; S03 supports supply/demand-coupled mixed-integer planning. Ratings Critical / Critical / High: topology governs enterprise coverage and downstream scheduling; hub, fleet and demand choices interact. These are engineering assessments.
Dependency: feeds FLEET, TIMETABLE and CARGO; fleet feasibility can constrain network redesign, but the foreground baseline edge describes accepted network requirements feeding composition.
Distinction: construction of movement capability precedes commitment to particular dated flights.

FLEET — Airline Fleet Composition
Decision: choose aircraft types and discrete fleet quantities over an acquisition/replacement plan to support transport requirements within investment and capability limits. Deliver an available aircraft capacity envelope.
Aliases: fleet planning, fleet mix, acquisition/replacement planning. Variants: passenger, all-cargo, scenario trees, retirement and replacement, owned/leased capability. Individual financing and supplier commitments are external.
Substrate: network requirements, aircraft alternatives, costs and demand scenarios. Construction: select aircraft options and quantities. Primary #design, primary #select. A lease financing variable does not automatically justify #provision; securing an unnamed contingent right would require a separately evidenced contract.
Template: fleet-capability planning | multi-period mixed-integer planning; scenario-based stochastic MILP when demand uncertainty matters. S04 establishes scenario-tree composition; S05 establishes an industrial all-cargo fleet/network application.
Ratings Critical / Critical / High: aircraft capability defines the feasible operation and major investment; future demand and commonality couple discrete choices. Published alternatives do not establish a universal current SOTA winner.
Dependency: NETWORK feeds FLEET; FLEET feeds FLEETING and MAINTENANCE.
Distinction: FLEETING uses an existing fleet to cover specific flights; it does not buy or retire aircraft.

GROUND_CAPACITY — Ground Service Fleet Sizing
Decision: choose the type mix and number of airline-controlled service vehicles needed to meet a supplied flight-service demand profile and reliability requirements. Installed ground movement capability is the artifact.
Aliases: GSE fleet sizing, ground support fleet planning. Variants: electric vehicles, service-specific fleets, charging as a coupled resource, seasonal demand.
Substrate: turnaround demand, vehicle alternatives, task duration/travel distributions and availability. Construction: select a discrete vehicle complement. Primary #design, primary #select; no invented vehicle-base placement field.
Template: service-fleet capacity analysis | discrete sizing search with simulation; coupled procurement/scheduling MILP for electric fleets. S06 supplies a carrier-specific electrification/fleet study; S07 supplies integrated sizing and charging formulations; these do not prove a common commercial deployment method.
Ratings High / High / High: carriers with substantial self-handling need sufficient vehicle capacity; stochastic service and charging couple fleet counts and peak feasibility.
Dependency: TIMETABLE demand informs sizing; sizing feeds GROUND_ROUTES and GROUND_DISPATCH. The #design decision can be revisited after a proposed timetable, so the cross-band incoming edge is intentional.
Boundary: fixed charger civil/electric works and utility supply are CIVIC/ENER contracts; buying staffing capacity is HR.

TIMETABLE — Airline Flight Schedule Design
Decision: choose dated/periodic flight legs, operating frequencies and departure/arrival times on the permitted service network under fleet, airport and connection conditions.
Aliases: schedule design, flight timetabling, frequency-and-time planning. Variants: hub banks, point-to-point, cargo cutoffs, incremental or network-wide timetabling, robust buffer placement.
Substrate: network and access opportunities, travel-demand forecasts and fleet capability. Construction: select flights and position them in time. Primary #plan; primary #place plus #select. Optional connection choices do not make supplied airport graph edges the Primary Structure.
Template: flight-program and connection planning | time-space/discrete-time MILP with fleet-feasibility integration, decomposition and heuristics. S08 supports network-wide timetabling with passenger choice; S09 supports joint flight selection/timing/fleeting/routing. S10 supports robustness through retiming/routing.
Ratings Critical / Critical / Critical: the flight program commits core service at carrier scale; demand, connections and subsequent feasibility require multiphase/integrated approximations in published methods.
Dependency: NETWORK feeds TIMETABLE; TIMETABLE feeds FLEETING, ROTATIONS, GROUND_CAPACITY, GATES, CARGO, GROUND_ROUTES, TRUCKS and integrated RECOVERY.
Distinction: published literature often calls timetabling “schedule design”; normative Regime is #plan because it commits future flights, not topology. Buffer distribution is a timing variant, not another canonical Problem.

MAINTENANCE — Aircraft Maintenance Visit Scheduling
Decision: choose aircraft withdrawal windows and maintenance visit locations from approved work-package/due-date requirements while preserving fleet availability and compatible aircraft movements.
Aliases: fleet maintenance calendar, maintenance-check scheduling. Variants: line or heavy-check visit windows, usage-based limits, uncertain durations, rolling check calendars.
Substrate: named-aircraft usage state, mandatory approved maintenance requirements and provider capacity. Construction: place visits in time and assign aircraft visits to allowed stations; primary #plan, primary #place plus #map.
Template: fleet availability and maintenance-window planning | multi-objective MILP; dynamic programming and look-ahead methods are near-equals for check-calendar variants. S11 supports maintenance calendars with tail-assignment coupling; S12 supports airline-data check scheduling. S13 verifies line task assignment/time planning exists, but it must not be used to silently include stand-alone shop work under LOG.
Ratings High / High / High: serviceable fleet availability and due windows materially constrain operations; tail history and movement compatibility interact.
Dependency: FLEET feeds MAINTENANCE; MAINTENANCE feeds ROTATIONS, TAILS and AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY. ROTATIONS and TAILS can return feasibility information to later maintenance revisions; this is iterative feedback rather than a compulsory reciprocal edge on every solve.
Boundary: the AIRL card commits unavailable/available aircraft windows and visits. Detailed repair jobshop calendars, component manufacture and independent technician allocation remain PROD/HR.
No airworthiness rule or interval is asserted here; approved requirements are inputs.

ROTATIONS — Aircraft Rotation Planning
Decision: construct station- and time-compatible chains of supplied flight legs for the fleet with maintenance access, overnight position and aircraft availability requirements.
Aliases: aircraft routing, aircraft maintenance routing, lines-of-flight generation. Variants: daily/weekly, cyclic/noncyclic, robust routing, ferry legs, optional technical stops and coupled fuel policies.
Substrate: TIMETABLE flights and FLEETING types, maintenance windows, airports and turn times. Construction: choose flight-successor relations to form valid paths/cycles, and assign legs to compatible route groups. Primary #plan; primary #link plus #map.
Template: aircraft rotation and maintenance-access planning | route-based set partitioning with column generation/branch-and-price; time-space MILP is a material near-equal. S14 establishes branch-and-price for maintenance routing; S15 establishes weekly routing and tail-assignment extensions; S16 supports robustness evaluation.
Ratings Critical / Critical / High: feasible aircraft circulation is essential; maintenance and endpoint conditions constrain large path sets.
Dependency: TIMETABLE, FLEETING and MAINTENANCE feed ROTATIONS; ROTATIONS feeds TAILS and AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY.
Distinction: TAILS maps named aircraft onto already supplied rotations. ROTATIONS constructs those rotations. If a tail-assignment algorithm regenerates routes, it has crossed into the integrated routing/assignment variant, not created a third Problem.
Supplied time-space arcs are not automatically #link; chosen flight-successor relations are.

FLIGHT_PATH — Flight Route and Level Planning
Decision: choose a physical path and permitted flight-level transitions for a flight under weather, restricted-route and aircraft-performance conditions. Deliver a dispatchable flight path, not a service-network arc between airports.
Aliases: operational flight planning, airway routing, discrete-continuous trajectory planning. Variants: time-dependent airway costs, free-route segments, alternate airports and technical-stop choices.
Substrate: waypoint/airway/level alternatives and flight conditions. Construction: choose successive path segments and position the trajectory at permitted levels/waypoints. Primary #plan; primary #link plus #place.
Template: performance- and weather-aware flight planning | constrained/time-dependent shortest-path search; discrete-continuous trajectory refinement for free-route variants. S17 provides the method distinction; S18 supplies original error-bound research.
Ratings High / High / High: fleet operations repeatedly require feasible paths; restrictions and coupled performance/time conditions complicate rich routes. Simple static shortest-path restrictions are routinely solvable and must not inherit this rich-contract rating uncritically.
Dependency: TAILS supplies aircraft-specific capability to FLIGHT_PATH; FLIGHT_PATH constrains LOAD and AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY.
Boundary: continuous-only speed/fuel control without a discrete choice is not a standalone ICO Problem. Shared ATC conflict resolution/airspace coordination is not an airline-owned path contract. Fuel amounts alone are excluded from ICO here.
Structure #place refers to the delivered geometric/level path, not to a shortest-path graph used only for encoding.

PACKING — Air Cargo Unit Load Device Packing
Decision: assign physical cargo pieces to ULDs and choose their positions/orientations within permitted contours under weight, support and compatibility limits.
Aliases: air cargo palletization, ULD build-up packing, air-cargo loading at package level. Variants: pallets/containers, irregular contours, dangerous-goods compatibility, staged or joint aircraft loading.
Substrate: cargo pieces, ULD types/contours and physical limits. Construction: place oriented pieces and map them to ULDs. Primary #plan; primary #place plus #map. Optional exclusion/choice of ULD types can add #select in a scoped variant; not required in the fixed-ULD foreground.
Template: cargo consolidation and contour packing | three-dimensional packing MILP for restricted sizes; extreme-point/constructive heuristics with improvement for larger instances. S19 supports combinatorial packing/loading; S20 supports practical three-dimensional pallet loading; S21 reports a two-stage packing/build-up-calendar formulation.
Ratings High / High / High: cargo carriers and combination carriers depend on feasible ULDs; shapes, weight and loading rules make packing scale-sensitive.
Dependency: CARGO feeds PACKING; PACKING feeds LOAD and BUILDUP.
Distinction: PACKING defines ULD contents/geometry; BUILDUP defines workstation/time commitments; LOAD places completed ULDs in aircraft hold stations.

LOAD — Aircraft Load and Balance Planning
Decision: select admissible payload units and assign them to aircraft hold stations while meeting aircraft mass, station load and center-of-gravity-envelope limits.
Aliases: weight and balance optimization, aircraft stowage, air-cargo load planning. Variants: lateral balance, multi-leg unloading, different aircraft configurations and combined palletization.
Substrate: completed ULDs/payload, aircraft station geometry/capabilities, flight/fuel conditions and certified limits supplied externally. Construction: map payload to fixed hold stations and select units to transport; primary #plan, primary #map plus #select. Fixed stations do not require an additional geometric #place tag.
Template: aircraft load control | binary assignment/selection MILP with envelope constraints; multileg unloading order can enter the same load contract. S22 supports cargo selection/weight balance; S23 distinguishes true CG envelopes from target deviation; S24 supports pickup/delivery loading.
Ratings High / Critical / Medium: feasible loading is operationally mandatory and has direct safety/capacity impact; structured single-aircraft MILP is established, while geometric/multileg restrictions can be harder.
Dependency: TAILS, FLIGHT_PATH, CARGO and PACKING feed LOAD; LOAD feeds TURNAROUND and AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY.
Boundary: choosing payload that physically fits/flies is LOG even when revenue weights value the choice. Fare availability, overbooking and sales acceptance without physical stowage are MKT.
No source-reported optimizer is asserted to certify flight safety.

GROUND_ROUTES — Ground Service Vehicle Routing and Scheduling
Decision: assign service visits to a supplied heterogeneous ground fleet and construct feasible visit routes/times around flights, stands and turnaround task windows.
Aliases: ground-handling vehicle scheduling, GSE routing, baggage tractor routing, passenger-bus routing. Variants: towing, catering, refueling visits, baggage trains, charging/reloads, multi-trip pickup/delivery.
Substrate: flights, gates, service demands, task windows, vehicle capacity and road geometry. Construction: choose vehicle-successor visits, assign tasks and place service starts. Primary #plan; primary #link plus #map plus #place.
Template: synchronized heterogeneous ground-service routing | VRPTW MILP with routing heuristics/decomposition; multi-trip pickup/delivery for baggage transport. S25 supports ground fleet scheduling; S26 supports baggage transit routing. Service precedence couples routes to turnaround but does not convert this into employee rostering.
Ratings High / High / High: ground fleets can be a recurring departure bottleneck; synchronization and service-specific capacities require rich routing methods.
Dependency: GROUND_CAPACITY, TIMETABLE and GATES feed GROUND_ROUTES; routes provide TURNAROUND resource windows and GROUND_DISPATCH initial plans.
The TURNAROUND calendar can feed revised GROUND_ROUTES; this operational coupling is cyclic across planning iterations, and neither card owns every routing/scheduling variable of a joint model.
Distinction: a precommitted route/calendar is #plan; selecting the next available vehicle action from live state is GROUND_DISPATCH.

TRUCKS — Air Cargo Terminal Truck Scheduling
Decision: assign inbound trucks to docks and arrival/unloading times coordinated with airline outbound flights and terminal handling capacity.
Aliases: cargo terminal truck appointments, dock/arrival scheduling. Variants: direct transfer versus temporary storage, shipment unloading order, confirmed arrival slots.
Substrate: truck consignments, dock capability, flight cutoffs and unload requirements. Construction: assign trucks to docks and position their arrival/unloading activities. Primary #plan; primary #place plus #map.
Template: flight-cutoff-coordinated terminal arrivals | time-indexed integer programming with Lagrangian-relaxation or constructive scheduling heuristics. S27 is an original model and algorithm paper; its strong NP-hardness claim applies to its truck-scheduling model, not the entire AIRL territory.
Ratings Medium / High / High: recurrent mainly at cargo terminals with carrier control over appointments; dock availability and flight cutoffs couple detailed unloading.
Dependency: TIMETABLE and CARGO feed TRUCKS; TRUCKS feeds BUILDUP.
Boundary: this is included only for airline-controlled freight-transfer programs. Shared landside infrastructure or independent road-carrier routing remains neighboring CIVIC/LOG territory. Truck inventory is supplied, not designed.

BUILDUP — Air Cargo ULD Build-Up Scheduling
Decision: assign ULD preparation jobs to compatible terminal workstations and choose their times/order under arriving cargo, limited handling space and departure cutoffs.
Aliases: pallet build-up scheduling, air freight hub batching. Variants: dynamic batching, rolling look-ahead, break-down/build-up coupling; staffing capacity is supplied or a coupled resource.
Substrate: cargo readiness, planned ULD contents, flight departure deadlines and workstation capacity. Construction: assign ULD jobs to workstations and place their execution/relative order. Primary #operate with Secondary #sequence; primary #place plus #map.
Template: flight-cutoff-driven terminal handling | resource-constrained scheduling with MILP/CP; logic-based Benders decomposition for integrated allocation/scheduling variants. S28 identifies original batching and Benders research; S21 supports a parallel-workstation model. Evidence establishes published methods, not enterprise deployment superiority.
Ratings High / High / High: cargo terminal flow must finish ULDs for flights; arrival, floor-space and workstation interactions constrain calendars.
Dependency: PACKING, TRUCKS and CARGO feed BUILDUP; BUILDUP feeds TURNAROUND and GROUND_DISPATCH.
Boundary: ULD geometry is PACKING; employee shifts/skills as the sole artifact are HR. Breaking down and repacking cargo stays physical handling; manufacturing a ULD is PROD.
No case ID, benchmark instance choice or public-code claim is made.

FLEETING — Aircraft Fleet Assignment
Decision: assign an available aircraft type to each supplied flight under flow balance, fleet count, range/capacity and maintenance-access conditions.
Aliases: fleet assignment problem, FAP, fleeting. Variants: itinerary-aware recapture, dynamic up/down-gauging, multiple fleets and integrated schedule/routing.
Substrate: flights, aircraft type capacities/costs and network continuity. Construction: map flight legs to types; primary #allocate, primary #map. Flow balance constrains assignment but need not be a chosen #link construction in the fixed-flight contract.
Template: fleet-type-to-flight capacity matching | time-space multicommodity-flow MILP; decomposition and itinerary-aware formulations. S29 is original fleet-assignment research; S30 supplies the established model/algorithm tutorial.
Ratings Critical / Critical / High: types commit carrier capacity on flights; leg assignments are coupled by fleet circulation and travel demand.
Dependency: FLEET and TIMETABLE feed FLEETING; FLEETING feeds ROTATIONS, TAILS and CARGO.
Distinction: purchasing fleet options is FLEET; mapping specific tails to fixed rotations is TAILS.

TAILS — Aircraft Tail Assignment
Decision: assign individual aircraft to supplied feasible rotations under initial/final location, usage history, configuration and maintenance restrictions.
Aliases: tail assignment problem, TAP, aircraft-to-line assignment. Variants: daily look-ahead, fixed-route repair and tail-specific compatibility; integrated route regeneration belongs jointly with ROTATIONS.
Substrate: named tails and pre-generated lines. Construction: map tails to route groups; primary #allocate, primary #map. Primary #link is not needed when rotations are supplied and immutable.
Template: named-aircraft rotation allocation | constrained assignment MILP; route-based branch-and-price when route regeneration is permitted. S31 explicitly establishes assignment to supplied lines and look-ahead maintenance; S32 supports close-to-operation integrated routing/tail assignment.
Ratings Critical / High / Medium: each operated rotation requires an actual compatible aircraft; fixed-line variants are structured assignments, with higher difficulty when reassignment changes rotations.
Dependency: ROTATIONS, FLEETING and MAINTENANCE feed TAILS; TAILS feeds FLIGHT_PATH, LOAD and AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY.
Distinction: this fixed-route allocation is retained as an independent industrial handoff, rather than treating tail assignment and route construction as synonyms.

GATES — Airline Gate and Stand Assignment
Decision: assign airline-controlled flights/aircraft to compatible gates or remote stands and permitted occupancy windows under conflict and passenger-transfer requirements.
Aliases: gate assignment, stand allocation, tactical gate planning. Variants: remote parking, towing, disrupted reassignment and passenger connection walking time.
Substrate: airline flight plan, available stand opportunities and aircraft/stand compatibility. Construction: map flights to stands and place occupancy intervals where movable. Primary #allocate with Secondary #adapt for disrupted reassignment; primary #map plus #place.
Template: airline gate/stand occupancy planning | binary assignment and interval-conflict MILP; rolling gate reassignment and local search. S33 explicitly distinguishes an airline-specific gate system; S34 supports passenger-connection-aware disrupted assignments.
Ratings High / High / High: self-controlled hub stands affect turnaround/transfer reliability; interval conflicts and transfer costs couple choices.
Dependency: TIMETABLE and TAILS feed GATES; GATES feeds GROUND_ROUTES, TURNAROUND and HOLDING.
Boundary: airport-wide concession rules, common-use asset design and shared apron-capacity governance are not this airline operating contract.
Cycle: ground readiness can revise gate windows; after a disruption, integrated recovery can feed an updated stand plan.

CARGO — Air Cargo Itinerary Assignment
Decision: assign accepted physical shipments to flight or permitted ground-feeder paths under capacity, transfer and delivery-window limits.
Aliases: cargo routing, cargo flow allocation, shipment-to-flight assignment. Variants: splittable/indivisible orders, freighter/belly mix, through cargo, truck feeders, empty-ULD movement on available flights.
Substrate: accepted shipments and network flight capacities. Construction: map shipments to leg resources and choose connected travel paths; primary #allocate, primary #map plus #link.
Template: time-definite cargo movement planning | time-space multicommodity-flow or path-based integer programming with column generation. S35 establishes joint fleet/cargo paths; S36 supports cargo network planning. Commodity path formulations support Template existence; they do not prove every claimed variant uses the same method.
Ratings High / High / High: cargo carriers and combination fleets must deliver finite-capacity shipment programs; indivisible consignments and transfers complicate routing.
Dependency: NETWORK, TIMETABLE and FLEETING feed CARGO; CARGO feeds PACKING, LOAD, TRUCKS, BUILDUP and CARGO_RECOVERY.
Boundary: booking acceptance, prices and overbooking without physical movement construction belong to MKT; stocking/replenishing ULDs belongs to SCM. Empty ULD physical transfer can remain a route commodity here.
Continuous divisible flow restrictions may be easy; industrial granularity and physical compatibility determine difficulty.

SEATING — Operational Passenger Seat Assignment
Decision: assign already accepted passengers/groups to physical cabin seats within supplied cabin configuration, fixed seat commitments and movement/grouping constraints.
Aliases: cabin seat assignment, passenger seat allocation. Variants: families/groups, boarding interference, reserved seats, physical separation conditions supplied by an operating policy.
Substrate: booked travelers, seat layout and fixed commitments. Construction: map people to seats; primary #allocate, primary #map. Seats already have fixed coordinates; #place is not inferred from that layout.
Template: group-aware cabin seating | constrained-assignment MILP; boarding-aware simulation evaluation where seating affects boarding. S37 supplies operational group/separation assignment; S38 supplies assignment with carry-on interference. Pandemic separation is a historical variant, not an asserted current requirement.
Ratings Medium / Medium / Medium: operational reassignment occurs but many seats are preselected commercially; constrained group assignment can be tractable, while interference adds complexity.
Dependency: TAILS provides physical cabin configuration to SEATING; SEATING feeds BOARDING.
Boundary: charging for seats, commercial upsell, cabin-product design and revenue availability are MKT/PROD decisions. No commercial preference or medical policy is silently added.

TURNAROUND — Aircraft Turnaround Scheduling
Decision: assign the starts/order and available service resources for aircraft ground tasks so aircraft become departure-ready within safety precedence and stand/resource conditions.
Aliases: turn scheduling, airline hub turnaround control. Variants: synchronized loading/fueling/boarding, deicing windows, integrated ground routing, uncertain task durations.
Substrate: aircraft arrivals, ground-task requirements/precedences, load readiness, stand and vehicle windows. Construction: place tasks in time and assign supplied service resources; primary #operate with Secondary #sequence, primary #place plus #map. Given precedences are constitutive feasibility laws; they are not a constructed #link.
Template: airline ground-task critical-path coordination | RCPSP/MILP or CP; simulation-supported rolling scheduling. S39 supports airline ground RCPSP recovery; S40 supports integrated aircraft/turnaround assignment.
Ratings High / High / High: turnaround readiness repeatedly governs departure performance; scarce shared resources, uncertain arrivals and task dependencies interact.
Dependency: LOAD, GATES, GROUND_ROUTES and BUILDUP feed TURNAROUND; TURNAROUND feeds BOARDING, GROUND_DISPATCH, HOLDING and RECOVERY.
Boundary: task order is integrated physical service orchestration, not stand-alone employee rosters; detailed repair-work content remains PROD.
Cycle: routing windows and task starts are solved iteratively or jointly; foreground direction represents baseline route availability consumed by the task calendar.

BOARDING — Passenger Boarding Grouping and Sequencing
Decision: group checked-in passengers and choose release order to board a supplied aircraft under seat locations, party cohesion and baggage/aisle interference.
Aliases: boarding policy, boarding groups, boarding order. Variants: outside-in, back-to-front, individual/group releases, dynamic seats as a coupled SEATING decision.
Substrate: checked-in passengers, assigned seats and cabin/aisle structure. Construction: place groups/people in release order and map people into groups; primary #operate with Secondary #sequence, primary #place plus #map.
Template: cabin-flow and interference control | sequence/group optimization with simulation; MILP or local search for defined group models. S41 supports group determination; S42 is original boarding-order/simulation research.
Ratings Medium / Medium / Medium: useful on short turns but compliance and observed behavior limit gains; practical group models and simulation searches are established.
Dependency: SEATING and TURNAROUND feed BOARDING; BOARDING feeds HOLDING.
Distinction: publishing a boarding sequence commits the order of work already in turnaround; it is not #design merely because a policy can be reused.
No laboratory-optimal sequence is asserted to be the universal operational best practice.

GROUND_DISPATCH — Ground Service Vehicle Dispatch
Decision: choose the next service requests and available vehicles to activate or reposition from live aircraft, request, vehicle and readiness states.
Aliases: online ground-service dispatch, apron vehicle activation, baggage/tow/bus dispatch. Variants: charging/resource failures, emergent tasks, dispatch priorities.
Substrate: available fleet, initial routes, task calendar and evolving state. Construction: map requests to vehicles and select actions for immediate release. Primary #dispatch, primary #map plus #select. Full downstream route reconstruction is a GROUND_ROUTES/recovery coupling; the core next-action contract does not require #link.
Template: event-driven ground-service control | fast sequential assignment/routing heuristics; rolling-horizon MILP for constrained dispatch windows. S43 explicitly supplies a real-time dispatch-oriented heuristic; S25/S26 establish service-specific routing context.
Ratings High / High / High: short-cycle action affects aircraft readiness; state changes and heterogeneous vehicle compatibility restrict latency.
Dependency: GROUND_CAPACITY, GROUND_ROUTES, BUILDUP and TURNAROUND feed GROUND_DISPATCH; dispatched service feeds updated turnaround readiness.
Direction TURNAROUND -> GROUND_DISPATCH records calendar consumption; GROUND_DISPATCH -> TURNAROUND records state feedback. This cycle is intentional.
Workforce availability is supplied; separate employee activation/rosters belong to HR.

HOLDING — Departure Connection Protection
Decision: choose whether and how long to hold a ready outgoing flight for connecting passengers while respecting downstream aircraft, stand and crew feasibility.
Aliases: connection management, departure holding, wait/depart control. Variants: passenger priority, uncertain transfer time, coordinated nearby holds.
Substrate: current arrival/transfer estimates, outgoing readiness, passenger connections and movement commitments. Construction: select held departures and place release times; primary #dispatch, primary #select plus #place.
Template: passenger-connection and delay-propagation control | constrained hold/release optimization within a passenger-aware RCPSP or network MILP; local search for integrated variants. S39 and S44 directly establish holding within airline recovery methods; S33 establishes gate holding effects.
Ratings High / High / High: hubs repeatedly trade missed connections against propagating delay; route and stand effects complicate local choices.
Dependency: GATES, TURNAROUND and BOARDING feed HOLDING; HOLDING feeds PASSENGER_RECOVERY and RECOVERY.
Distinction: a localized release decision preserves the movement program where possible; broad flight cancellation/rerouting belongs to #adapt RECOVERY.
No passenger-rights threshold or mandatory holding rule is asserted.

AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY — Aircraft Schedule Recovery
Decision: revise flight operation, times and aircraft rotations after aircraft outages, airport restrictions or delay while respecting maintenance and recovery-end positions.
Aliases: ARP, aircraft recovery, fleet recovery, aircraft schedule repair. Variants: swaps, cancellations, ferrying, diversions, maintenance flexibility and uncertain disruptions.
Substrate: committed aircraft program and changed availability/capacity. Construction: change flight-successor links, map aircraft to legs and select kept/cancelled flights with revised times. Primary #adapt; primary #link plus #map plus #select plus #place.
Template: aircraft-centric irregular-operations control | time-space/network MILP; route-column generation and large-neighborhood repair. S45 supports two-stage uncertain recovery; S46 supports capacity/maintenance-aware column generation; S47 supports constraint-specific networks.
Ratings Critical / Critical / High: moving aircraft back into feasible service is central to irregular operations; large network repairs and time pressure require specialized search/decomposition.
Dependency: ROTATIONS, TAILS, MAINTENANCE, LOAD and FLIGHT_PATH feed AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY; revised flights/capacities feed PASSENGER_RECOVERY, CARGO_RECOVERY and RECOVERY.
Boundary: crew feasibility remains an input or integration constraint; a crew-only recovery artifact is HR.
Independent aircraft recovery does not guarantee a good passenger result; this motivates RECOVERY rather than reclassifying passenger recovery as an alias.

PASSENGER_RECOVERY — Passenger Itinerary Recovery
Decision: replace disrupted booked passenger journeys with feasible remaining itineraries and allocate available seats, preserving completed travel and group/cabin conditions when specified.
Aliases: PRP, passenger reaccommodation, passenger rerouting, rebooking after disruption. Variants: preemptive rerouting, other-carrier or air-rail alternatives, split groups under explicit policy.
Substrate: disrupted journeys, revised flight program, existing bookings and remaining physical capacity. Construction: map people/groups to replacement itineraries and select connected travel legs; primary #adapt, primary #map plus #link.
Template: disrupted journey completion | integer multicommodity-flow/path assignment; stochastic programming for preemptive rerouting. S48 supports an original passenger-recovery integer-flow model; S49 supports two-stage preemptive rerouting.
Ratings Critical / Critical / High: affected travelers must compete for residual journey capacity; many linked alternatives and indivisible groups constrain network-wide assignment.
Dependency: AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY and HOLDING feed PASSENGER_RECOVERY; completion costs/feasibility feed RECOVERY.
Boundary: recovery completes accepted movement claims. Pricing replacement tickets or solving commercial sales acceptance without transport completion is MKT; financial compensation calculation alone is FIN.
A path may be supplied as an alternative, but choosing its legs determines the delivered replacement journey; #link reflects that industrial artifact, not just column generation.

CARGO_RECOVERY — Air Cargo Itinerary Recovery
Decision: revise previously committed cargo paths after flight, capacity, deadline or shipment changes with permitted transshipment and alternative transport.
Aliases: cargo recovery, air-cargo schedule recovery when shipment scope is fixed. Variants: demand changes, belly/freighter alternatives, trucking, priority orders and indivisible freight.
Substrate: accepted shipment transport commitments and altered movement opportunities. Construction: map shipments to replacement capacity and choose connected paths. Primary #adapt; primary #map plus #link. Flight-program changes belong jointly to RECOVERY when decisions are cooptimized.
Template: cargo disruption and delivery-window repair | path-based integer programming and matheuristics; integrated aircraft/cargo column-and-row generation as an extension. S50/S51 establish cargo disruption recovery and a matheuristic; S52 supplies aircraft/cargo integration.
Ratings High / High / High: time-definite cargo operations need reallocation under scarce capacity; deadline, handling and physical compatibility constrain revisions.
Dependency: CARGO and AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY feed CARGO_RECOVERY; revised cargo feasibility feeds RECOVERY, PACKING and LOAD.
Distinction: original cargo path allocation and revision of an accepted path are separate industrial handoffs, although the #adapt algorithm can reuse the allocation formulation.
Cargo/passenger differences are physical handling and shipment indivisibility, not another Territory.

RECOVERY — Integrated Airline Disruption Recovery
Decision: jointly revise the flight program, aircraft use, passenger/cargo journeys and material coupled crew/ground commitments after disruption.
Aliases: IARP/IADRP, integrated schedule recovery, airline disruption management. Variants: aircraft/passenger, aircraft/crew, full multi-resource, passenger or cargo-oriented, uncertain/rolling recovery, speed control as a continuous coupling.
Substrate: already committed transport program and live disruption. Construction: select flight actions, change movement-successor relations, map resources/claims and revise times. Primary #adapt; primary #select plus #link plus #map plus #place.
Template: airline operations-control coordination | decomposed network MILP with column generation or large-neighborhood search; conic mixed-integer models for speed-coupled variants. S53/S54 supply original integrated recovery architectures; S55 establishes a public simplified aircraft/passenger contract; S56 supports aircraft/ground-turnaround coupling.
Ratings Critical / Critical / Critical: joint resource and journey feasibility governs enterprise recovery; model size and short decision windows require restriction, decomposition, aggregation or heuristic repair.
Dependency: TIMETABLE, TURNAROUND, AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY, PASSENGER_RECOVERY, CARGO_RECOVERY and SLOT_PRIORITY feed RECOVERY; RECOVERY revises flight, tail, gate, cargo and ground movement commitments.
This is admitted as a distinct joint movement-program contract, not an additional stage that every deployment must run. Stand-alone subproblem outputs can be inputs/warm starts; simultaneous optimization need not impose sequential implementation.
Boundary: joint crew variables support an airline movement program, consistent with normative LOG/HR interfaces; crew-only repair remains HR. A benchmark without crews is not a full industry model.
Evidence does not show that one integrated method dominates all airline scales or disruption patterns.

SLOT_PRIORITY — Airline ATFM Slot Reprioritization
Decision: revise which of an airline’s flights consume supplied delayed arrival/departure opportunities, using approved substitution and prioritization mechanisms.
Aliases: airline slot substitution, UDPP, airline delay reprioritization. Variants: intra-airline swaps, authorized exchanges, frozen flights, goal-slot priorities.
Substrate: operator-assigned capacity opportunities, airline flights and delay-cost/feasibility conditions. Construction: remap flights to available times and choose priorities/substitutions. Primary #adapt; primary #map plus #select. Slots are supplied temporal resources; #place is not needed unless new times are generated.
Template: airspace-user delay-priority management | integer slot-assignment/prioritization optimization; manual or semi-automated operational procedures remain the reported comparator. S57 is official UDPP context; S58 reports an integer-model implementation and explicitly distinguishes current manual/semi-automated practice.
Ratings High / High / High: regulated carrier networks face materially different delay propagation costs; mechanism restrictions and downstream feasibility complicate reassignment.
Dependency: TIMETABLE and TAILS feed SLOT_PRIORITY; SLOT_PRIORITY feeds AIRCRAFT_RECOVERY and RECOVERY.
Boundary: slot ownership auctions, access-right sales and commercial clearing are MKT; network-manager allocation across airlines and shared airport/airspace service coordination require separate ownership.
No automatic permission to swap slots or an operational deployment level is inferred from an optimization model.

KNOWN GAPS AND UNCERTAINTY

No global algorithm ranking is established. Industrial systems use incremental, sequential and integrated methods at different stages; published integration is not proof of ubiquitous deployment.
The ground-fleet sizing evidence includes carrier/airport case studies and newer electrification models; operational applicability requires matching vehicle duty, charge and reliability assumptions.
Passenger seating evidence includes special separation/boarding variants. The canonical scope is physical operational allocation; commercial seat choice is excluded, and no historical separation policy is treated as current.
Terminal handling ownership is conditional on an airline movement contract. General forwarding terminals and independent airport infrastructure remain outside AIRL.
Spare-aircraft reservation and contingent capacity rights lack a sufficiently distinct supported contract in this search; they remain variants/interfaces rather than forced unused Regime bands.
Source access varies. Public abstract/institutional evidence establishes problem/method identity; later Case work must verify datasets, license, runnable implementations, complete objective/constraints and reference results.
Airworthiness, crew-law, dangerous-goods and ATFM rules are supplied operational inputs. This taxonomy does not restate or certify those rules.
Formal classes are not transferred from a familiar knapsack/VRP submodel to all rich airline contracts.
Research saturation is a practical coverage judgment. A future industrial contract with a distinct artifact may justify an additional Problem after research/control.
No case-level work has begun.
