EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE
Source facts are the limited observations recorded in the source register. Contract definitions, candidate classifications, qualitative ratings, exclusions, and dependency directions are analytical inferences unless explicitly identified as source facts.
Published model evidence supports Template existence. It does not establish widespread industrial adoption, current global SOTA, or solver dominance. Official software evidence supports an operational function, not performance leadership.
Some publication pages could not be opened. Their primary-publication search-index abstracts were inspected. Those records support only the stated abstract-level facts; algorithm details, exact guarantees, datasets, and performance are not inferred from them.
Templates are defensible established approach families. Material near-equals are retained. Public CP-SAT is a reproducible architecture reference; neither proprietary APS internals nor a universal best solver are claimed.
Priority measures centrality across the domain; Relevance measures the conditional value of improvement; Hardness measures practical industrial effort. All ratings are reasoned judgments, not measured deployment-frequency statistics or estimated monetary returns.
Complexity is variant-specific. General NP-hard labels are limited to contracts containing the general scheduling models supported by S29. No whole-family complexity theorem is invented for the other parents.

TERRITORY
Physical contract: select or configure manufacturable products and production capabilities; plan fabrication, assembly, and recovery; allocate manufacturing resources; and control execution of discrete workpieces.
Automotive and appliances: configuration, line balancing, mixed-model streams, tool and fixture setups, tolerance matching, production-maintenance integration.
Aerospace and defense manufacturing: feature routes, scarce capabilities, multiresource shops, fixtures, discrete-part batches, assembly and rework. Security context alone does not transfer ordinary production to INTEL.
Electronics and contract manufacturing: feeder setups, placement execution, family changeovers, flexible loading and shops, test-system design.
Semiconductor fabs and back-end: qualification, reentry, batching, reticles and secondary resources, constrained dwell time, robotic cluster cycles, release and dispatch. Wafer identity and fabrication feasibility define PROD; generic computation is COMP.
Industrial equipment and engineer-to-order systems: manufacturable configuration, machining routes, cellular production, spatial layout and large assemblies.
Shipbuilding: cutting/nesting, block process plans, fixed-position and spatial assembly scheduling. Hull-block manufacturing is PROD; public construction execution is CIVIC.
Additive systems: orientation, build grouping and placement, machine scheduling, post-processing as shop operations. Chemistry control alone remains PROC or a non-ICO process model.
Battery pack assembly and packaging: component matching, assembly routes, line balances and unit sequences. Cell chemistry and recipe transformation remain PROC.
Furniture, sheet metal, clothing and footwear: cutting/nesting, assembly and shop plans. Their industry labels do not create additional Domain classes.
Remanufacturing: core selection, physical recovery, disassembly, reprocessing and reassembly. Collection, transport and return-network positioning remain LOG/SCM.

BOUNDARIES AND INTERFACES
PROC: primary ownership follows whether recipe/material-state transformation or discrete component/fabrication/assembly feasibility defines the contract. A furnace processing identifiable parts can be PROD; a reactor recipe can be PROC.
SCM: demand, purchased materials, external capacity offers and component availability feed PROD. Supplier selection, replenishment and supply-position decisions remain SCM. Production lot activations are PROD even when inventories couple them.
LOG: transport and warehouse kit delivery feed production. A handling action belongs in a PROD robotic-cycle decision only when cell occupancy and manufacturing synchronization define the delivered artifact.
HR: personnel skills and staffing can constrain a production plan. Standalone employee rosters remain HR. Coupled task-to-station balances remain PROD where manufacturing work content defines feasibility.
ENER: utility prices, power limits and service availability are production inputs. Utility-system commitment and network dispatch remain ENER.
COMP: MES computation and circuit physical design are COMP where digital-resource or information feasibility dominates. Semiconductor fabrication and component mounting remain PROD.
CIVIC/LAND: manufacturing site-building and land-use decisions are external. Locating production departments within an existing factory belongs to PROD.
MKT: product offers, prices, assortment, and commercial order acceptance are external. Choosing a physically realizable BOM and manufacturing route belongs to PROD.

DISCOVERY AND IDENTITY
Searches covered system design, group technology, factory layout, line balancing, product configuration, machining and assembly process planning, capacity and lot planning, machine eligibility, tooling, fixture setups, cutting, additive manufacturing, tolerance matching, inspection, batch processors, lot streaming, robotics, maintenance, release, dispatch and disruption repair.
Discovery was based on the normative territory and independent literature searches, not PROD.md or a previous numbered list. The 30 keys preserve industrial decisions rather than one card per algorithm or scheduling notation.
Shop topology variants are merged into SHOP. The following additional artifacts justify separate parents: BATCH chooses co-processing groups; STREAM chooses transfer sublots; BUILD chooses build-space arrangements; ROBOT chooses repeated manufacturing cycles; MIXED orders the product stream. They can be integrated with SHOP without becoming synonyms.
An integrated model does not automatically justify an additional Problem. PROCESS+SHOP, CELL+LAYOUT, LOT+SHOP, BUILD+SHOP, and production+energy coupling retain their constituent contracts. MAINT is admitted because maintenance actions are themselves chosen and determine available production capacity.
LOAD produces resource workload commitments; SHOP produces operation calendars. QUAL constructs capability permissions before LOAD or SHOP consumes them. A fixed eligibility matrix belongs in the downstream input and does not create a qualification decision.
Known evidence limits: universal method leadership and deployment prevalence are not established. Detailed fixture geometry, product-specific inspection risk, stochastic recovery yields, and live-control latency require case-level evidence later. These limits do not prevent identifying the parent decisions and established Template families.

KEY SHOP — Shop Production Scheduling
DECISION CONTRACT Assign manufacturing operations to eligible resources and times under precedence, setups, and capacity limits.
SEARCH ALIASES Job-shop scheduling; flexible job-shop scheduling; machine scheduling.
VARIANTS Flow shop, hybrid flow shop, parallel machines, open shop, reentrant fab, assembly shop, no-wait, blocking, spatial block assembly, secondary resources.
IDENTITY AND BOUNDARY Fixed routes and supplied precedences do not create a network-design problem. Vehicle delivery is LOG; supplied worker eligibility does not make this a standalone HR roster.
OWNERSHIP INFERENCE PROD is primary because discrete manufacturing, fabrication or assembly defines this contract. Relevant interfaces follow the boundary above; no Industry label changes that primary ownership.
COMMITMENT INFERENCE Primary #plan. Prepares a future manufacturing plan before its execution.
CONSTRUCTION INFERENCE Primary #place; secondary #map. Places operations in time and maps flexible operations to resources. Fixed routings are inputs. Machine exclusivity, supplied precedence, and assembly synchronization define feasible schedules. Spatial variants also place blocks on the shop floor.
TEMPLATE EVIDENCE IE: Finite-capacity production scheduling. OR: Interval CP/CP-SAT or scheduling MILP; decomposition and local search. [S21, S29, S34, S35]
MATERIAL ALTERNATIVES CP handles rich resource calendars; MILP supports tight integrated cost models; shifting-bottleneck and neighborhood search remain useful at scale. S21 is publicly implementable, not a claim of industrial dominance.
RATING INFERENCE Priority Critical: Detailed manufacturing calendars are a central factory coordination decision. Relevance Critical: Large-factory schedules can affect enterprise output, service and asset use. Hardness High: Large operation sets, sequence-dependent setups, reentry, and secondary resources require tailored search or decomposition.
COMPLEXITY SCOPE NP-hard in the general case [S29]. Practical Hardness is not deduced from this label.
INFORMATION OUTPUTS DISPATCH RESCHEDULE. The issued calendar feeds immediate dispatch and is the prior commitment that repair revises.
REFINEMENTS ##schedule ##precedence ##setup ##reentrant.
LIMITS AND UNCERTAINTY An established formulation family is supported. No publicly established universal strongest solver exists across these variants.

EXCLUSIONS, MERGES, AND RESERVED QUESTIONS
Flexible job shop, hybrid flow shop, reentrant fab, tool-constrained shop, assembly shop and spatial block scheduling: SHOP variants. Different feasible calendars do not by themselves establish new parent decisions.
Setup sequencing and changeover minimization: SHOP or MIXED variants when the chosen artifact is an ordinary production order. TOOL and PCB retain separate physical setup choices when those choices are endogenous.
Machine-fixture-pallet calendar scheduling: SHOP variant. FIXTURE is admitted only for choosing physical setups and part placements.
Product-family formation: CELL when it establishes production groups, or TOOL/PCB setup-family variants when used for setup planning. No generic algorithm-named clustering card.
WIP threshold calibration, continuous aggregate planning, continuous buffer tuning, statistical quality estimation and continuous motion optimization: excluded when there is no combinatorial decision. Discrete policy selection or activation can instantiate an admitted parent.
Industrial engineering methods such as lean, Kanban, CONWIP, MRP, digital twins, simulation, CP, MILP, reinforcement learning and genetic algorithms: practices or methods, not separate Problems.
Order acceptance, pricing, quoting and customer order promising: MKT when the delivered decision is a commercial commitment. Internal finite-capacity feasibility is a SHOP/AGG input to that decision; do not duplicate the full commercial contract here.
Supplier choice, make-or-buy procurement and supply-network design: SCM when sourcing defines the output. Allocation across already available manufacturing resources remains AGG/LOAD.
Warehouse slotting, line-side material routing, kit picking, AGV delivery and external packing: LOG. Constraints from these activities can couple to PROD without changing ownership.
Standalone workforce assignment and rosters: HR. Production task and machine assignments remain PROD when physical manufacturing feasibility dominates.
Generic plant energy optimization: ENER or continuous process engineering unless discrete manufacturing actions are the defining choices.
CAD topology design and purely continuous tolerance optimization: not admitted as ICO. CONFIG covers discrete manufacturable module choices; MATCH covers discrete physical component combinations.
Inspection/test scheduling with fixed tests: SHOP. Inspection-system selection and stage assignment are INSPECT. Choosing repair routes is PROCESS or RECOVERY; scheduling rework is SHOP/RESCHEDULE. No unsupported generic quality-disposition parent is forced.
Original-component reunion during remanufacturing: RECOVERY/SHOP traceability variant. Tolerance-based selective assembly is MATCH.
Construction crews and public project scheduling: CIVIC/HR according to the decision contract. Assembly of manufactured ship blocks and factory equipment remains PROD.
Semiconductor wafer fabrication: PROD. VLSI netlist, placement and wiring decisions: COMP when information/circuit design feasibility dominates.
Physical assembly network synthesis could require #link if assembly relations are genuinely chosen. Current admitted ASSEMBLY uses supplied product relations and chosen operations/order; no unsupported graph-design parent is introduced.

SEARCH COVERAGE AND STOP RULE
Initial broad discovery was followed by targeted searches on missed operational functions: qualification, selective matching, inspection allocation, remanufacturing, fixture-pallet setup, PCB pickup architecture, cyclic robotic cells, configuration, and shipbuilding spatial scheduling.
Later targeted results added CONFIG and enriched SHOP with spatial assembly rather than adding a new spatial-scheduling parent. Results for fixture-constrained scheduling, remanufacturing matching, ship-block planning and tool indexing were absorbed as variants or integration evidence after checking their delivered decisions.
Stop basis: all named manufacturing territories in 000_DOMAIN_research.txt now map to admitted parents or explicit boundary/exclusion decisions. The latest searches mainly produced aliases, model variants, methods and cross-domain interfaces. This is a bounded practical coverage assessment, not a proof of exhaustive discovery or a quantitative saturation statistic.
No unresolved material ownership or parent-identity split is silently decided. Broader robot motion, product shape design, quality economics and supply-network integration remain restricted or excluded as specified above. They can return for Problem-layer review if case evidence establishes a distinct commercially material ICO contract.

DEPENDENCY SEMANTICS AND GRAPH
Edges below are inferred industrial/information contracts supported by the candidate scopes, not mathematical reductions or a claim that every factory executes every edge.
Each candidate's outgoing keys record the immediate meaningful constraints or information it supplies. Independent decisions need no artificial dependency. An edge remains conditional on that activity existing in the deployment.
Design edges pass equipment, group, space, buffer, task-balance or eligibility commitments to later planning. Process-plan edges pass admissible operations and setups. Aggregate and lot edges pass workload, quantities and due requirements. Operational edges pass authorized work, immediate actions and revised commitments.
Cycles: SHOP -> RESCHEDULE -> SHOP; RELEASE -> RESCHEDULE -> RELEASE; DISPATCH -> RESCHEDULE -> DISPATCH. They represent repair after observed change, not backwards initial design. SHOP, MIXED, MAINT and RELEASE can feed DISPATCH/RESCHEDULE. Compilation must retain feedback rather than invent a DAG.
PROCESS and SHOP can be jointly optimized, as can LOT and SHOP. Those integration possibilities are not mandatory reverse dependency edges: a revised plan can be handled through RESCHEDULE or a new planning iteration. CAPACITY, CELL, LAYOUT, BALANCE and BUFFER also co-design in some deployments; the chosen one-way graph records a conventional commitment interface rather than asserting a universal one-pass engineering sequence.
Direct edges are used for ordering; transitive reachability is not duplicated in every card. Evidence and inference are sufficient to select a sequence with design before planning, allocation before detailed calendars where possible, and adaptation last. Provisional keys are translated only in foreground compilation.
