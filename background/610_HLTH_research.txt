TERRITORY AND DECISION BOUNDARY

HLTH owns a healthcare decision when clinical, patient or direct-care conditions materially define feasibility or service. The contract may coordinate care resources or construct a patient-specific physical treatment configuration. A hospital address, medical product name or patient-safety objective alone does not transfer a generic logistics, employment, inventory or construction contract into HC.

Operating territory includes regional health-service networks; primary and specialist ambulatory care; perioperative services; inpatient wards; emergency departments; intensive/intermediate care; diagnostics; oncology infusion; radiotherapy; chronic dialysis; multidisciplinary rehabilitation; mental-health and community services; long-term and residential care; home nursing; emergency medical services; transplant procurement and paired donation; transfusion services; public-health vaccination and screening; mass-casualty medicine and military medical care where clinical feasibility dominates.

Mental-health consultations use BLUEPRINT/APPOINT; multi-stage care coordination uses PATHWAY. Residential and long-term care use patient-compatible BED, NURSE and recurring care/therapy contracts when clinical need governs feasibility. Home and community treatment use HOME, REHAB and APPOINT as applicable. These are operating contexts, not forced new problem identities.

OWNERSHIP AND INTERFACES

HC / HR: shift patterns, employee duties, leave and generic skills rostering are HR even in a hospital. Acuity, patient-care responsibility, caregiver continuity and clinically synchronized actions define HC NURSE/HOME. HR supplies available qualified personnel; HC consumes that capacity. Physician titles alone do not settle ownership.
HC / LOG: generic patient transport, shortest paths, organ shipping and specimen movement are LOG. Patient severity, medical vehicle capability, receiving-clinical capacity and time-dependent treatment feasibility can make the integrated EMS/MCI or direct-care visit contract HC. Route cost alone is insufficient.
HC / SCM: medical procurement, blood inventory targets, vaccine cold-chain stock and generic shortage allocation are SCM. Patient antigen matching and clinically eligible dose/course allocation are HC. Inventory positions are inputs to HC allocation; clinical issuing returns stock consumption to SCM.
HC / CIVIC: generic facility construction, civic evacuation and buildings are CIVIC. Clinical access/capability network design and direct-care workspace access can be HC. Construction coordination remains CIVIC despite an eventual hospital use.
HC / INTEL: adversarial protection, patrol and threat-denial feasibility are INTEL. Medical casualty treatment remains HC even in military or disaster settings when the patient contract determines the solution.
HC / COMP: risk prediction, scheduling software hosting and compute infrastructure are COMP contexts or analytical inputs. The actual patient/resource construction remains HC. A predictor alone is not ICO.
HC / FIN: billing, reimbursement claims, insurance risk and financial reconciliation are FIN. Intervention packages defined by patient health outcomes are HC even if financial protection is a co-objective.
HC / MKT: commercial price and counterpart allocation are MKT; clinical compatibility in organ or blood allocation makes HC primary. The word market in an exchange algorithm does not change patient ownership.
HC / PROC and PROD: drug manufacture, device manufacture and laboratory transformation without constitutive patient-service conditions remain physical transformation domains. Clinical diagnostic queues and patient-specific radiation configurations remain HC.

RESEARCH AND COVERAGE METHOD

Discovery started with the foundational HC coverage but did not treat its seeds as exhaustive. Separate searches covered clinical network/capacity design; master surgery and individual surgery; admission versus bed assignment; staff-patient coupling; clinic blueprint versus live booking; diagnostics and care pathways; infusion, dialysis, radiation, rehabilitation and home care; EMS bases, dispatch, destination and relocation; transplant allocation and exchange; blood issuing; vaccine and screening; intervention portfolios; critical-care access; casualty treatment and schedule repair.

A follow-up sweep tested discharge coordination, rehabilitation, medication administration, risk-based screening, group therapy, care-space layout and integrated healthcare timetabling. Discharge timing was retained within PATHWAY because it consumes clinical readiness and prior treatment completion. Chemotherapy administration found established infusion/template contracts. The search did not establish a separate broad medication-administration Problem beyond these and clinically specified task scheduling. The care-space source supports mobile ICU interior layout, not arbitrary hospital architecture.

Evidence access varies. Full text or original author/institutional pages were opened where available. Some publisher requests failed and some PMC requests returned a browser challenge; indexed primary abstracts/model excerpts are then the evidence ceiling. The source register and industry application section state these access limits explicitly. No case-level data access, software licence, reproduced objective value or complete SOTA ranking is claimed. Technical architectures are supported by primary papers; the relocation review is used only to delimit outcome evidence.

Ratings, Domain ownership and Structure are analytical inferences from the decision contract and normative definitions. They are not ratings assigned by the cited authors. Complexity labels below apply only to the stated specialization. An integer formulation alone is not proof of NP-hardness. A standard budgeted subset can contain 0–1 knapsack; a free facility subset can contain covering/facility location; independent clinical resources with precedence can contain scheduling. Restricted matching, flow and ranking models can be polynomial. Industrial Hardness reflects coupling, uncertainty, response limits and data/model burden as well as mathematical search.

DEPENDENCY INTERPRETATION

Dependencies below are conditional industrial or information edges, not mandatory use of every module, and not mathematical reductions. Source models establish the information consumed; the modular graph is this research's analytical decomposition. SHARE→ORGAN supplies access geography. PACKAGE→NETWORK/SCREEN/VACCINE represents funded-service constraints. NETWORK→CAPACITY/ADMIT/PATHWAY/ICU supplies clinical capabilities. EMSBASE→dispatch/relocation/casualty response supplies available medical vehicles and bases. CAPACITY→BLOCK/BLUEPRINT/ADMIT and treatment courses supplies resource envelopes. BLOCK→SURGERY and BLUEPRINT→APPOINT supply capacity calendars. BEAM→RADIOTIME supplies delivery duration/capability; SCREEN/VACCINE→APPOINT supplies eligible demand. ORGAN/EXCHANGE→SURGERY supplies accepted transplant plans. ADMIT→PATHWAY/BED supplies dates and episodes. PATHWAY→departmental execution supplies required steps and timing windows. BED→NURSE supplies patient locations; NURSE→INFUSION supplies patient-care coverage. BED/ICU→EMSDISPATCH supplies receiving capacity and admission eligibility. SURGERY→ORREPAIR supplies the incumbent schedule. Dispatch→standby relocation supplies post-call vehicle state.

Feedback and cycles are real: BED↔NURSE when walking/workload alters rooms; admissions↔bed occupancy when observed stays change dates; PATHWAY↔department schedules when local feasibility changes the global plan; EMS dispatch↔relocation when a joint policy is used; ED/ICU/BED→EMS destination and EMS destination→arrivals; SURGERY→repair→later surgery-state inputs. Foreground uses first-commitment directions to avoid depicting repeated state feedback as contradictory permanent numbering. These couplings are retained here, not silently denied. Integrated variants may solve the coupled decisions jointly.

Distinction/ownership: Commits the standing access geography before actual donor-recipient assignment. NETWORK chooses clinical facilities/capabilities; SHARE chooses eligibility-sharing groups. Medical access and viability define #HC; moving an accepted organ is #LOG.
Construction: #map assigns areas to sharing groups; a partition is constructed. Geographic adjacency is a feasibility rule, not an independently chosen #link artifact.
Regime: #design; creates persistent clinical sharing structure.
Templates: Transplant geographic equity and sharing-region design | Set-partitioning IP, geographic decomposition and allocation simulation (S77); heterogeneous-circle nonlinear IP and MILP reformulations (S78) are materially different alternatives outside the disjoint-district card.
Ratings inference: Priority Medium (transplant system specialization); Relevance Critical (access to scarce transplant treatment); Hardness High (many geographic partitions and patient-level effects require structured search and simulation). No full-contract formal class is asserted.
Evidence: S77, S78. Published models are policy-analysis methods, not a statement of the currently enacted policy.
Dependencies: SHARE -> ORGAN.

APERTURE — Multileaf Collimator Delivery Planning
Decision/output: Construct deliverable aperture patterns and their leaf positions and exposures for a prescribed patient fluence map under machine movement and dose-delivery limits.
Aliases/variants: IMRT leaf sequencing; fluence-map segmentation; step-and-shoot aperture decomposition; dynamic leaf trajectory optimization.
Distinction/ownership: BEAM chooses incident beams; APERTURE consumes their fluence maps and constructs a deliverable radiation modulation plan; RADIOTIME books the patient course. Machine manufacture and generic equipment motion are outside the patient treatment contract.
Construction: #place + #select; positions collimator leaves across delivery segments/time and selects the aperture patterns used. The supplied fluence matrix is input; its indices and auxiliary decomposition variables are not extra #map constructions.
Regime: #plan, even when the method is called sequencing; it prepares a treatment configuration before treatment execution.
Templates: Fluence-map segmentation and machine-feasible radiation delivery | IP decomposition and combinatorial search for static apertures; trajectory MIP and stochastic search for dynamic leaves (S70, S71). These are alternative delivery modes, not a universal ranking.
Ratings inference: Priority Medium; Relevance High; Hardness High because aperture geometry, exposure and movement feasibility interact. Fixed simple leaf-sweeping specializations may be easier.
Evidence: S70, S71. No reported experiment is claimed reproduced.
Dependencies: BEAM -> APERTURE -> RADIOTIME.

BRACHY — Brachytherapy Seed Placement
Decision/output: Position permanent radioactive seeds at permitted implant sites under target-dose, organ-at-risk, needle access and spatial distribution rules.
Aliases/variants: LDR prostate implant planning; permanent seed implant optimization.
Distinction/ownership: Produces a spatial implant prescription, not external beam orientation, fixed-catheter continuous dwell-time adjustment, seed localization from images or procedure booking.
Construction: #place; chooses physical implant positions. Binary site occupancy encodes that spatial choice; no independent assignment to patient groups is constructed.
Regime: #plan; prepares a patient-specific approved implantation configuration.
Templates: Inverse permanent-seed planning with dosimetric and expert spatial rules | Seed-site IP/MILP with branch-and-bound or model-specific search (S72, S73). Clinician-built plans are the practice comparison; no autonomous clinical prescription claim.
Ratings inference: Priority Medium (radiation implant specialization); Relevance Critical (patient dose and tissue exposure); Hardness High (many dose-coupled positions and spatial rules). Formal full-contract complexity is not asserted.
Evidence: S72, S73. S73 has a linked corrigendum; only its abstract-level model architecture is used.
Dependencies: BRACHY -> SURGERY when implantation uses scheduled procedure resources; this is conditional, not a universal requirement.

DISCHARGE — Clinical Discharge Sequencing
Decision/output: Order and time discharge processing for patients with supplied medical readiness under processing capacity, desired departure windows and downstream bed requests.
Aliases/variants: Same-day inpatient discharge scheduling; discharge-bed-release coordination.
Distinction/ownership: PATHWAY coordinates the entire clinical episode; DISCHARGE consumes readiness and schedules remaining release work. It does not decide that a clinically unready patient is safe to leave. Post-acute facility selection is not evidenced by the cited dissertation and is not asserted.
Construction: #place; constructs the discharge order and times. Processing capacity and bed requests are supplied.
Regime: #operate + #sequence; fixes near-term order of known discharge work inside the ongoing inpatient service. Material readiness changes trigger local revision but do not make every batch plan primarily dispatch.
Templates: Ready-for-discharge work and bed-release coordination | Two-stage stochastic scheduling with decomposition; deterministic-equivalent optimization and shortest-expected-processing-time heuristic comparators (S74).
Ratings inference: Priority High (recurring inpatient bed-release work); Relevance High (boarding and avoidable stays); Hardness High (uncertain processing and bed requests couple release timing). Source-derived comparative performance is not generalized to all hospitals.
Evidence: S74; dissertation abstract opened. S75 supplies emerging barrier prioritization, not evidence of a routine matching system for post-acute facilities.
Dependencies: PATHWAY, BED -> DISCHARGE; released beds feed subsequent BED updates.

EXCLUSION LEDGER

Admitted candidates are the 31 inherited dossier keys after the ORGAN/DIAG revisions and the four new keys SHARE, APERTURE, BRACHY and DISCHARGE. Fixed-slot APPOINT and free-start departmental scheduling remain distinct deliverables. INFUSION, DIALYSIS, RADIOTIME and REHAB are not disease-label splits: their constitutive resource patterns differ (changing nursing demand, recurring connection tasks, radiation-course continuity, and recurrent multidisciplinary care relationships). A one-off visit without these conditions stays APPOINT. PATHWAY is a cross-department care-episode coordination contract, not another name for every departmental scheduler.

Merged aliases/variants: PAS fixed-stay instances merge into BED, while actual admission-date choice is ADMIT; isolation cohorting stays BED; chemotherapy is INFUSION and its blueprint/booking stages; home dialysis is HOME plus clinically specified session requirements; organ types are ORGAN variants; exchange chain limits, fairness and crossmatch recourse stay EXCHANGE; vaccination age groups and vaccine types stay VACCINE; telehealth mode choice stays clinically constrained APPOINT; emergency insertion with an incumbent surgery schedule stays ORREPAIR. Generic hospital-wide schedule repair has no separate card without a defined independent commitment; its care-state updates stay PATHWAY.

Integrated room/nurse assignment is a BED/NURSE variant supported by S31. Joint EMS dispatch/relocation/destination is a supported integrated EMS variant, not an extra duplicate of its component contracts. Inter-hospital surge care can combine ICU, BED and MCI. When clinical treatment capability and patient assignment dominate, HC remains primary; a stock-sharing or transport-only contract remains SCM/LOG.

Excluded or deferred candidates:
- Generic nurse and physician rostering, technician shifts, staff hiring and leave: HR. Clinical staff-to-patient assignment remains NURSE. No generic workforce Problem is rebranded HC.
- Blood ordering, donor collection supply, medical procurement, drug formulary inventory and vaccine warehouse distribution: SCM; travel execution: LOG. S57 is explicit boundary evidence.
- Radiation fluence-only continuous optimization: outside this ICO scope. Continuous biological dose/fraction optimization is distinct from prescribed-course scheduling. A newly discovered mixed-integer dose/break model (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S240563162600103X) is retained as emerging patient-specific prescription research; one indexed model does not establish a commercially recurring canonical operations contract at v0.1.
- Clinical diagnosis, treatment efficacy prediction, triage classification and length-of-stay prediction: analytical inputs, not solution constructions by themselves.
- General individual treatment recommendation, drug selection and surgery selection based solely on comparative clinical efficacy: outside this operational catalogue unless a separate resource-constrained, clinically approved choice contract is evidenced. BEAM is admitted because physical dose delivery has established discrete configuration methods.
- Separate medication-administration calendar, clinical trial cohort matching, transplant crossmatch test selection, infection-control contact assignment and mass-testing group design: potential ICO contracts but insufficient stand-alone commercially recurring operational Template evidence in this run. Existing care-task, admission, exchange and public-health contracts cover the evidenced portions. They are deferred rather than treated as aliases without proof.
- Hospital architecture without direct-care workspace requirements; generic maintenance or cleaning; asset purchase and device manufacture: boundary to CIVIC, HR, SCM or PROD. Sterile instrument turnaround with constitutive surgical readiness belongs in perioperative scheduling; a separate sterile-processing operations contract needs dedicated evidence.
- General evacuation, shelter allocation and disaster supply logistics: LOG/SCM/CIVIC; medical casualty treatment and clinical destination allocation: MCI.

COVERAGE LIMITS AND LATER RESEARCH

This is a broad practical v0.1 set, not a mathematical proof of exhaustive healthcare decisions. Later discovery mostly returned aliases, solver changes, narrow medical contexts, integrated variants or neighboring-domain contracts recorded above. Search saturation is weakest for sterile services, clinical medication-task scheduling, long-term care with distinctive resident/group assignment and clinical trial operational allocation. These gaps are explicit; unsupported cards are not added to manufacture completeness.

Formal lower-bound arguments and algorithm guarantees must be rechecked against each later selected instance. Fixed-policy organ ranking is excluded from the chosen construction; compatible blood flow and basic appointment matching have easier specializations. No source-supported universal solver ranking exists across all admitted HC contracts. Current operating practice, publication method and reproducible benchmark must be distinguished in the Case layer; an accessible paper or an institutional abstract does not establish accessible patient data.

Case research requires explicit human approval against the fixed parent set. No case IDs, canonical starter instances, performance uplifts or production clinical recommendations are selected here.

CASE RESEARCH — HLTH506 PATIENT-TO-ROOM AND BED ALLOCATION
10 October 2026 | Third calibration Case | INDUSTRY first

PARENT
HLTH506 Patient-to-Room and Bed Allocation.
The parent Problem is fixed.

INDUSTRY CASE — HLTH506c

CASE
Assign admitted inpatients to clinically compatible rooms or beds under live occupancy, specialty, isolation, room-capability and transfer constraints.

SOTA
The strongest defensible current industrial state is a hybrid command-center and EHR-integrated bed-management process.
Real-time platforms expose occupied, assigned, unassigned and blocked beds; forecast census, admissions and discharge; aggregate clinical and operational placement data; and provide prescriptive prioritization or matching support [H01-H05].
Experienced bed managers or patient-placement teams still retain a central operational role in mature deployments. Johns Hopkins explicitly staffs bed management 24/7 with experienced nurses who assign the right patient to the right bed [H01].
UCHealth uses predictive and prescriptive inpatient-flow software across 12 hospitals to balance capacity and support placement decisions [H02].
Humber River combines bed-allocation staff, real-time bed/census views, AI-supported command-center analytics and patient-flow interventions [H03].
Commercial platforms from TeleTracking, Epic, GE HealthCare and LeanTaaS expose patient placement, capacity, forecast and workflow capabilities, but the public material inspected does not disclose a common combinatorial assignment formulation, objective or solver that can be reproduced as the industrial SOTA [H04-H07].
Published OR is technically more explicit: current research combines ML arrival forecasting with hyper-heuristic bed assignment on real hospital data, and recent PAS/PASU work uses MIP, large-neighborhood and matheuristic methods [H08-H10].
The industrial SOTA is therefore best represented as real-time predictive/prescriptive decision support plus expert human placement, with optimization evidence stronger in research than in transparent deployed product descriptions.

CONTEXT
Acute-care inpatient operations at high occupancy.
Incoming patients can arrive from ED, surgery, direct admission, transfers or step-up/step-down care.
Material feasibility includes specialty and level-of-care eligibility, isolation/cohorting, room or equipment capability, current occupancy and bed readiness.
Operational quality depends on boarding delay, off-service placement, avoidable internal transfers and preservation of scarce specialized beds.
Length of stay and emergency demand are uncertain.
Clinical eligibility, admission priority and staffing availability are supplied constraints for HLTH506; choosing nurse responsibility remains HLTH507.

GAP
Industry deployments are strong at real-time state visibility, demand/census forecasting, command-center coordination and placement workflow, while research demonstrates explicit optimization gains.
The two evidence streams do not meet in a common public industrial benchmark.
Legacy PAS/PASU families introduced in 2010-2012 remain heavily used in current research; 2024 work still evaluates matheuristics on those benchmark families [H09].
They capture useful room, specialty, equipment, gender, capacity and length-of-stay conditions, but they are synthetic/legacy rather than calibrated to a modern command-center event stream.
A 2023 operational patient-bed study used real data from a large German hospital and reported up to 3.3% overall improvement from ML forecasting plus a hyper-heuristic, but the hospital data are confidential [H08].
No inspected public dataset jointly supplies current occupancy state, timestamped arrivals and bed readiness, modern compatibility/clinical placement rules, forecast uncertainty, transfer history and a reproducible expert or deployed-software baseline.
Proposed uplift direction: build a rolling-event HLTH506 benchmark from de-identified or calibrated synthetic hospital states, then test whether v10 structural assignment/decomposition produces repeatable reductions in boarding, off-service placement and transfers beyond strong PAS/PASU optimization and expert/rule-based placement baselines.

ATTEMPTS / CURRENT METHOD FAMILIES
- 24/7 expert bed-management teams and daily capacity huddles.
- EHR-integrated command centers with real-time capacity views, alerts and escalation.
- Predictive admission, discharge and census analytics.
- Rule-based and prescriptive prioritization of patient placement.
- MIP and reduced/aggregated integer models for patient-room assignment.
- Adaptive large-neighborhood, matheuristic and hyper-heuristic search.
- ML forecasting coupled to rolling operational bed assignment.
These are alternative or complementary methods for HLTH506; system-wide care-pathway orchestration remains HLTH308.

BENCHMARK SUPPORT
Public benchmark support exists but is structurally dated.
- The Demeester/Ceschia-Schaerf PAS/PASU families supply hundreds of standard patient-room instances and remain active research references [H09, H10].
- Dynamic PAS research includes emergency arrivals and uncertain stay lengths, but public instances are generated rather than operational event histories [H09].
- A 2024 integrated patient-room/nurse study provides artificial instance generation plus real-world experiments, but the integrated nurse decision crosses into HLTH507 and is not the baseline identity for HLTH506 [H11].
- The 2023 real-hospital ML/optimization study is especially relevant operationally, but its underlying case-hospital data cannot be released [H08].
Therefore a purpose-built benchmark is warranted.

PURPOSE-BUILT BENCHMARK BRIEF — HLTH506

CLAIM TO TEST
Can v10 improve rolling patient-to-room/bed assignment beyond strong current optimization and expert/rule-based placement while preserving every clinical compatibility and capacity requirement?

CORE EVENT AND STATE DATA
- de-identified patient request identifier and arrival timestamp;
- arrival source and supplied clinical priority;
- specialty and level-of-care eligibility;
- isolation/cohorting requirements;
- required room or bed capabilities/equipment;
- current room/bed occupancy and blocked status;
- bed-cleaning/readiness timestamps where they affect availability;
- expected discharge and LOS distribution or scenarios;
- current patient location and permissible transfer set;
- room/bed capabilities and specialty ownership;
- supplied unit staffing/capacity limits when they restrict usable beds;
- actual or simulated emergency-arrival stream;
- timestamps for assignment, bed-ready and occupancy events;
- objective weights or KPI definitions used by each baseline.

REGIME AXES
Vary:
- hospital/ward scale;
- occupancy tightness;
- emergency-arrival share;
- LOS forecast error;
- specialty/acuity heterogeneity;
- isolation prevalence;
- scarcity of specialized beds;
- room/bed turnover delay;
- transfer penalty;
- proportion of patients with multiple feasible units.

BOUNDARIES
Do not make HLTH506 choose admissions, discharge readiness, nurse-to-patient responsibility or hospital-wide clinical pathway actions.
Those can be supplied state or interfaces.
A benchmark extension may expose their impact as fixed scenarios without silently merging HLTH505, HLTH507 or HLTH308.

BASELINES
At minimum:
- transparent expert/rule placement policy reflecting realistic clinical priorities;
- strong MIP or constraint-aggregation approach where scale permits;
- strong ALNS/matheuristic/hyper-heuristic PAS/PASU baseline;
- rolling version with forecast uncertainty;
- current commercial decision-support output when a partner can export it.
Do not claim superiority to a proprietary platform if its assignment decision cannot be reproduced.

CONSTRUCTION AND VALIDATION
Prefer de-identified event and bed-state extracts from a partner hospital.
Where unavailable, use a calibrated generator whose arrival, LOS, occupancy, compatibility, bed-readiness and transfer distributions match audited hospital summaries.
Separate training/calibration periods from held-out evaluation windows.
Use clinical/bed-management review to certify that every generated assignment constraint is operationally plausible.
Publish the generator, schema, baseline implementations and checker where privacy and licensing permit.

DELIVERABLE
A purpose-built HLTH506 benchmark can itself be a service artifact:
- public synthetic/calibrated core;
- private hospital-specific event overlay;
- baseline and replay harness;
- KPI mapping from optimization objective to bed-management operations;
- reproducible validation of any claimed v10 uplift.
