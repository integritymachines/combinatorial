TERRITORY AND OWNERSHIP

Refining/petrochemicals: network/equipment/storage design; heat and water integration; feedstock/quality selection; crude charging; product blending; turnaround; transfer and reactive scheduling. Supplier contracting is SCM; tanker journeys and external distribution pipelines are LOG; utility generation/commitment is ENER.
Specialty chemicals/polymers: formulation, recipe/procedure development, flexible batch and continuous campaigns, grade changes, equipment/path allocation, cleaning and repair. A polymer grade is a state/quality variant, not a new Domain or Structure.
Pharmaceuticals/bioprocessing/fermentation: product composition, validated procedures, multipurpose equipment, batch/state schedules, finite/zero-wait storage, contamination-compatible cleaning and release. Clinical care is HC. Packaging-only sequencing is PROD. Microbial host or technology selection is a formulation/process-design variant, not a separate canonical Problem without a distinct contract.
Food/beverage: ingredient/intermediate selection, lot activation, shared tanks, CIP, shelf-life/quality constraints and bulk-to-pack synchronization. Commercial assortment and pricing are MKT. Secondary PROD coupling is material in integrated bulk/pack decisions.
Pulp/paper: digester/material balance and paper-machine grade schedules fit production/campaign/repair families (S39). Trim cutting and converting decisions without process transformation feasibility belong elsewhere, normally PROD.
Cement/minerals: raw blend and grinding/kiln/mill production activation and grade schedules fit blending/production/continuous families; electricity tariff effects do not transfer process ownership to ENER (S40). Extraction/block destination decisions are EXTR.
Industrial gases: air-separation technology and equipment, discrete process modes, production/storage and real-time configuration are PROC (S12,S21,S38). Installed utility compressor networks can be ENER where utility service, rather than process-product manufacture, defines the contract.
Glass: furnace/melt process decisions are PROC; mold/cavity and discrete container-forming decisions are PROD. Integrated glass studies demonstrate this boundary (S42); industry labels alone cannot resolve the primary decision.
Steel process side: charge/material formulation, steelmaking/refining/casting synchronization and thermal/wait limits fit batch/continuous families where molten material states define feasibility (S41). Part fabrication/assembly belongs to PROD; by-product fuel distribution or utility balance alone is ENER.
Industrial effluent/recovery: process treatment technology and material transformations are network/water design and batch/mode scheduling (S06,S44). Basin rights are LAND, withdrawal/depletion is EXTR, utility distribution is ENER, and civic service construction/maintenance is CIVIC.

ADMISSION AND IDENTITY RULES APPLIED

Integrated contracts are retained only where their delivered decision crosses a meaningful industrial boundary: process-specific multi-site planning, crude-quality feed programs, and bulk-to-pack synchronization. A model containing two decisions does not automatically create another family. Batch/continuous scheduling remains the generic production timetable; tank, equipment/path, cleaning and transfer contracts can be scoped independently when their required inputs are fixed.
Production quantity planning and lot/run activation are one integrated family. Campaign, grade-transition and parallel-continuous-train scheduling are one timeline family. STN/RTN, zero-wait, no-intermediate/finite storage and hybrid MILP/CP are representations, policies or methods within batch scheduling. Mandatory cleaning calendar and active CIP reservations are one cleaning family. These decisions are not multiplied by time horizon, solver or mathematical encoding.
Process flowsheet, heat-recovery and water-treatment networks remain separate stand-alone contracts because they choose different engineering entities and can be optimized from supplied plant streams without reselecting the material-conversion flowsheet. Joint synthesis is an integrated instance, not an additional family. Storage configuration and equipment capacity have different objects; actual tank occupancy is an allocation contract downstream of design.

DEFERRED OR EXCLUDED CANDIDATES

Standalone utility/cogeneration design and boiler/chiller unit commitment: ENER. Keep utility capacity and tariffs as PROC feasibility/input conditions and a material interface.
Operator shifts, skill rosters and generic crew planning: HR. Process-task schedules may consume crew capacity; staffing a chemical plant does not alone transfer workforce ownership.
Supplier selection, purchasing contracts, generic distribution network/inventory optimization: SCM. Keep recipe/material-quality lot choices in blend planning when transformation dominates.
Ship arrival sequencing, external pipeline routing and terminal-only tank movements: LOG. Keep supplied receipt windows and transport capacities at PROC interfaces.
Standalone plant construction and generic project execution: CIVIC. Keep process topology, equipment placement and isolation-constrained turnarounds in PROC according to contract.
Sensor placement, generic laboratory instrument queues and digital automation resources: COMP where sensing or digital-resource operation defines feasibility. Process analytical tests tied to material qualification and held-lot availability have a narrower PROC contract; quality-testing scheduling is admitted using S45-S47. This ownership is conditional on the promised material-release service, not on the laboratory being located in a process company.
Continuous setpoint optimization/MPC, kinetic parameter estimation, continuous recipe trajectories with no chosen steps, and fixed-choice linear blends: outside ICO.
Batch genealogy recording, traceability, contamination checking, validation and safety verification alone: constraints, descriptors or information systems, not a chosen ICO construction. Genealogy/state restrictions can refine batch, tank and material-allocation decisions.
Off-spec rework, salvage and release/disposition optimization: plausible process contracts, but dedicated material-scope and Template evidence remains insufficient after the present searches. Discrete reprocessing paths can fit procedure/network and batch scheduling; no extra family is admitted until a distinct recurring contract is evidenced.
Product assortment, price and bid selection: MKT, even when the commercial product is a chemical.
Capacity expansion, retrofit, stochastic/robust planning, cyclic scheduling, energy-aware schedules, fermentation residence time and batch heat integration: variants of supported families, not independent admissions.

STRUCTURAL AND TEMPLATE CHECKS

Supplied recipes, plant graphs, eligibility relations and demand are substrate. Only selected equipment/materials/actions, assigned roles/resources, chosen connections/transitions and placed activity/spatial positions contribute Structure. A successor binary can encode temporal order without requiring link in addition to place. Continuous quantities and quality balances are associated decisions/conditions, not additional canonical Structures. Auxiliary big-M, event-point, GDP or inventory variables do not determine Structure.
Templates below retain credible industrial framings and demonstrated formal architectures. Engineering judgment/operator workflows, published models and public reproducible benchmarks are different evidence levels. Evidence for quality testing adds published dual-resource MILP and dynamic/tabu heuristics; a vendor integration announcement supplies operational coupling without establishing a complete-factory baseline. No source supports one universally best method across every variant; explicit near-equals are kept where their modelling trade-off is material. Template existence is sufficient at this layer; later Case research must establish actual public data, implementation and baseline evidence.

RATING BASIS

Priority: Critical for domain-defining process topology, production commitments and production timetables; High for recurring capacity, material, cleaning, outage and execution contracts; Medium for specialized design/maintenance/refinery families. Relevance: Critical where the decision determines long-lived plant capacity, whole-site outage losses or refinery feed continuity; otherwise High for substantial recurring material, service, safety or energy value. No unsupported percentage, monetary magnitude or universal prevalence is asserted.
Hardness: generally High where evidence describes interacting discrete choices, nonlinear quality/state models or industrial decomposition/heuristics. Medium for immediate release, where a feasible rule baseline is established and the permitted search scope may be limited. These are qualitative industrial assessments with the card-specific mechanism below, not deductions from NP-hardness. Restricted polynomial or trivial instances do not receive the full-family complexity claim.

DEPENDENCY INTERPRETATION

Every listed edge is conditional industrial/information flow, not a claim that every plant runs every family or that distinct Problems must be solved sequentially. Joint solves replace selected edges with simultaneous commitments. Edges are inferred by matching the cited industrial output of one contract to the input of another. The cited paper need not use these candidate keys. External interfaces are described above; no fictitious ENER/HR/SCM Problem IDs are introduced.
Default production-to-cleaning, production-to-transfer and bulk-to-pack edges express the first demand for detailed resource reservations. Reverse edges retain cleaning/transfer/buffer feedback. Continuous-to-fouling and fouling-to-continuous form a condition/capacity loop. Dispatch-to-repair conveys completed/in-progress commitments; repair-to-dispatch supplies the next revised release program. These cycles cannot be made acyclic without dropping material industrial feedback.
Design can also be iterated against downstream schedules, and multi-site plans can consume local feasibility envelopes. No universal direction is forced for those joint-design/planning refinements. The quality-testing → packaging edge applies only where tests qualify bulk material before filling; post-pack final-product testing is a different timing variant and this edge is not imposed there. The explicit graph supplies a default commitment flow and the identified recurring feedback, sufficient to derive a canonical sequence without claiming a universal industrial algorithm.
