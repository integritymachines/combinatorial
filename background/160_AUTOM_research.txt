EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE
Source observations are confined to the source register. Candidate contracts, ontology assignments, qualitative ratings, admission reasoning and dependency edges are analytical inferences unless explicitly identified as source facts.
Primary indexed abstracts support only their displayed claims. A failed page opening is not represented as a full-paper inspection.
Sources carried from existing PROD research are labeled. Their transferable architectures support method existence; automotive context requires automotive evidence in addition.
Published methods, industrial software and reproducible benchmarks are different evidence types. No method is called a universally strongest solver. Templates retain material architecture alternatives where superiority depends on the contract.
Industrial Hardness is a judgment about industrial search, modeling and coordination effort. NP-hardness is not its proxy. Formal-class claims are restricted to demonstrably contained general subproblems.
Availability of a publication or official challenge is not a certificate of data access, license, executable baseline, or reproducibility. Those require the approved Case layer.

INDUSTRY DOMAIN IDENTITY AND TERRITORY TEST
Code 160 and the manufacturing identity are supplied by the user. Code 160 lies in the #TRANS 110–199 Industry Domain range and is a cluster-head code. No further member code is assigned by this work.
Apply Primary Domain first: vehicle and component fabrication, assembly compatibility, line capability and physical workpiece execution define #PROD. Automotive industry context cannot absorb supply, transport, workforce, utility or financial decisions with different contracts.
Default identity ##AUTOMOTIVE × #PROD is insufficient if a distinct development territory is retained. Evidence supports the following residual split:
1. Artifact: product-platform/module sharing decisions deliver a new product-family architecture [S41, S42]. Manufacturing decisions consume released product definitions and deliver factory capability, production procedures, manufacturing calendars or physically realizable vehicle/component output [S01–S06, S11].
2. Practice: platform/commonality and product-family engineering have a distinct inventory and development practice [S41, S42]. Press allocation, weld balancing, plant master scheduling and restricted body resequencing have their own manufacturing practice and inventory [S02, S04, S06, S19].
3. Domain residual: product/component architecture choices remain #PROD when manufactured-product feasibility dominates; factory fabrication/assembly choices also remain #PROD. Commercial portfolio choice is MKT and generic project staffing is HR, so neither is used to manufacture this residual.
4. Not a variant: changing a platform's component architecture and running a released build program change different industrial contracts. They are not merely two horizons or #design/#operate stages of one manufacturing Problem.
Conclusion: 160 names ##AUTOMOTIVE × #PROD × #production. A development Industry Domain is distinguishable, but its code and foreground are not established here. There is no pre-existing published 160 meaning to narrow or retire.
Within 160, press shop, body shop, paint shop, final assembly, component fabrication, electronic assembly and remanufacturing are applications of one production Territory. They do not earn separate Territory codes.
Regime is not Territory: configuring a production line, production buffer or fixture delivers manufacturing capability, so #design can occur within #production.
Interface qualification: prototype builds used to define a new product are development; routine realization of released components or manufacturing aids is production. Department names and lifecycle terms alone do not settle this boundary.
Generic PROD retains its generic coverage. This Industry Domain specializes that coverage; it does not transfer all automotive decisions out of PROD or reclassify its foreground.

COVERAGE MAP
OEM vehicle realization: installed capacity, station work, layouts, buffers, master order-pool commitments, paint streams, final-assembly streams, buffer reachability, release and recovery from disruptions.
Body and metal-component production: press toolkits, die setups, blank nesting, weld load allocation, executable robot cycles, assembly processes and dimensional-quality capability.
Powertrain, trim and other suppliers: machining routes, tools, cellular systems, mold-injection lots, heat-treatment batches, flexible operation calendars and tolerance matching.
Automotive electronics: feeder/pickup programs and fixed-capacity board production; installed computation remains outside this contract.
Additive manufacturing: released series components and manufacturing aids; build grouping/orientation/packing is distinguished from product-development prototypes.
Remanufacturing: physical disassembly and rebuilding of components. Return collection, core procurement and reverse network positioning remain interfaces.
Candidate-position fixture design is admitted with fixed released datums [S56]; product datum redesign is outside manufacturing.
Shared constraints: component availability, skills, utility limits, transport availability, quality observations and due dates constrain manufacturing decisions. They do not alone establish another Primary Domain.

DISCOVERY, GRANULARITY AND SEARCH SATURATION
Independent broad searches covered production hierarchy, body-shop robotics, press scheduling, master planning, paint/assembly sequencing and buffer control.
Targeted searches then covered standalone flexible-machine loading and factory grouping/layout, inspection, fixture discreteness, selective assembly, tooling, additive production, automotive electronics, disassembly, recovery, release, maintenance and line rebalancing.
The generic PROD inventory was a seed for missed functions, not the final set. Automotive evidence admitted press-toolkit allocation, paint sequencing, restricted-buffer control and order-to-body reassignment as distinct industrial contracts.
Alias merges: car sequencing and workload-based mixed-model sequencing become SEQUENCE; lean leveling is its IE framing. Colored paint batching remains PAINT. Job/flow/hybrid shops, flexible/matrix assembly, test calendars and molding calendars remain SHOP variants.
Additional artifacts: LOAD commits machine workload without start times; NEST builds a cutting pattern; BUILD builds a packing arrangement; BATCH chooses co-processing groups; TOOL commits magazine contents. They are not mere synonyms for an operation calendar.
Integrated-problem decisions: CAP+BALANCE, CELL+LAYOUT, LOT+SHOP, ROBOT+BALANCE, paint/PBS/assembly and production+maintenance can be solved jointly. Composition alone does not create another canonical Problem. MAINT is retained because maintenance actions and windows are chosen, not merely supplied.
Candidate exclusion: product platform selection is development after the residual split. Continuous machining parameters, robot servo control, chemical process setpoints, defect classification, failure prediction and continuous locator tuning alone do not establish ICO. Discrete alternatives can place a properly scoped decision back in PROCESS, FIXTURE or another admitted contract.
Qualification-network configuration lacks automotive-specific evidence sufficient for a separate parent here. Fixed manufacturing eligibility is an input to CAP, SHOP and DISPATCH. It is not promoted by analogy to semiconductor qualification.
Transfer-batch/lot-streaming choices are material LOT/SHOP variants [S21 and generic PROD research]; no separate automotive parent is forced from a parameter change. Future study can return a genuinely distinct transfer-sublot contract to R.
The latest targeted results predominantly supplied variants, integrated models and neighboring contracts. Coverage is broad within the stated scope; it is not a statistical proof that no niche has been missed.
Evidence limits: FIXTURE is restricted to verified discrete fixture choices; no full industrial hardness distributions or universally dominant algorithms are established. Niche additive/electronic contexts use explicit transferable method evidence. These limitations must remain visible to B.

DEPENDENCY BASIS
Edges mean that one admitted decision's industrial artifact can constrain another. They are analyst inferences from the decision contracts and production hierarchy, not mathematical reductions or mandatory execution order for every plant.
S01–S04 support installed-capability/work-allocation/cycle order. S06 supports aggregate output, named order commitments, buffers and subsequent sequencing. S08/S19 support paint-to-buffer-to-assembly flow. S37/S38 distinguish physical release from work-centre activation. S18/S43/S54 support revision of prior work-content or calendar commitments.
The inspection system supplies measurements used to choose DISASSEMBLY procedures; ASSEMBLY supplies workholding-access requirements to FIXTURE. PROCESS/CELL/LOT/RECOVERY supply operation requirements and job quantities to LOAD; LOAD supplies tool demand and workload assignments to TOOL/SHOP.
Each candidate record identifies its outputs. The graph is conditional on an integrated plant or supplier system that uses both endpoints; it does not assume every deployment has every Problem.
ASSEMBLY → BALANCE is cross-band: a planned procedure informs configured station content. Regime bands take precedence over that backward dependency when compilation numbers the Problems.
PAINT → SEQUENCE carries upstream workpiece availability/order; SEQUENCE → BUFFERSEQ carries target downstream restrictions. The physical arrival edge PAINT → BUFFERSEQ is separate. BUFFERSEQ → REMATCH allows order attachments to be repaired after attainable body order changes; REMATCH → SEQUENCE closes a conditional feedback cycle.
REPAIR → MASTER/SHOP/SEQUENCE/RELEASE/DISPATCH returns revised commitments. This is an intentional adaptation feedback cycle. REBALANCE → ROBOT/SEQUENCE returns revised work content and robot assignments.
No capacity-right provision or class-window reservation parent is admitted from horizon terminology alone. No unused Regime band is filled for aesthetic completeness.

STRUCTURAL AND TEMPLATE CHECKS
Supplied eligibility, precedence, geometry and observed attributes are distinct from delivered choices. A known precedence graph is not a chosen #link construction. Constraints and objective terms do not add a Structure.
Every approach entry cites method-existence evidence. Simulation is a performance/geometry evaluator where explicitly stated. Alternative CP, MILP and search architectures are retained where contract differences make one universal ranking indefensible.
Industrial priority/relevance/hardness ratings are conditional judgments grounded in the stated manufacturing applications, not adoption-frequency or monetary measurements.

CASE RESEARCH — AUTOM703 MIXED-MODEL ASSEMBLY SEQUENCING
10 October 2026 | First calibration Case | INDUSTRY first

PARENT
AUTOM703 Mixed-Model Assembly Sequencing.
The parent Problem is fixed. This Case work does not reopen its identity, Regime, Structure, ratings, dependencies or numbering.

INDUSTRY CASE — AUTOM703c

CASE
Order committed vehicles on a variant-rich final assembly line under workload, option, paint, material-flow and delivery constraints.

SOTA
The strongest defensible industry state is plant-data-driven multi-objective sequencing and resequencing using constraint-rich exact or constraint models together with large-neighborhood, metaheuristic or simulation-optimization search.
Evidence is heterogeneous rather than one universal product:
- Ford-Werke reports deployment of a multi-objective resequencing algorithm at Saarlouis using four weeks of recorded production-event data. Reported effects versus the old control software include about 30% larger paint batches, 23% fewer color changeovers and 10% lower spread of vehicles planned for a date [C01].
- Fiat Powertrain research reports a real-world dynamic mixed-model line with parallel stations and walking workers, solved with a mathematical model, hyper-heuristic and simulation-optimization; the authors report particular effectiveness of the simulation approach in the dynamic case [C02].
- Robust car-sequencing research reports expected work-overload reductions of 72% relative to industry solutions by incorporating failure probabilities and a tailored metaheuristic [C03].
- Renault plant evidence shows that workload-oriented mixed-model sequencing and option-ratio car sequencing expose materially different feasible spaces; real scheduling rules can still admit sequences with work overload [C04].
- Commercial automotive APS products now advertise constraint-rich order sequencing, digital-twin scenario evaluation and dynamic replanning [C06-C10].
This supports a SOTA description centered on multi-objective, plant-data-driven constrained sequencing with sophisticated heuristic/search support. It does not establish one universally strongest implementation.

CONTEXT
Automotive final assembly with high product variety.
Material conditions can include option-ratio and station-workload constraints, paint or setup objectives, just-in-sequence material conditions, delivery-date objectives, and disruption-driven removal/reinsertion or resequencing.
The exact mix differs by plant. Do not fuse every known condition into one benchmark instance merely for completeness.

GAP
Current industrial and published systems demonstrate substantial plant-specific KPI gains, but no common public industrial-scale benchmark found in this sweep establishes the residual gap of the strongest deployed multi-objective sequencing methods to the best attainable sequence across the combined industrial constraint set.
The public ROADEF 2005 Renault challenge remains a strong reproducible car-sequencing tradition, but its option-ratio and paint criteria are narrower than current deployed multi-objective plant scope [C05].
The v10 test therefore needs both:
- a reproducible public benchmark against strong current algorithms; and
- explicit qualification of any industrial constraints absent from that benchmark.
Proposed uplift direction: construct benchmark regimes spanning variant mix, workload tightness and option restrictions, then test whether v10 structural operator design produces repeatable objective uplift over strong modern neighborhood, LNS or metaheuristic baselines.

ATTEMPTS / CURRENT METHOD FAMILIES
- Option-ratio car sequencing and workload-based mixed-model sequencing.
- Exact MILP/CP or dynamic-programming methods on restricted instances.
- Adaptive local search, variable-neighborhood and large-neighborhood methods.
- Robust and stochastic sequencing for vehicle failures.
- Simulation-optimization for dynamic line behavior.
- Multi-objective resequencing using live or recorded production data.
- Integrated sequencing with adjacent routing or material-flow decisions.
These are competing or complementary SOTA families, not evidence for separate Problems.


BENCHMARK SUPPORT
ROADEF 2005 Renault car-sequencing challenge [C05].
Useful because it is a public, established automotive sequencing benchmark with option-ratio restrictions and paint-batch criteria.
Limitation: it does not represent the full current plant-data-driven, multi-objective and disruption-aware industrial scope.
Ford Saarlouis deployment [C01] supplies a strong industrial SOTA reference but not, from the inspected public record, a reusable public instance set.
Fiat Powertrain [C02] supplies a dynamic real-world case and data reported as available with the article/supplementary material; reproducibility must be checked before proprietary benchmarking.
No benchmark is promoted yet as a complete industrial surrogate.

PURPOSE-BUILT BENCHMARK BRIEF — AUTOM703

CLAIM TO TEST
Can v10 produce repeatable sequencing uplift beyond a strong modern AUTOM703 SOTA baseline under industrially credible mixed-model constraints, rather than merely outperforming a weak legacy heuristic?

DATASET REQUIREMENTS
Core instances should represent one fixed parent Problem: vehicle sequence construction for a supplied order pool.
Required data dimensions:
- vehicle/order pool and variant attributes;
- vehicle-specific station processes or processing times;
- takt and station-workload limits;
- option or feature-spacing restrictions where industrially material;
- supplied production-date or delivery commitments when used by the objective;
- objective components and weights needed to reproduce the baseline comparison.

Optional controlled overlays may add paint/setup attributes, material availability, disruptions, or supplied resequencing/buffer restrictions.
Do not merge neighboring AUTOM702 paint sequencing or AUTOM802 buffer-control decisions into the parent merely to make the benchmark richer.

SCALE AND DIVERSITY
Use plant-scale day or shift order pools calibrated from public industrial evidence or partner data.
Do not assume a fixed N before evidence.
Include multiple variant-mix, workload-tightness and option-restriction regimes so that a structural uplift is not demonstrated on one favorable configuration only.

BASELINES
At minimum:
- a transparent option-ratio or workload-based exact/constraint baseline where scale permits;
- a strong modern neighborhood, LNS or metaheuristic baseline;
- a simulation-optimization or plant-specific comparator when the instance has dynamic conditions.
A reconstructed public approximation of a proprietary incumbent must be labeled as such.
Do not claim uplift beyond a named client's undisclosed proprietary algorithm without direct benchmark access.

CONSTRUCTION AND VALIDATION
Prefer anonymized real order pools or process-time distributions when available.
Otherwise reconstruct or generate instances from controlled public ranges and disclose the generation process.
Validate aggregate workload, variant, constraint-tightness and objective distributions against industrial evidence.
Use held-out instances for final uplift claims.

DELIVERABLE
A purpose-built AUTOM703 benchmark may itself be a service output:
- public/reproducible core where disclosure permits;
- controlled private extensions for client-specific constraints and data;
- baseline implementations and evaluation protocol sufficient to separate algorithmic uplift from dataset construction choices.
